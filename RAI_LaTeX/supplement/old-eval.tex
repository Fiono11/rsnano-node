\para{Prototype.}
We implemented RAI in RsNano~\cite{rsnano}, a Rust implementation of the Nano node~\cite{nano}. Account elections follow Section~\ref{sec:account} with the equal-weight thresholds of Table~\ref{tab:thresholds} ($f=p=1$, $N=6$), late notarization, the early-vote rule, and the prefix-wide overlap rule; checkpoints carry origin-stamped records and are built by Algorithm~\ref{alg:build} with Rule~3 promotion and matching-origin discharge. Epochs end at fixed times $T_0+kD$ on every validator's local clock. Every first, final, late, and close-election vote, left epoch, frozen report, and overlap certificate is written to an LMDB environment of its own and synced before its signature leaves the process; the epoch origin and decided checkpoints are durable too, and a restarted node reloads these records, refuses whatever they forbid, and fetches the checkpoints it missed by the certified value's hash. A manifest carries late notarizations and settled first votes as bit sets over the committee, and marks the closing-epoch witness of every overlap certificate its evidence proves. A first vote names its base by the installed checkpoint's state hash, and a settled vote counts toward an $\FF$ only where that checkpoint is installed. A position whose first votes are already split so that no block can reach $q$ is not re-voted at the boundary.

\para{Convergence by rateless set reconciliation.}
A report's two committed sets are reconstructed with rateless invertible Bloom lookup tables~\cite{iblt,whatsthedifference,rateless}. Each set element is a fixed 105-byte item naming account, height, hash, parent, and tag. A validator that needs a report starts from its own live projection of the closing epoch and asks any holder of the reconstructed set for coded symbols in growing batches; peeling them against the local projection yields the symmetric difference, which the requester applies and checks against the signed root (C1). It then fetches, by hash, the signed votes behind every $\tagN$ and $\tagF$ entry and every residual record it does not already hold.
\para{Consensus by a Kudzu-style election.}
The closing committee decides the checkpoint with one leader-based voting instance that adapts Kudzu's voting construction~\cite{kudzu}: first votes, second-look notarization support, timeout votes, and certificates with thresholds $q$ (normal), $N-p$ (fast), and $q$ timeout votes to skip a slot. Election votes and account votes are separate domains, and every election signature binds the handoff context, the deciding committee, the slot, and the proposal hash or timeout value. Four points differ from Kudzu and provide A1--A4. There is one instance per handoff: only a child of the genesis slot may introduce a fresh payload $(Q,\mu_e,d_e)$, and a proposal on a certified parent copies its parent's payload, which with Kudzu's exclusion of a conflicting certificate in a decided slot gives A1. A validator first-votes a proposal only after reconstructing its reports, checking every manifest claim, running $\Build$, and matching $d_e$ (A2), so a final quorum contains correct validators that validated the fixed evidence (A3). The outgoing committee decides and the successor only verifies the decision proof; Kudzu's erasure-coded payload distribution and communication bound are not used. Leaders rotate in canonical key order; a slot whose leader is offline or Byzantine times out, a validator waiting up to three times longer while it still checks the leader's proposal or while a usable reporter's proposal has not arrived, and a later leader proposes again a payload it already validated. Under recurring prepared windows with $N-f$ responsive correct members and a local timeout satisfying $\Delta_E/(1+\rho)>\kappa_E+\tau_E+\delta$, where $\kappa_E$ bounds slot-entry skew and $\tau_E$ bounds proposal preparation and validation~\cite{dls}, Kudzu's progress argument gives A4.

\para{Departures from the protocol.}
Witnesses that discharge records of $S_{e-1}$ are re-derived by every validator from the manifest's votes rather than bound to their claims; a base names the checkpoint, not the per-block admission evidence; retained vote records keep a cap of four epochs plus whatever may still discharge a surviving record; and \texttt{fsync} on the host does not force the drive's cache. The supplementary material details these and the defects found while measuring.

\para{Baseline and setup.}
The baseline is unmodified RsNano (\texttt{develop}), which runs Nano's open representative voting: a block is confirmed once final votes reach 67\% of the online weight, after a round of non-final votes; it has no epochs and no checkpoints, and since final votes cannot be withdrawn, a fork whose votes split can leave no side able to reach the quorum. All validators run as separate processes on one MacBook Air (Apple M1, eight cores, 16\,GiB) over loopback; signing records are synced with \texttt{fsync}, the ledger is not. Six equal-weight representatives hold the voting weight. A load generator funds 45{,}000 accounts and then publishes 45{,}000 blocks at an offered 2{,}000 blocks/s, each on the frontier of a randomly chosen account, following the owner policy of Section~\ref{sec:account}: it builds on a confirmed frontier or, once an unconfirmed non-fork frontier has waited 5\,s, extends it with a fresh child, and never extends a position where it published a fork. With forks enabled it publishes a conflicting sibling for 5\% or 10\% of the blocks, each sibling to half of the validators. An \emph{offline} validator runs no node but keeps its weight; a \emph{Byzantine} validator also runs no node, and the generator votes with its key at random, choosing any vote kind for recent blocks and contradicting itself. RAI uses 8\,s epochs. A run \emph{settles} when every running validator holds the same cemented state and, for RAI, has decided the same checkpoints; a position left retained by a split vote counts as settled when it is uncemented on every validator alike. We report non-fork blocks only: goodput is the number of non-fork blocks confirmed divided by the publishing-to-last-confirmation time, and latency runs from publication to confirmation at one validator. Each variant ran once per system on an otherwise idle machine. The baseline rows were taken in an earlier pass on the same host with unchanged baseline code; the RAI rows in one pass on the final prototype commit.

\para{Without forks.}
Table~\ref{tab:eval} shows both systems keeping up with the offered load. RAI's median latency is 116 to 131\,ms against 189 to 207\,ms: an uncontested block finalizes on one round of $N-p$ settled first votes, where the baseline needs a round of final votes after a quorum of non-final ones. With one validator offline or Byzantine RAI's p95 is 257 to 290\,ms against 234 to 238; with all six online one close took 7.9\,s, blocks one supporter short of a witness waited for it, and p95 reached 742\,ms. The other closes took 2.6 to 4.0\,s, one of them in a second round when leader rotation reached the missing validator. Of the blocks late-notarized during a close, 91 to 97\% finalized before the checkpoint.

\para{With forks.}
The baseline finished none of the six fork variants: forks whose votes split stay unresolved and occupy election slots, delaying the non-fork blocks behind them. RAI settled all six with every running validator deciding the same three to five checkpoints. At 5\% forks medians were 173 to 294\,ms and p95 0.9 to 1.4\,s; at 10\%, 383 to 740\,ms and 2.8 to 4.3\,s. Closes grow with the retained positions that reports, manifests, and witnesses carry: the longest took 8.4 to 9.8\,s, except at 10\% forks with one validator offline or Byzantine (14.2 and 15.7\,s), where a round led by that validator costs the timeout or a manifest to fetch and check. Most forked positions were resolved when the runs settled, by a matching-origin discharge or by the continuation of the retained block; 722 and 2{,}962 stayed retained in two variants, identically on every validator. Late-notarized blocks reached their closing-epoch witness in 80 to 90\% of cases.

\para{Restarts, reconfiguration, and handoff.}
A second harness kills validators mid-load or as a close starts, restarts them on their data, and checks every logged signature for conflicts. Synced signing raised the fork5 median from 139 to 173\,ms to 282 to 310\,ms with goodput unchanged; restarted validators rejoined by checkpoint catch-up. With eight validators of which six form the committee, at 800 blocks/s with 5\% forks, replacing two members settled in 3 of 3 runs, with a Byzantine member in every committee in 2 of 2, and replacing every correct member of a four-member committee ($f=1$, $p=0$) in 1 of 1; a departing member killed when its last close starts and kept down 30\,s left every checkpoint decided identically, with its handoff evidence released only after $N-f$ durable acknowledgements. No run produced a conflicting signature. These runs found a final vote the prototype released after installation without rechecking eligibility, which Section~\ref{sec:account} forbids, and a straggler that dropped the close-round votes it needed when its inbound queue overflowed. Committing overlap certificates and naming the base changed goodput and latency less than the spread between runs.

\para{Limitations.}
Each variant ran once, and the reconfiguration and restart scenarios once to three times; whether split positions end up resolved or retained has varied between passes. All validators share one host, so CPU contention rather than network latency sets the delays. Process kills test crash consistency only, not power loss.

\section{Conclusion}
RAI makes epoch transitions for account-local payments pipelined and safe: the next epoch's voting starts at each validator's local boundary while the previous epoch closes, immutable reports preserve latent and late finality, a deterministic construction produces a checkpoint with origin-stamped locks, and a predecessor gate with a prefix-wide overlap exception, fed by late notarizations and guarded by the early-vote rule, keeps successor finality compatible with whatever the outgoing committee could still certify. Safety and termination are relative to two abstract services, and model checking found the three failures that written arguments had missed. A prototype with synced signing settles all nine workload variants with non-fork medians of 116 to 131\,ms without forks, and stays consistent across restarts, committee changes, and a departing member's crash.
