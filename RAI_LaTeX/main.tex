\begin{abstract}
FastPay and Nano confirm each account's payments separately, without a global order. When the validators change, old votes can still complete a confirmation after the handover, so deployed systems pause the fast path at every epoch boundary. And when an owner signs two conflicting payments, the account stays locked until the next epoch, or forever. RAI changes validators without pausing. Each epoch closes with a checkpoint built from signed reports of the outgoing validators while the next epoch already votes, under rules that keep the two epochs consistent. The same checkpoint keeps one side of every double spend, which becomes final within two epochs of normal operation, so the owner gets the account back. We prove safety and termination and model-check the handover rules. Across six regions, a prototype keeps confirming through transitions that stall a stop-and-drain handover, with 6.7 to 38 times lower 95th-percentile latency.
\end{abstract}
