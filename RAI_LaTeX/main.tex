\begin{abstract}
Single-writer payments finalize from account-local quorums without a global transaction sequence. Replacing the validators that issue those quorums is the hard part: votes already released by the outgoing committee can still form a final certificate after the handoff, and a successor that does not honor them may finalize a conflicting history. RAI is an epoch-transition protocol that lets the next epoch's account voting proceed while the previous epoch closes, and that keeps the two safe against each other. Each outgoing validator freezes one signed report committing to a tagged ledger set and to the blocks it voted for outside that set. RAI defines which report selections are eligible checkpoint inputs and a deterministic construction that turns them into a checkpoint carrying finality and locks. Two components are left abstract: a \emph{convergence} service that makes frozen reports reconstructible at every correct validator, and a \emph{consensus} service that decides one eligible checkpoint per epoch. RAI is safe and terminates given the safety and termination of those two services, together with explicit fault, retained-data, and progress assumptions. Lagged committees, a predecessor gate for new work, and a narrow overlap exception make the pipeline safe without stopping account voting. We instantiate convergence with rateless set reconciliation and consensus with a Kudzu-style election in RsNano. The prototype keeps median non-fork latency near 100\,ms across three checkpoints at an offered 2{,}000 blocks/s and settles forked workloads, with one offline or one Byzantine validator, that the unmodified node does not finish; its latency tails show the pipeline's cost, since work published while an epoch closes waits for the checkpoint.
\end{abstract}
