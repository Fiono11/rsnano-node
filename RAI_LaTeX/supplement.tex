\section{Full Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}
\label{sec:correctness}
The arguments concern the core protocol, including the prefix-wide overlap guard, late notarization, the early-vote rule, origin-stamped records, and Rule~3 promotion, and are relative to the services of Appendix~\ref{sec:services}: safety uses C1 and A1--A3, termination additionally C2 and A4. Cryptographic guarantees hold except with negligible probability.

\begin{lemma}[Account exclusion]
\label{lem:exclusion}
In one account instance at most one block has an exclusion witness; in particular at most one has an $\NC$. If an $\FC$ or $\FF$ for $B$ can be formed in that instance, no conflicting block has an exclusion witness. If an $\FF$ for $B$ can be formed, every conflicting block has at most $f+p<r$ supporters.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Two sets of $q$ supporters intersect in at least $2q-N=f+1$ identities, one of them correct, and a correct validator supports one block per instance (Definition~\ref{def:support}), by a first vote or by a late notarization. An $\FC$ for $B$ has $q$ final voters; a correct final voter first-voted $B$ in the same instance, so the same intersection applies against any $\XW$ of a rival. An $\FF$ has $N-p\ge q$ first voters. For the last claim let $f'\le f$ be the actual number of Byzantine members. An $\FF$ has at least $N-p-f'$ correct first voters, so at most $p$ correct members and $f'$ Byzantine members can support a rival: at most $p+f'\le p+f$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Notarized ancestry]
\label{lem:ancestry}
If an epoch-$e$ $\NC$, $\FC$, or $\FF$ for a block $D$ can be formed, then every ancestor $X$ of $D$ is final, or is held under a notarization lock of a decided checkpoint (as its target or as an ancestor of it), or has an epoch-$e$ $\NC$ that can be formed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each of the three certificates contains at least $q\ge f+1$ first votes for $D$ (a correct final voter first-voted the same block in the instance), so some correct validator first-voted $D$. By the attachment rule of Appendix~\ref{sec:eligibility} it did so on a parent $P$ that it held final, held under a notarization lock, or held complete in epoch $e$, that is, with an $\NC_e(P)$ in hand. Ancestors of a final block are final, and ancestors of a notarization-lock target are retained under that lock, which Rule~1 creates with the target's ancestry and the inherited-state rule never drops while the target is unresolved. In the third case $\NC_e(P)$ can be formed and the argument repeats for $P$; the chain is finite.
\end{proof}
The lemma rests on the re-notarization rule of Appendix~\ref{sec:eligibility}: a tip held by recovery records alone is not a final parent. If such a tip could always be extended, the lemma would fail, and with it Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} (Case~5 of Appendix~\ref{app:cases}).

\para{Proof structure.}
Theorems~\ref{thm:closure} and~\ref{thm:safety} are proved by one induction over closures, from genesis. The step for closure $e$ uses Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} for finality through epoch $e$ (in Rule~1's exclusion of conflicts with $F^\star$ and in the earlier-epochs case) and Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} for closure $e-1$ (the closure invariant of $S_{e-1}$); Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} up to epoch $e+1$ uses Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} for closures up to $e$. Each proof below cites the other only within this order.

\begin{lemma}[Late notarization adds no old finality]
\label{lem:late}
Every epoch-$e$ $\NC$, $\FC$, or $\FF$ consists of first and final votes released by correct signers before they left epoch $e$, together with Byzantine votes. A correct validator releases a final vote, or records an $\tagN$ entry, only for a block for which it holds an $\NC$ of $q$ first votes.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By Definition~\ref{def:support} and the certificate definitions of Appendix~\ref{sec:voting} none of the three certificates counts a late notarization, and a correct validator releases no epoch-$e$ first or final vote after leaving $e$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:lifecycle}). Completeness is defined on $\NC_e$, and notarization and final voting require completeness.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
A restarted prototype validator signs its recorded votes again (Appendix~\ref{app:proto}), so a vote recorded but not signed before a crash can be released for the first time after the validator left the epoch, which the lemma's first sentence excludes. The proofs are unaffected: the decision was recorded before the report froze and none follows the freeze, so the report lists the vote, and the first votes behind a final vote are written with its decision, so a report built after a restart still tags the block $\tagN$. Lemma~\ref{lem:exposure} therefore holds for it, and Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} counts it whenever a certificate is completed. \texttt{RaiCloseRestart} shows that the durable notarization is needed; no model covers signing a vote again.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Frozen-report exposure]
\label{lem:exposure}
Let $i$ be a correct reporter for closure $e$. If $i$ released an epoch-$e$ final vote for $B$, then $T_i$ tags $B$ with $\tagN$ or $\tagF$. If $i$ released an epoch-$e$ first vote for $B$, then $T_i$ tags $B$ with $\tagN$ or $\tagF$, or $h(B)\in G_i$. This holds whatever other tag $B$ inherits from $S_{e-1}$, and it persists after the report is frozen.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A final vote follows durable notarization, which is an $\tagN$ entry unless finality has replaced it by $\tagF$. For a first vote, $h(B)\in V_i^{(e)}$, and $G_i$ removes from $V_i^{(e)}$ exactly the hashes tagged $\tagN$ or $\tagF$. Stopping precedes freezing; roots and tags are then immutable, and C1 ensures that what a correct validator reconstructs is what was frozen.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
With $G_i=V_i^{(e)}\setminus\keys(T_i)$ instead, the second statement fails when $S_{e-1}$ carries $B$ under a recovery record: $T_i$ tags $B$ with $\tagR$ and the fresh vote appears nowhere. \texttt{RaiClose} with \texttt{FIXB = FALSE} reaches two conflicting fast certificates this way (Appendix~\ref{app:models}).
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Signers hold the witness]
\label{lem:holds}
Every correct signer of an eligible epoch-$e$ $\FC$ or $\FF$ for $B$ holds, as retained evidence, an exclusion witness discharging each recovery record of $S_{e-1}$ on a block that conflicts with $B$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An $\FF_e(B)$ consists of settled first votes. A correct signer of one had installed $S_{e-1}$ when signing; the voting rule of Appendix~\ref{sec:install} refuses a rival of a retained block unless every record on it is discharged, and the signer's admission witness fixes the witnesses it used, so it holds them. A correct final voter either released its final vote after installing $S_{e-1}$, with the same check, or before. In the second case the early-vote rule required it to hold $\XW_{e-1}(B)$ for $B$ and every unresolved block of the selected prefix. That witness discharges every record on a rival that closure $e-1$ created, since those have origin $e-1$ (Rule~2 stamps the closing epoch). Records that $S_{e-1}$ inherits from $S_{e-2}$ were known to the voter, because $S_{e-2}$ is installed before the overlap of closure $e-1$ begins (at most one closing epoch per validator), and the overlap guard required the matching-origin witness for each of them.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Closure invariant]
\label{def:invariant}
A closed state $S_e$ satisfies the closure invariant if, for every epoch $e'\le e$ and every block $B$ with an eligible epoch-$e'$ account final certificate that can be formed from released signatures, $S_e$ holds $B$ (i)~as final, (ii)~under a notarization lock, or (iii)~under a recovery record of origin $e'$, directly or as an ancestor of a block so held.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Closure preserves latent and late finality]
\label{thm:closure}
Let $S_{e-1}$ be a valid closed predecessor that satisfies the closure invariant, and let $v$ be any candidate with $\Elig_e(v)$ and state $S_e$. Let $B$ have an eligible epoch-$e$ account final certificate that can be formed, possibly completed after reports froze or after the checkpoint was decided, and assume
\begin{itemize}
\item[(W)] $B$ conflicts with no lock record carried by $S_{e-1}$, or every correct signer of the certificate holds, as retained evidence, an exclusion witness that discharges each such record.
\end{itemize}
Under the early-vote rule, (W) holds for every eligible certificate (Lemma~\ref{lem:holds}); without that rule it can fail (Appendix~\ref{app:open}). Then $S_e$ holds $B$ as final, under a notarization lock, or under a recovery record of origin $e$, directly or as an ancestor of a block so held; no admissible evidence discharges that hold; and $S_e$ satisfies the closure invariant for all certificates covered by (W). The same holds for every ancestor of $B$ that the certificate finalizes with it.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $Q$ be the selected reporters, $|Q|=N-f$, and let $A$ be the signer set of the certificate.

\emph{Eligibility of $B$ in the construction.} If $B$ conflicts with no carried record there is nothing to show. Otherwise, by (W), each correct signer holds an exclusion witness for every carried record that $B$ bypasses. That witness is retained evidence; reconstructing the signer's report material delivers it, the manifest contains it, and the record is discharged at line~6 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:build}. So $B$ is eligible for Rules~1 and~2 whenever a correct signer is selected, which the counts below guarantee.

\emph{Normal certificate.} $A$ is a set of $q$ final voters, so $|(Q\cap A)\setminus\Byz|\ge(N-f)+q-N-f=q-2f=p+1\ge1$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:exposure} each such reporter froze an $\tagN$ or $\tagF$ entry for $B$. A verified $\tagF$ entry puts $B$ in $F^\star$. Otherwise $B$ is a represented $\NC$ target; it is the unique one at its position by Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}, it does not conflict with $F^\star$ by the induction hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:safety}, and Rule~1 retains it under a notarization lock.

\emph{Fast certificate.} $A$ is a set of $N-p$ settled first voters, so $|(Q\cap A)\setminus\Byz|\ge(N-f)+(N-p)-N-f=N-p-2f=r$. Take these reporters. If any selected report has a verified $\tagF$ entry for $B$ we are in the first case. Else if any has a verified $\tagN$ entry for $B$, Rule~1 applies as above. Else, by Lemma~\ref{lem:exposure}, each of the $r$ reporters has $h(B)\in G_i$ with a verified settled first vote, whether or not $T_i$ tags $B$ with $\tagR$; hence $|U_Q(B)|\ge r$.

Rule~2's threshold pass works down from the highest position, so we first show that no record placed by Rule~1 or by that pass above $B$'s position is on a branch that conflicts with $B$. Such a record is on an eligible block $D$ with a represented $\NC_e(D)$ or with $r\ge f+1$ selected reporters' settled first votes, so some correct validator first-voted $D$ in epoch $e$. Let $Y\neq Z$ be the blocks of $D$'s and $B$'s branches at the first position where they differ, $Z$ being $B$ or an ancestor of $B$. The argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestry}, applied to that correct first vote, shows that $Y$ is final, held under a notarization lock of a decided checkpoint, or has an $\NC_e(Y)$ that can be formed; Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestry} for $B$'s certificate shows the same for $Z$. If either is final or notarization-locked, the other's branch conflicts with a final block, contradicting Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} through epoch~$e$, or bypasses a notarization lock, which makes $B$ or $D$ ineligible. So both $\NC_e(Y)$ and $\NC_e(Z)$ can be formed, or $\NC_e(Y)$ and $\FF_e(B)$ if $Z=B$, contradicting Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}. Hence when the threshold pass reaches $B$'s position, either a record above holds a descendant of $B$, and $B$ with it, or the position is not yet held and Rule~2 runs there. No rival has a represented $\NC$, and every rival $B'$ has $|U_Q(B')|\le f+p<r$, both by Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}; so $B$ is the unique eligible block reaching $r$, and the threshold pass retains it under a recovery record of origin $e$. The keep pass runs afterwards, only where nothing is held, and retains no block that conflicts with a record already chosen.

The order is essential. Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion} bounds only the rivals at $B$'s own position. A rival of an ancestor $Z$ of $B$ may have $r$ reporter first votes when $Z$ has only an $\NC_e$, and a pass that worked upward would retain that rival at $Z$'s position before reaching $B$, and then drop $B$ as conflicting. Appendix~\ref{app:bottomup} gives the run.

\emph{No discharge.} A notarization lock is never bypassed. A record of origin $e$ on $B$ is discharged only by $\XW_e(B')$ for a conflicting $B'$, which Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion} excludes because an epoch-$e$ final certificate for $B$ can be formed. Evidence of other epochs does not apply to it, and discharging records of other origins on $B$ leaves it in place.

\emph{Finality through a descendant.} Let $X$ be an ancestor of $B$ that is not final before the certificate. If $S_{e-1}$ holds $X$ final or under a notarization lock, the inherited-state rule keeps it. Otherwise $S_e$ holds $X$ as an ancestor of $B$, by the cases above, and the question is whether that hold can be discharged. A hold through $B$ ends only when every record on $B$ is discharged or a final block excludes $B$. A record of origin $e$ on $B$ is discharged only by $\XW_e(Y)$ for the block $Y$ of a conflicting branch at the first position where that branch leaves $B$'s; that position holds $B$ or an ancestor $X$ of $B$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestry}, $X$ is final, under a notarization lock, or has an $\NC_e(X)$ that can be formed; the first two exclude the conflicting branch outright, and in the third Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion} excludes $\XW_e(Y)$. A notarization lock on $B$ or finality of $B$ is never undone. If instead the threshold pass holds $B$ through a record of origin $e$ on a descendant $D$, that record is discharged only by an $\XW_e$ for a block that leaves $D$'s branch at some position; $D$ has a correct settled first voter, so, as above, the block of $D$'s branch at that position is final, under a notarization lock, or has an $\NC_e$ that can be formed, and Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion} excludes the witness. This step needs Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestry}: if a tip held by recovery records alone could be extended, an $\XW_e(Y)$ could exist at an ancestor's position, and in Case~5 of Appendix~\ref{app:cases} $Y$ even becomes final.

\emph{Earlier epochs.} For $e'<e$ the predecessor holds $B$ as final, under a notarization lock, or under a record of origin $e'$. Finality is inherited. A notarization lock and a record of origin $e'$ are carried forward by the inherited-state rule, since the only ways to drop them are a conflicting final block, excluded by Theorem~\ref{thm:safety}, and an $\XW_{e'}$ of a rival, excluded by Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}.

The argument uses only signatures in $A$ and their intersection with $Q$, so it does not matter when the certificate is assembled, and unselected or Byzantine signatures cannot remove the correct intersections.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Checkpoint-promotion safety]
\label{lem:promotion}
A Rule~3 promoted block cannot conflict with a block that has a closing-epoch exclusion witness, $\FC$, or $\FF$. Hence a decision that finalizes the promoted prefix is compatible with old-domain finality and with core overlap finality.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every promoted block has a verified closing-epoch $\NC$ of first votes, which is an exclusion witness; Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion} excludes an exclusion witness, and so an $\FC$ or $\FF$, for a conflicting block. Core overlap finality for a conflicting successor block requires a closing-epoch exclusion witness at the first divergent position, which is the same contradiction. Promotion stops at retained forks and never promotes recovery-only evidence.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Acyclic final dependencies]
\label{lem:dag}
For any finite set of valid final blocks closed under account ancestors and required finalized sends, parent and send-to-receive edges form a directed acyclic graph.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Order blocks by the earliest event at which valid final evidence justifies them. Along a parent edge this event cannot become earlier at the child; along a send-to-receive edge it is strictly later at the receive, because admission required the send's finality proof and Rule~3 cannot supply it for the checkpoint under construction. A cycle cannot consist only of parent edges, since slots increase strictly, so it contains a send-to-receive edge and a strict increase around a cycle, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The overlap verdict does not depend on installation order]
\label{lem:verdict}
Let closure $e$ be in progress, let $B$ and $B'$ conflict at one position, and let $\OC$ be an overlap certificate for $B$: an exclusion witness $\XW_e(B)$, an $\NC_{e+1}(B)$, an epoch-$(e+1)$ $\FC$ or $\FF$ for $B$ or a descendant selecting it, and an exclusion witness of the matching origin for every recovery record on $B'$ that $S_{e-1}$ carries, with no notarization lock on $B'$ in $S_{e-1}$. Then for every candidate with $\Elig_e$ and state $S_e$: (a)~$S_e$ does not finalize $B'$; (b)~$S_e$ carries no notarization lock on $B'$; (c)~every recovery record that $S_e$ carries on $B'$ is discharged by a witness contained in $\OC$. Consequently a validator that holds $\OC$ accepts $B$ as final under the overlap exception before installing $S_e$ if and only if it accepts $B$ by ordinary rechecking after installing $S_e$, and a later manifest that contains $\OC$ verifies an $\tagF$ claim for $B$ against $S_e$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(a) $S_e$ finalizes a block through inherited finality, an explicit closing-epoch account certificate, or Rule~3. Inherited finality of $B'$ contradicts the check of $\OC$ against $S_{e-1}$, which is installed before the overlap of closure $e$ begins. An epoch-$e$ $\FC$ or $\FF$ for $B'$, and the $\NC_e(B')$ that Rule~3 requires, are excluded by $\XW_e(B)$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}. (b) A new notarization lock needs a represented $\NC_e(B')$, excluded the same way; an inherited one is excluded by hypothesis. (c) A record on $B'$ in $S_e$ is either created by Rule~2 at this closure, with origin $e$, and then $\XW_e(B)\subseteq\OC$ discharges it; or inherited from $S_{e-1}$, and then $\OC$ contains its discharge witness by hypothesis.

For the consequence: before installation the validator checks exactly the contents of $\OC$ against $S_{e-1}$. After installation, ordinary rechecking tests $B$ against the frontier, the finalized history, and the carried records of $S_e$; by (a)--(c) these tests pass on the evidence in $\OC$ and on no other. The same evaluation is what line~6 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:build} performs at closure $e+1$. The lemma is stated for one divergent position; the prefix-wide guard applies it at the first divergent unresolved position of the finalized prefix, as the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} does, and the \texttt{RaiPrefix} model of Appendix~\ref{app:models} checks that guard on two positions.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Deterministic construction]
\label{lem:construction}
Assume the predecessor satisfies the closure invariant. For fixed usable selected reports and a valid immutable manifest, $\Build$ returns one canonical candidate state at every correct validator. It preserves inherited finality, carries all mandatory surviving records, and applies only explicit account finality plus Rule~3 promotion. All-correct usable inputs yield a finite valid construction.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By C1 every correct validator reconstructs the same $(T_i,G_i)$ for each selected header, and the manifest fixes witnesses and proof choices; hence explicit finality, discharges, and $R_Q$ are the same everywhere, with lock records compared as sets of (block, strength, origin). Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}, predecessor validity, attachment, and Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} make the retained finality and records compatible. Rule~2's two passes visit positions in a fixed order, each against the records already chosen, and use only the selected settled votes, the predecessor, and the hash order, so they choose the same records everywhere. Rule~3's tests fix $P_Q$, safe by Lemma~\ref{lem:promotion}; Lemma~\ref{lem:dag} and the canonical tie-break fix replay. An $\tagN$ entry of a correct reporter is backed by an $\NC$ of first votes (Lemma~\ref{lem:late}), which the manifest can carry, so the entry verifies. An $\tagF$ entry of a correct reporter for a block finalized under the overlap exception is backed by its overlap certificate, which is retained evidence; by Lemma~\ref{lem:verdict} line~6 accepts the claim and discharges every conflicting record of $S_{e-1}$, so no all-correct selection is rejected on that account. Malformed Byzantine inputs are excluded by usability; all-correct usable inputs yield a finite state.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Transfer safety across epochs]
\label{thm:safety}
Assuming C1 and A1--A3, finalized histories at correct validators are compatible and irreversible across all core-protocol epochs; finalized balances are nonnegative, and each send is received at most once.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct from genesis, jointly with Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}. \emph{Within an epoch}, let two blocks be final through epoch-$e$ certificates for $D$ and $D'$ whose branches conflict, and let $X\ne X'$ be their blocks at the first position where the branches differ. Conflicting finality need not be visible at a single position: either certificate may finalize $X$ or $X'$ only as an ancestor. By Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestry} each of $X$, $X'$ is final through older evidence, held under a notarization lock, or has an $\NC_e$ that can be formed. Two $\NC_e$ at one position contradict Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}. A notarization lock on $X$ is never bypassed: a correct validator that installed it refuses $X'$ and its descendants, and one that had not accepts $D'$ only under an overlap certificate, whose $\XW_{e-1}(X')$ contradicts the closing-epoch $\NC$ behind a new lock (Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}) and whose check against the latest closed state refuses an inherited one. Finality through older evidence is the boundary case. At a boundary, A2 ensures that the decided $v_e$ is eligible, so by Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} it preserves every formable old final branch and by Lemma~\ref{lem:promotion} its promotion is compatible; A1 and A3 ensure that every correct validator installs the state of the same $v_e$, which by Lemma~\ref{lem:construction} is the same state. For overlap, the first divergent unresolved position on an early successor-final prefix has a closing-epoch exclusion witness for the successor block by the prefix-wide guard, whose late notarizations add no old finality that Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} would have to preserve (Lemma~\ref{lem:late}); a conflicting closing-epoch certificate or a conflicting Rule~3 promotion would require a conflicting $\NC$, contradicting Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}; and by Lemma~\ref{lem:verdict} correct validators that hold the same overlap certificate reach the same verdict whether they verify it before or after installing $S_e$, so they agree on which overlap blocks are final and not only on the compatibility of what they have finalized. Matching-origin discharge and inherited records remain enforced after installation, and the base/overlay union with applied-operation identifiers prevents rollback and duplicate application. Replay checks balances and finalized-send dependencies.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Correct reports become usable]
\label{lem:reports-live}
Under the data service, finite relevant admitted work, fair processing, and C2, every correct report eventually becomes usable at every correct validator.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A correct reporter stops old first and final voting, so its old vote set and roots are finite, and its report is well formed: every $\tagN$ entry rests on an $\NC$ of first votes (Lemma~\ref{lem:late}), every $\tagF$ entry on inherited finality, an account certificate, or a retained overlap certificate, and every $G_i$ member on its own first vote and on no hash tagged $\tagN$ or $\tagF$. By C2 every correct validator eventually reconstructs $(T_i,G_i)$. The data service delivers the blocks, ancestry, signed votes, late notarizations, and overlap certificates behind its claims, and fair processing completes the finite validation. A withholding Byzantine reporter can make only its own report unusable; $N-f$ correct reports remain.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Handoff termination]
\label{thm:handoff-live}
Assume the data service, fair processing, C2, A4, and recurring prepared windows with $N-f$ responsive correct members of $K_e$ long enough for reconstruction, candidate preparation, and the consensus service's rounds. Every enabled handoff is eventually decided and, with eventual retained-data delivery, installed by correct successors.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:reports-live}, $N-f$ correct reports become usable at every correct member, which by Lemma~\ref{lem:construction} can then construct an eligible candidate; the canonical selection order makes correct members that hold the same usable reports construct the same one. A4 then decides some eligible value in a sufficient window; A3 lets successors verify it, and retained authorizing data let them recompute and install the state, which enables the next deferred boundary.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Account continuation and forked positions]
\label{thm:account-live}
Assume handoff termination, available final-send dependencies, and recurring common-open ready intervals.
(a)~An owner following the retry-or-fresh-child policy of Appendix~\ref{sec:account} eventually finalizes each target of a finite valid uncontested continuation, provided each required new position has such an interval, and the finalized targets eventually enter a checkpoint.
(b)~Let $(a,v)$ be a position whose parent is final when epoch $e$ starts and at which some valid block is available. If epochs $e$ and $e+1$ are \emph{good}, some block at $(a,v)$ is final by the end of epoch $e+1$, whatever the owner and the Byzantine validators do. An epoch is good if every correct member of its committee installs the predecessor checkpoint, receives every block and vote that a correct validator sends at the position, and casts every first and final vote the rules allow it before freezing its report.
(c)~Hence the owner of a forked account, once it signs no further conflicting blocks, finalizes its continuation as in (a) after the forked position is final; and at most one block at the position ever becomes final.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(a)~In a ready interval, $N-f\ge q$ correct first votes create an $\NC$ and the same validators can final-vote; after they install the predecessor their first votes are settled, so an available $N-p$ fast quorum finalizes in two stages. An attempt interrupted by a boundary is preserved by Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}; an omitted block can be retried, and a uniquely retained notarized target may finalize at the handoff.

(b)~Consider epoch $e$. Every correct member installs $S_{e-1}$ and first-votes at the position before freezing; where the position is contested for it, it votes only after installing $S_{e-1}$ and then, if $S_{e-1}$ holds an unresolved block $K$ there, only $K$. Suppose no block at the position is final at the end of $e$. Then the position is contested at every correct member: if $S_{e-1}$ holds a block there, every correct member installed it; otherwise, if all correct members first-voted the same block $B$, then $B$ had $N-f\ge q$ correct first votes, every correct member final-voted it after installing $S_{e-1}$ (nothing there refuses it), and $B$ would be final; so correct members voted two blocks, and each has seen both.

Next, $S_e$ holds an unresolved block $K'$ at the position or finalizes one. If a selected report represents an eligible $\NC_e$, Rule~1 locks it, and Rule~3 promotes it unless another block stays retained there. Otherwise, a record of $S_{e-1}$ that survives keeps its block, or the threshold pass of Rule~2 keeps a block there, directly or through a descendant. If neither does, the keep pass reaches the position: it retains $S_{e-1}$'s block there if it is still eligible, and otherwise the eligible block with the most settled reporter votes. One has a vote: the selected reports contain at least $N-2f\ge1$ correct reports, each listing its member's epoch-$e$ first vote unless it tags it $\tagN$ or $\tagF$, and in a good epoch every correct first vote is settled. Every block at the position is eligible there, since nothing final and no record, inherited or new, holds the position.

Now consider epoch $e+1$. The position is contested at every correct member, so each first-votes there only after installing $S_e$, and then only $K'$, unless a rival is final, which would end the argument. The cross-epoch rule does not interfere, since it lapses at installation. $K'$ is retained, so every correct member admits its instance, and in a good epoch it receives $N-f\ge q$ correct first votes. The same members final-vote $K'$ after installation, which the termination rules allow because $K'$ is the block $S_e$ keeps, and $q$ correct final votes make it final. A block whose parent position is final needs no complete parent, and the re-notarization rule concerns only children of $K'$.

(c)~Once a block at the forked position is final, the account's later positions are uncontested if the owner signs no further conflicting blocks, and (a) applies; a fresh child of the final block is the owner's continuation. At most one block at the position becomes final by Theorem~\ref{thm:safety}.

The rules of (b) only withhold votes, except Rule~2's choice, which retains every block that may be fast-final (Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}); so they do not weaken safety. By Lemma~\ref{lem:flip}, the side the checkpoints keep at the position changes at most once on the way. Without them the position can stay open forever (Appendix~\ref{app:stall}). Normal finality takes three delivery stages after broadcast and an $N-p$ settled first-vote quorum two; a sufficient account interval budget is $D/(1+\rho)>\kappa_A+R_A+3\delta$, where $\kappa_A$ bounds opening skew and $R_A$ readiness after the last opening, including predecessor closure.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[One change of side]
\label{lem:flip}
Under the termination rules, let a decided checkpoint $S_k$ hold a block $K$ at a position whose parent is final. Then $S_{k+1}$ holds $K$ there, or a conflicting block $K'$ that has an eligible final certificate or a notarization lock in $S_{k+1}$; every later checkpoint then holds $K'$. Hence the block that the checkpoints hold at such a position changes at most once.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A final block and a notarization lock are never released, and a block with an eligible final certificate stays held by Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}; so it suffices to consider $S_k$ holding $K$ by recovery records only, and $S_{k+1}$ holding neither $K'$ case. A correct validator that installed $S_k$ casts no first vote at the position or above it on a branch that bypasses $K$'s records, unless the bypassing block is final at it, which is the first case (\emph{checkpoints decide}). So every settled epoch-$(k+1)$ first vote of a correct validator on a branch through the position is on $K$'s branch. A threshold record at or above the position needs $r\ge f+1$ selected settled votes, one of them correct, so it holds $K$; a Rule~1 record of a rival is excluded by assumption. If a record of $S_k$ on $K$ survives, $S_{k+1}$ holds $K$. Otherwise the keep pass reaches the position with nothing held there and retains $S_k$'s block $K$, which is still eligible: it conflicts with no final block and no notarization lock by assumption, and with no other record by the above.
\end{proof}
The proof uses both parts of Rule~2. With the hash order in place of the predecessor's block, a witness that discharges $K$'s records lets a Byzantine listing move the position, and \texttt{RaiRecoverMany} reaches two changes of side (\texttt{Many2\_routeA\_down\_hashpick\_flip}). With every first vote counted, simulation found no second change (\texttt{Many2\_routeA\_down\_allvotes\_flip}): \emph{no early votes at a contested position} already keeps early votes away from a position once an installed checkpoint holds it. With every first vote counted and the downward order, the safety checks found no violation either, in 32{,}503 random traces and in every run of up to 15 steps (\texttt{Many2\_routeA\_any\_down\_allvotes\_safe} and \texttt{\_E3\_bfs}), and the prototype finalizes forks left after the load about twice as fast with it (Appendix~\ref{app:proto}). We count settled votes because the proof then does not rely on that rule, and because the recipes of Table~\ref{tab:tlc-recover} that count every first vote in another order change sides twice.

\subsection{Early votes against a carried lock}
\label{app:open}
Without the early-vote rule, hypothesis (W) of Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} is not cosmetic. The following run violates the theorem when the early-vote rule is dropped, whatever the definition of the second set. \texttt{RaiHandoff} finds it with \texttt{RULE = FALSE}. Take $N=6$ with validator~6 Byzantine, and conflicting blocks $B$, $B'$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Epoch $e$: validators 1, 2, 6 first-vote $B'$. Validators 3, 4, 5 cross their boundary without voting at this position.
\item Before $S_e$ is known, 3, 4, 5 first-vote $B$ in epoch $e+1$ and late-notarize it in $e$. Validator~6 signs both votes for $B$ and shows its late notarization to validator~1 only.
\item Closure $e$ selects reports 1, 2, 3, 4, 6. Rule~2 gives $B'$ a recovery record of origin $e$.
\item Validators 3, 4, 5 install $S_e$ without holding $\XW_e(B)$; their early work on $B$ is invalid in their view. Validator~1 installs $S_e$, holds $\XW_e(B)=\{3,4,5,6\}$, discharges the record and first-votes $B$ in $e+1$. Now $\{1,3,4,5,6\}$ is an $\FF_{e+1}(B)$ that can be formed.
\item Closure $e+1$ selects reports 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. The only correct holder of the witness is not selected, the manifest has no $\XW_e(B)$, the votes for $B$ are an unexplained speculative path, and $S_{e+1}$ retains $B'$ only.
\item In $e+2$ validators 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 first-vote the retained $B'$: $\FF_{e+2}(B')$. Validator~6 then publishes its signatures; $B$ has an $\FF_{e+1}$ whose eligibility against $S_e$ follows from $\XW_e(B)$.
\end{enumerate}
With a committee change the same failure needs no late notarization: four new members early-vote $B$, the old committee's first votes for $B$ plus one withheld Byzantine vote are the exclusion witness, and the second closure sees four unexplained votes. The cause is that the eligibility of an early first vote can depend on a witness its signer never held. The early-vote rule removes the dependency: in step~4 the set $\{1,3,4,5,6\}$ is not a fast certificate, because three of its votes are early, and validators 3, 4, 5 final-vote $B$ only once they hold $\XW_e(B)$, in which case their reports deliver it to the next closure.

Two alternatives fail. Retaining both blocks at the second closure keeps the one-round path but can stall a contested position permanently after a committee change. Counting only early votes of members of the closing committee is unsafe: the committee-change run above defeats it.

\subsection{Why forked positions need the termination rules}
\label{app:stall}
Without the termination rules of Appendix~\ref{sec:voting} and Rule~2's keep pass, a forked position can stay open forever although every epoch is good. Take the first run of Case~5 with the re-notarization rule, which refuses the owner's child $C'$ until $B'$ is notarized again. In each later epoch validators 3, 4, 5 first-vote $B$ again: before installing the checkpoint the cross-epoch rule allows re-voting the same block, and after it the old witness $\XW_e(B)$ still admits $B$. Validators 1 and 2 vote $B'$, and validator~6 withholds the one vote either side needs. Neither block is notarized, the next checkpoint keeps $B'$ again, and the owner cannot help: $C'$ waits for $B'$. Nor could a different child rule help: in the second run $B$ may already be final when the owner publishes $C'$, and validators 1 and 2 cannot tell the two runs apart. Recovery cannot rest on the owner extending the kept block either, since that route is unsafe.

Each rule removes one ingredient. With \emph{checkpoints decide}, validators 3, 4, 5 stop voting $B$ after installing the checkpoint; with \emph{no early votes at a contested position}, they do not re-vote it before (without this rule alone, \texttt{RaiResolve} finds the stall in 20 steps: in two good epochs the correct validators re-vote their old sides early, two against one, and the Byzantine validator withholds the third vote); and with Rule~2's keep pass, a checkpoint never leaves a voted position empty, which an adversary could otherwise split again. \texttt{RaiResolve} (Appendix~\ref{app:models}) finds the stall with the rules off, also when the position starts uncontested, and checks with them on that two good epochs finalize the position.

\subsection{Rule~2 from the lowest position}
\label{app:bottomup}
Consider a Rule~2 that keeps, position by position from the lowest, the block with the most selected reporters' first votes of any kind, ties by hash. It violates Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} and Theorem~\ref{thm:safety}. \texttt{RaiRecoverMany} finds the following run in 40 steps (\texttt{Many2\_allup\_hole\_tight}), and its mirror image without a script (\texttt{Many2\_allup\_nc}, 57 steps). Take $N=6$ with validator~6 Byzantine, conflicting blocks $A_1$, $A_2$ at position~7, and a child $C_i$ of each $A_i$ at position~8. The owner signs $C_i$ only once a checkpoint keeps $A_i$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Epoch~1: validator~1 first-votes $A_1$, validator~2 first-votes $A_2$, and nobody else votes at position~7. Closure~1 keeps $A_2$ under a recovery record of origin~1: the Byzantine report lists $A_2$, two votes against one.
\item Epoch~2, before installing $S_1$: validators 2 and 4 first-vote $A_2$ early, 3 and 5 first-vote $A_1$ early; each has seen one block only, and $S_0$ keeps nothing at position~7. 3 and 5 also late-notarize $A_1$ in epoch~1, so with 1's vote and 6, $A_1$ has an $\XW_1$.
\item Validator~1 installs $S_1$ and first-votes the kept $A_2$: with 2, 4, and 6 that is an $\NC_2(A_2)$, so under the re-notarization rule $C_2$ may now be voted. Validators 1, 2, 4, 5 install $S_1$ and first-vote $C_2$; with 6 that is an $\FF_2(C_2)$, which finalizes $A_2$ too. Validator~5 voted $A_1$ at position~7 and $C_2$ at position~8, which no rule forbids, since a vote for a child is not a vote for its parent.
\item Closure~2 selects reports 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, none of which shows $A_2$ notarized. $\XW_1(A_1)$ discharges $S_1$'s record on $A_2$. Rule~2 at position~7 counts 3, 5, and 6 for $A_1$, which is $r$, and 2 and 4 for $A_2$; it keeps $A_1$ under a record of origin~2. At position~8, $C_2$ now conflicts with that record and is dropped, although $\FF_2(C_2)$ can be formed.
\item Epoch~3: every correct validator installs $S_2$ and votes only $A_1$ at position~7. Validators 1, 2, 3, 4 first-vote it settled; with 6 that is an $\FF_3(A_1)$. $A_1$ and $A_2$ are both final.
\end{enumerate}
The proof's fast-certificate case bounds rivals only at $B$'s own position by $f+p$, but $A_1$ is a rival of $C_2$'s parent, which had only an $\NC$. Counting settled votes only removes this run, since 3's and 5's votes for $A_1$ are early, but not its shape: an owner that signs $C_1$ at once lets $C_1$ be voted in the fork epoch itself, where nothing is kept yet. In epoch~1, 2, 3, 4 first-vote $A_1$, which is notarized with 6; 1 and 5 vote $A_2$; 1, 2, 4, 5 vote $C_1$, an $\FF_1(C_1)$ with 6; closure~1 selects 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, where $A_2$ has the $r$ settled votes of 1, 5, and 6, and keeps $A_2$ (\texttt{Many2\_routeA\_any\_up\_safe}, 20 steps). The threshold pass from the highest position removes both runs: $C_i$ has the settled votes of $r$ selected reporters and is retained first, with its parent, and Rule~2 skips position~7. Ancestor support, under which a first vote for a child also supports its parent, would remove them as well, since no correct validator could then support $A_1$ at position~7 and a child of $A_2$ at position~8 in one epoch; we chose the order because it changes only the construction.

\section{The TLA\textsuperscript{+} Models}
\label{app:models}
\label{sec:mc}
The nine modules (the eight models of the main text, which counts \texttt{RaiRecover} and \texttt{RaiRecoverMany} as one), their TLC configurations, a run script, and the TLC outputs (including the violating traces) are in the \texttt{tla/} directory of the artifact. \texttt{RaiClose}, \texttt{RaiOverlap}, and \texttt{RaiHandoff} take one account position with two conflicting blocks $X$ and $Y$, $N=6$, $f=p=1$, five correct validators and one Byzantine validator whose signatures are available for every block, vote kind, and epoch and whose report is chosen adversarially subject to the usability checks of Appendix~\ref{sec:usable}. \texttt{RaiPrefix} and \texttt{RaiRecovery} take two positions and are run with $N=4$ ($f=1$, $p=0$) and $N=6$. \texttt{RaiResolve} takes one position over three epochs and is run with $N=4$. The consensus service is the nondeterministic choice of one eligible candidate from any $N-f$ usable reports. TLC explores all interleavings; a \texttt{VIEW} identifies validators up to permutation. The runs of Table~\ref{tab:tlc} used TLC build 2026-10-06 (two workers) and TLC~2.20 (eight workers, \texttt{RaiPrefix}).

\para{Invariant names.}
\texttt{NoConflict} and \texttt{Agreement}: no two conflicting blocks are final (Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} at the position). \texttt{ClosureThm1}, \texttt{ClosureThm1a}, \texttt{ClosureThm1b}: Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} for the respective closure, stated without hypothesis (W); some archived outputs name them \texttt{Thm64}, \texttt{Thm64a}, \texttt{Thm64b}. \texttt{OneFinal}: a checkpoint finalizes at most one block per position. \texttt{SameVerdict}: Lemma~\ref{lem:verdict}. \texttt{UsableReports}: Lemma~\ref{lem:reports-live} once every validator has left the epoch. \texttt{WitnessAdmissible}: line~6 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:build} at the next closure can verify a correct $\tagF$ claim against an inherited lock on its rival. \texttt{EarlyCompatible} (\texttt{RaiPrefix}): every block finalized early is compatible with every formable epoch-1 certificate. In \texttt{RaiRecovery}, \texttt{NoConflict} counts a parent as final when its child is. \texttt{Probe*}: reachability probes, expected to be violated, which show that the invariants are not vacuous.

\para{\texttt{RaiClose}: three voting epochs, two closures.}
Votes are cast only after the voter's predecessor checkpoint is decided, so there is no early voting, late notarization, or overlap finality. Each closure runs $\Build$ with Rules~1--3, inherited records with origins, and matching-origin discharge; the manifest's discharge set is any subset of the dischargeable records that contains those a selected correct vote relied on. Switch \texttt{FIXB}: $G_i$ lists fresh votes for $\tagR$-tagged blocks (\texttt{TRUE}, Appendix~\ref{sec:reports}) or omits every key of $T_i$ (\texttt{FALSE}). Abstractions, each of which only adds behaviours: a validator's activity in different epochs is carried by separate identities, so a validator may still sign in epoch $e$ while epoch $e+1$ votes; a validator that observes its $\NC$ releases its final vote in the same step; reports are forgotten once their closure is decided.

\para{\texttt{RaiOverlap}: closing epoch, open epoch, one closure.}
Validators cross their local boundary one by one. After it, and before installing $S_1$, a validator may first-vote in epoch~2 under the cross-epoch guard and then also signs a late notarization in epoch~1. A block may finalize before $S_1$ is known under the overlap exception, or by rechecking against $S_1$ after installation. Switches: \texttt{NCSPLIT} (a validator records $\tagN$ and final-votes only on an $\NC$ of $q$ first votes; off, late notarizations count too), \texttt{DISCHARGE} (a recovery record is discharged by a conflicting epoch-1 exclusion witness; off, never), \texttt{LATEEV} (late notarizations are admissible manifest evidence for discharge; off, they are ``never construction input''). Finality decisions are recorded globally rather than per validator: \texttt{ovf} for the overlap exception at a validator that has not installed $S_1$, \texttt{pof} for rechecking at one that has. Closure~2 is not built; its acceptance condition for the case of interest is \texttt{WitnessAdmissible}.

\para{\texttt{RaiHandoff}: early voting, two closures, changing committee.}
Epoch~1 is voted and closed by $K_1$, epoch~2 by $K_2$; the Byzantine validator sits in both. A member of $K_2$ may first-vote in epoch~2 before installing $S_1$ (an early vote); if it is also in $K_1$ and supported nothing else in epoch~1, that vote comes with a late notarization in epoch~1. The second set of Appendix~\ref{sec:reports}, the NC split, matching-origin discharge, and late votes as evidence are built in. \texttt{RULE = FALSE} drops the early-vote rule: a fast certificate counts all first votes and a validator final-votes on its epoch-2 $\NC$ alone. \texttt{RULE = TRUE} is the early-vote rule: a fast certificate counts only first votes cast after the signer installed $S_1$, and an early final vote is released only by a validator that holds the epoch-1 exclusion witness. A block is final when its certificate can be formed from released signatures and it is valid against $S_1$, through the overlap bundle or by rechecking with discharge. The builder of $S_2$ holds a discharge witness only if a selected correct reporter relied on it; otherwise the adversary decides, which models a withheld signature. Further abstractions: correct reports carry no $\tagF$ tag for an observed certificate (covered by the other two models); a claim that conflicts with a surviving record is ignored rather than invalidating the selection, which only adds checkpoints; epoch~3 is not modelled, so safety after $S_2$ is checked as Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} for $S_2$.

\para{\texttt{RaiPrefix}: the prefix-wide guard on two positions.}
One account with a parent $P$ and a rival $P'$ at position~1 and two children $C_1$, $C_2$ of $P$ at position~2. Epoch~1 is closing and voted by $K_1$, epoch~2 is open and voted by $K_2$; one Byzantine validator sits in both committees, and the two committees are identical, disjoint, or share $k$ members. Members of $K_1$ cross their boundary one by one. $S_1$ is never installed within the model, so every epoch-2 first vote is early: early votes form no fast certificate, and an epoch-2 final vote is released only under the overlap guard. A member of both committees that first-votes $x$ in epoch~2 also signs a late epoch-1 notarization for $x$, unless it first-voted at that position in epoch~1, and the cross-epoch rule stops it from first-voting a different block where it supported one in epoch~1. The closure and $\Build$ are left out: early finality is checked against latent epoch-1 finality, that is, against any formable epoch-1 $\FC$ or $\FF$. Switches: \texttt{GUARD = "prefix"} requires $\XW_1$ and $\NC_2$ for every unresolved block on the path to the anchor, \texttt{GUARD = "target"} checks only the finalizing block; \texttt{FRESHCHILD = TRUE} requires a child to have its own $\XW_1$, \texttt{FALSE} lets $\NC_2$ for the child plus a parent with $\NC_1$ and $\NC_2$ suffice. Abstractions that only add behaviours: votes are signer sets, checks read formable certificates, finality is global, and members of $K_2\setminus K_1$ may vote in epoch~2 from the start.

\para{\texttt{RaiRecovery}: building on a recovery-locked block.}
One account with conflicting blocks $X$ and $Y$ at position~1 and a child of each at position~2, $C_X$ on $X$ and $C_Y$ on $Y$. Epoch~1 is voted by $K_1$ at position~1 and closed as in \texttt{RaiHandoff}; epoch~2 is voted by $K_2$ at both positions, early (before installing $S_1$, with late notarization, the cross-epoch rule, and the early-vote rule) or settled. Before installation a child is first-voted only on a parent complete in epoch~2 or final in epoch~1; after installation, on a parent final in $S_1$, or complete in epoch~2 and admissible against $S_1$, or, with \texttt{EXEMPT = TRUE}, retained by $S_1$ (any retained tip may be extended). Early final votes need the prefix-wide overlap bundle with a fresh child witness. A final child finalizes its parent, and \texttt{NoConflict} counts that. Abstractions: nobody votes at position~2 in epoch~1, finality is global, and closure~2 is not built, so the model checks Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} within epoch~2 and against $S_1$. Run with $N=4$ and $N=6$.

\para{\texttt{RaiCloseRestart}: restarts before the report freezes.}
\texttt{RaiClose} with a validator that restarts before freezing its report and keeps its final vote; it runs with $N=6$ and with $N=4$ ($f=1$, $p=0$). Switch \texttt{RESTART}: the notarization survives (\texttt{durable}); it is lost, and the report lists the first vote without an $\tagN$ tag (\texttt{lose}); the report waits until the notarization can be assembled again without the Byzantine validator (\texttt{hold}); or, in addition, until frozen reports of others rule out a normal certificate (\texttt{release}). \texttt{NoStuck}: no state in which every correct validator still to report can never freeze while the Byzantine validator stays silent. With \texttt{durable} safety and \texttt{NoStuck} hold for both sizes. With \texttt{lose}, safety fails once $p+1$ validators restart: both sides final after 13 steps at $N=4$ and 19 at $N=6$. \texttt{hold} and \texttt{release} are safe, but \texttt{hold} gets stuck with one restart (7 and 10 steps), and \texttt{release} once all $f+p+1$ correct final voters of a block restarted (8 and 12 steps). Rule~2 keeps a side here by the most reporter votes of any kind, which approximates the keep pass. The configurations and outputs are in \texttt{tla/}.

\para{\texttt{RaiResolve}: termination at a forked position.}
One account position with conflicting blocks $X$ and $Y$, three voting epochs, two closures, one committee of $N=4$ ($f=1$, $p=0$). Voting follows Appendix~\ref{sec:account}: one first vote per epoch, the cross-epoch rule, admission with matching-origin discharge, early final votes only with the closing-epoch witness, settled votes only on the fast path; a validator leaves epoch $e$ only with $S_{e-1}$ installed. The Byzantine validator signs first votes for either or both blocks in any epoch, at any time, or withholds them, reports adversarially within the usability checks, and the manifest discharges any dischargeable subset. Validators see the two blocks when the adversary delivers them. Each epoch is flagged good or not; in a good epoch no correct validator leaves before every correct validator has installed the predecessor, seen both blocks, and cast every first and final vote the rules allow. \texttt{TwoGood}: two consecutive good epochs make some block final. Switch \texttt{RULES}: the two termination rules and Rule~2's keep pass. \texttt{Spec23} starts after an arbitrary fork epoch (any epoch-1 votes and Byzantine signatures, anything seen, any checkpoint closure~1 can build, installed by nobody yet) and makes epochs 2 and 3 good; \texttt{Spec} explores every pattern of good epochs and is run by simulation. Abstractions: no late notarizations and no committee change; a formable final certificate counts as finality, whose validity the safety models check; the Byzantine validator signs no final votes, which could only complete a certificate; a correct report tags a block $\tagN$ only after its final vote, which can only hide notarizations.

\para{Rule~2 keeping a side (\texttt{MAXKEEP}).}
\texttt{RaiClose}, \texttt{RaiOverlap}, \texttt{RaiHandoff}, and \texttt{RaiRecovery} take a switch \texttt{MAXKEEP} under which Rule~2 also keeps a side where no block reaches $r$: the block with the most reporter first votes of any kind, ties by hash. Configurations with the suffix \texttt{\_maxkeep} set it on; the keep pass of Appendix~\ref{sec:build} itself is checked by \texttt{RaiRecover} and \texttt{RaiRecoverMany}.

\para{\texttt{RaiRecover} and \texttt{RaiRecoverMany}: four epochs, three closures, recovery.}
One account with a fork at position~$v$ and a child of each forked block at $v+1$; $N=6$, $f=p=1$; four voting epochs and three closures, one committee. Each validator crosses its own boundary and installs checkpoints on its own, so a first vote is settled or early. Built in: the cross-epoch rule, late notarization, the early-vote rule, the prefix-wide overlap guard, the second set of Appendix~\ref{sec:reports}, Rules~1--3 with origins, and matching-origin discharge; Rule~1 runs at every position before Rule~2. \texttt{RaiRecover} has two blocks $A$, $A'$ with free epoch-1 votes. \texttt{RaiRecoverMany} has $B\in\{2,3\}$ rivals $A_1,\dots,A_B$; validator $i\le B$ first-votes $A_i$ in epoch~1 and the others nothing; validator~6 may deliver any block to anyone at any time, and a correct validator may vote any admissible block whenever it may vote. The owner signs a child once a decided checkpoint keeps its parent or the parent is final (\texttt{OWNER = "policy"}), or signs every block at the start, with free epoch-1 votes (\texttt{"any"}). Switches: \texttt{SETTLED} (Rule~2 counts settled first votes only); \texttt{PICKRULE} (\texttt{none}: only a block alone reaching $r$; \texttt{hash}: else the most votes, ties by hash; \texttt{prev}: the keep pass); \texttt{R2ORDER} (\texttt{down}: the threshold pass from the highest position; \texttt{up}: from the lowest); \texttt{IMPLIED} (ancestor support: a first vote for a child also supports its parent); \texttt{ATTACH} (\texttt{strict}: \emph{checkpoints decide} at later positions too; \texttt{permissive}: a witnessed rival may be voted); \texttt{RENOTARIZE}; \texttt{NOEARLY} (\emph{no early votes at a contested position}, the half that reads the installed checkpoint); \texttt{BYZ} (\texttt{all}: every Byzantine signature available; \texttt{release}: Byzantine first votes released one at a time, final votes never); \texttt{READY} (the good epochs). Invariants: \texttt{NoConflict}, \texttt{ClosureThm1} for each closure, \texttt{OneKept}, \texttt{FlipBound} (Lemma~\ref{lem:flip}), \texttt{KeptOrFinal} (every checkpoint keeps or finalizes a block at $v$), \texttt{RecoveredMany} (a block at $v$ is final when no correct validator or closure can move), \texttt{YieldNext} (a checkpoint of the epoch of an early certificate keeps its block), and probes. \texttt{RaiRecoverScenarios} and \texttt{RaiRecoverManyScenarios} add action constraints that confine a run to the roles of a trace; a constraint only removes behaviours, so a violation found under one is a run of the full model.

Configuration names: \texttt{allup} is a Rule~2 that counts every reporter first vote and keeps, from the lowest position, the block with the most, ties by hash, with re-notarization; \texttt{routeA} is Rule~2 of Appendix~\ref{sec:build} (settled votes, keep pass) with re-notarization; \texttt{\_down} the downward threshold pass, otherwise upward; \texttt{\_any} the forking owner; \texttt{patch} ancestor support with settled votes and the keep pass, without re-notarization, and \texttt{none}, \texttt{hash}, \texttt{perm} that variant with no keep rule, with the hash order, or with permissive attachment; \texttt{allvotes} and \texttt{hashpick} Rule~2 with every first vote counted or with the hash order in place of the predecessor's block. Suffixes: \texttt{safe} (\texttt{NoConflict}, \texttt{ClosureThm1}, \texttt{OneKept}), \texttt{flip}, \texttt{kof}, \texttt{live}, \texttt{two23}, \texttt{gap24} (\texttt{RecoveredMany} with epochs 3--4, 2--3, 2--4 good), \texttt{early} (early finality under the overlap rule, with \texttt{YieldNext}). Abstractions that add behaviours: finality from formable certificates; complete delivery and voting any admissible block; a correct report may omit an $\tagN$ tag; the manifest discharges any dischargeable set containing the records a selected correct vote relied on; a child is published as soon as a checkpoint keeps its parent; \texttt{NOEARLY} leaves out the seen-two-blocks half. Abstractions that remove behaviours: correct reports carry no $\tagF$ tag for an observed certificate; with \texttt{BYZ = "release"} no Byzantine final vote is released, so the recovery runs miss attacks that need one; the owner policy, so safety against a forking owner is checked only by the \texttt{\_any} runs; scripted epoch-1 votes in \texttt{RaiRecoverMany} unless \texttt{OWNER = "any"}; a block held only by the keep pass is never promoted; one committee. No configuration is exhaustive: runs are random simulation (depth 150, two workers, stopped after 5 to 25 minutes) or breadth-first search stopped at a time cap.

\section{Scripted Regression Cases}
\label{app:cases}
The random Byzantine voter of the benchmark harness does not find the runs of Appendix~\ref{app:models}. The following scripted cases were derived from the violating traces; $N=6$, validator~6 is Byzantine, $B$ and $B'$ conflict.
\begin{description}
\item[Case 1 (second set).] Epoch~1: validators 1, 2, 3 vote $B'$; 4, 5 vote $B$; 6 reports $B$ only; 3's report is not selected. Epoch~2: 1 to 4 first-vote the retained $B$, nobody assembles the $\NC$. Release the epoch-1 $\NC$ for $B'$ after the second close. Expected: the second checkpoint carries $B$ with an origin-2 record; $B$ finalizes; $B'$ never does.
\item[Case 2 (discharge).] Before the boundary 1, 2, 6 vote $B'$. After it 3, 4, 5 vote $B$ in the open epoch and late-notarize it; 6 joins both. Install the checkpoint at 1 and 2 before they see $B$'s certificate. Expected: every validator finalizes $B$, and the next closure builds.
\item[Mixed NC.] One validator still in the closing epoch receives three first votes and one late notarization for its block. Expected: it does not record $\tagN$; its report is usable.
\item[Case 3 (early votes).] The run of Appendix~\ref{app:open}. Expected: $B$ has no fast certificate; 3, 4, 5 final-vote $B$ only if they hold the witness, and then the second checkpoint finalizes $B$. Without the early-vote rule this run ends with both blocks final.
\item[Case 3, new members.] Epoch~1: 1, 2, 3 vote $B$; 4, 5 vote $B'$; 6 reports $B'$ only and withholds its vote for $B$. Four members who were not in the old committee early-vote $B$. Same expectation as Case~3.
\item[Case 5 (re-notarization).] Epoch~$e$: 1, 2, 6 first-vote $B'$ at position~7; 3, 4, 5 cross their boundary without voting there. Before $S_e$ is known, 3, 4, 5 first-vote $B$ in epoch $e+1$ and late-notarize it in $e$; 6 signs both for $B$, so $\XW_e(B)$ and $\NC_{e+1}(B)$ hold. Closure $e$ selects 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and keeps $B'$ under a recovery record of origin $e$. 3, 4, 5 final-vote $B$, either after installing $S_e$, where $\XW_e(B)$ discharges the record, or before it under the early-vote rule once 6 shows them its signatures; with 6, $B$ is final. The owner publishes $C'$ on $B'$ at position~8, and 1 to 5 first-vote it after installing $S_e$: a fast certificate for $C'$ finalizes $B'$ too. \texttt{RaiRecovery} with \texttt{EXEMPT = TRUE} finds a shorter run without early votes: in epoch~$e$, 1 and 2 vote $X$ and 3 votes $Y$ ($N=4$), the checkpoint keeps $Y$ on the Byzantine report, and after installing it 1, 2, 3 first-vote both $X$ (whose witness discharges $Y$'s record) and $C_Y$. Expected under the re-notarization rule: $C'$ is refused at its first vote, because $B'$ has at most the epoch-$(e+1)$ votes of 1, 2, and 6 at position~7; in a run where nobody voted $B$, $B'$ is notarized again and $C'$ finalizes both.
\item[Case 6 (termination).] The stall of Appendix~\ref{app:stall}: after the checkpoint keeps $B'$, validators 3, 4, 5 re-vote $B$ before installing it and vote $B$ again after installing it on its old witness; validator~6 withholds. Expected: under the termination rules, 3, 4, 5 do not first-vote at the contested position before installing the checkpoint and vote only $B'$ after it, which is notarized and final in the next good epoch; with a three-to-three split in the reports the checkpoint keeps the side with the smaller hash.
\item[Case 7 (Rule 2 order).] The run of Appendix~\ref{app:bottomup}. Expected: closure~2 retains $C_2$ and $A_2$ by the threshold pass, and in epoch~3 nobody votes $A_1$. With a forking owner: closure~1 retains $C_1$ and $A_1$.
\item[Case 4 (prefix).] Epoch~1: $P$ is notarized; $C_1$ has first votes from 1, 2, 3. After the boundary, members of a committee sharing two members with the old one first-vote $C_2$ early and late-notarize it. Expected: $C_2$ does not finalize early without its own epoch-1 witness, and no validator finalizes $C_2$ while an epoch-1 certificate for $C_1$ can form.
\end{description}
\texttt{RaiClose} with \texttt{FIXB = FALSE} prints Case~1 step by step, \texttt{RaiOverlap} with \texttt{DISCHARGE = FALSE} prints Case~2, \texttt{RaiHandoff} with \texttt{RULE = FALSE} prints both Case~3 runs, and \texttt{RaiPrefix} with \texttt{FRESHCHILD = FALSE} or \texttt{GUARD = "target"} prints Case~4, \texttt{RaiRecovery} with \texttt{EXEMPT = TRUE} prints the short form of Case~5, \texttt{RaiResolve} with \texttt{RULES = FALSE} prints Case~6, and \texttt{RaiRecoverMany} with \texttt{R2ORDER = "up"} prints Case~7. In the prototype, Cases 1, 2, Mixed NC, 3, and 4 exist as unit tests of the construction and of the voting rules, Case~5 as unit tests of the attachment rule (a child of a recovery-only tip is first-voted only once the tip is notarized in the child's epoch, and at once under a notarization lock), and Case~6 as unit tests of the termination rules (a witnessed rival of a kept block gets an instance but no vote; a contested position waits for the checkpoint; Rule~2 keeps the side with the most reporter votes, ties by hash). Case~7 exists as unit tests of the construction (a child with $r$ settled reporter votes is kept before a rival of its parent with as many; three early first votes lose to one settled vote; the keep pass prefers the predecessor's block over a rival with more votes) and of the voting rule (a child and a grandchild of a witnessed rival of a kept block earn no vote); the benchmark harness's Byzantine validator votes at random and does not play these scripts, and a live harness with a scripted Byzantine validator that withholds named votes was not built. We consider this the main gap between the models and the measurements.

\section{Prototype Notes}
\label{app:proto}
\label{sec:eval}
\subsection{Departures from the protocol}
First, the manifest commits to signed votes and marks the closing-epoch witness of every overlap certificate its evidence proves: the closing-epoch exclusion witness, the current-epoch $\NC$, a matching-origin witness for every record of the last closed state $S_{e-2}$ the block bypasses, and a parent finalized in $S_{e-1}$ or itself certified. The marks are a function of the evidence, the selection, and the two closed states, so a validator recomputes them and refuses a manifest that marks any other set. Blocks and ancestry are placed by the report items, and the witnesses that discharge records of the predecessor checkpoint are re-derived by every validator from the manifest's votes rather than bound to the claims they serve. Second, a first vote names as its base the state hash of the checkpoint its signer had installed, $d_{e-1}$ for a settled vote and $d_{e-2}$ for an early one, rather than a digest of the per-block admission evidence; a settled vote counts toward a fast certificate only at a validator that installed the checkpoint it names, and a validator that has not yet installed it holds the vote and counts it at installation. Third, retained vote records keep a fixed cap of four epochs, since the reports of the next three epochs may still cite an epoch's late finality, and beyond it only the support that may still discharge a surviving recovery record of its origin at its position. The handoff evidence, an epoch's frozen reports and their reconstructions, is released once members of the successor committee carrying $N-f$ of its weight acknowledge the checkpoint; a member acknowledges once the decided state is written and synced, empty checkpoints included, and unreleased epochs are kept up to sixteen, a forced drop being logged (none occurred in any run). Fourth, durability rests on the host's \texttt{fsync}: first, final, late, and close-election votes, frozen reports, overlap evidence, the epoch origin, and decided checkpoints are synced before any signature that depends on them leaves the process, but on the macOS host \texttt{fsync} hands the data to the drive without forcing its cache. A stricter mode issues \texttt{F\_FULLFSYNC} after every signing write (about 4\,ms each). Power loss was not simulated; the restart runs kill processes and test crash consistency only. A vote's record holds what it says, written before it is signed, not its signature. A restarted validator therefore signs its recorded first and final votes again, in the epochs it left too, and serves them on request: the same identity, block, kind, and epoch, so the new signature conflicts with nothing, and every such vote is one the validator's report lists. The first votes behind a final vote go into the same transaction as its record, each stored once and only as many as the certificate needs, and come back on a restart with the notarization they prove. Fifth, a position whose committed first votes are split so that no block can be notarized any more is not carried into the new epoch at the boundary; if the checkpoint then retains neither side, the position is voted afresh, and if it retains one side, that side is continued. Sixth, the \emph{pause} transition used as a baseline in the evaluation is a configuration switch of the same code: under it no instance of epoch $e+1$ is started before $S_e$ is decided locally, so the overlap rule and late notarization never apply; everything else, including reports, the construction, and installation, is unchanged. Seventh, a position counts as contested when any instance at its root holds two candidate blocks or the latest decided checkpoint retains an unresolved block there; a block forked at a different parent is not seen as contested until a checkpoint retains it. Eighth, an evaluation switch turns fast finalization certificates off; the one-host committee-change runs of Appendix~\ref{app:rotation}, with four seats, use it. Ninth, the Byzantine validator of the evaluation is the same node built with an evaluation-only feature: it signs a second vote for the sibling of every fork block it votes for, sends a report with a corrupted residual root to half of the validators, and, leading the first round of every close, proposes two values derived from two orders of the usable reports, voting for the second at once.

\subsection{Implementation notes}
\label{app:impl}
The following mechanisms are not part of the protocol but are what the prototype needs to meet the services' assumptions under load; none changes a rule.

\para{Reports and evidence.} A report is laid over the shared state $S_{e-1}$ and holds only what the epoch added, upgraded, or removed; its root is kept incrementally, and the checks and the construction read the changes alone. A validator records, for every block it votes for, the parent that block names, for as long as any slot state that voted the block survives, so that its residual records place the block; a report naming a block without its parent is malformed and never selected. A node's own signed votes enter its vote processing on a lane of their own, past the queue that network votes and the vote cache's replays share, so every vote a report lists is in its signer's vote records and is served to any validator that asks for it as evidence. A proposal carries the signature of every report it selects, as the decided value does with its report headers: a validator authenticates a version of a Byzantine report it does not hold and reconstructs it next to the one it holds, keeping at most four versions per reporter. Manifest chunks are requested from the proposal's sender. An epoch whose handoff evidence was released is remembered, so a peer's repeated report does not recreate it, and a repeated copy of a proposal is not checked again once the value is held or the epoch decided.

\para{Close rounds.} Close-round votes bypass the per-channel inbound queue, and requests for a close round's statements take the inbound queue's priority lane and a lane of the request aggregator, so a validator that lags on account positions still completes its closes. Proposals are checked one at a time, the newest round's first, and a round waits from the moment its leader's proposal is queued.

\para{Lagging validators.} A validator one close behind its peers fetches the decided value and state by the certificate's value hash from any holder; it asks for the first rounds' statements of a close that has been open for 2\,s, ends its epoch once members holding more than $f$ of the weight are seen voting in later epochs, and, while its epoch has no election, asks once per epoch for that epoch's close. A validator that falls behind is flooded with live votes and can drop evidence replies, which delays its readiness for a close.

\para{Retained blocks.} A late instance's discard rolls a block, and what was built on it, back from the ledger. The validator keeps the bodies of these blocks, forces a retained one back in from them when a checkpoint keeps it, and answers other validators' requests for it, so a block a checkpoint keeps is always held by some ledger. Carried instances whose lock cannot continue are discarded at installation. An instance first-votes a valid candidate when the block it saw first is not valid, and every validator continues the kept block once its ledger holds it.

\para{Request pacing.} Across regions a reply can take longer than the repeat intervals that suit one host, so a validator does not repeat a request for votes before its reply could arrive; the harness sets that floor to the longest round trip it measures (311 to 316\,ms in the runs of the main text). An instance this validator has not first voted in, of an epoch whose predecessor checkpoint it has not installed, does not start asking, since the other validators withhold the same votes. Without this, thousands of such instances asking several times a second overfill the request aggregators, the replies crowd the nearer validators, and those drop close votes, so the close that would release the instances stalls. A validator asks a report's evidence of the reporter first and then backs off, backs off a solicitation that brought nothing and a repeated broadcast of an instance's winner, and a terminated instance does not ask for statements it holds.

\para{Epoch boundaries.} The start of the run reaches the validators at different times, so their epoch origins differ by up to about a second. An epoch that a validator ends early, because members holding more than $f$ of the weight are voting in later epochs, keeps its boundary: the next epoch runs to the following boundary, so no epoch is cut short.

\para{Durable state and membership.} The epoch origin $T_0$, the genesis committee, the decided checkpoints, and the close votes are durable records; decided states and evidence are kept in a second LMDB environment, and deletions run in bounded transactions, so synced signing does not wait behind them. Committee members are known by membership, not by current weight, and committee derivation waits for the frontier blocks it reads.

\para{Limitations.} Every measured block is a representative change: sends and receives occur only in the setup and in the weight moves of the committee change, so no run exercises the finalized-send dependencies of $\Build$ under load. No run has more than seven validators, and how the close scales with $N$ (it reconstructs $N-f$ reports and checks $q$ signatures per claim) was not measured. With an earlier build one validator could fall 18 to 158\,s behind after the burst of votes at an epoch switch, mostly the new member. Its voter read the ledger for every election without a first vote while holding the elections lock, so past some number of elections its scans ran back to back, vote processing got a few percent of its time, its elections stayed open, and its requests reached 0.3 to 12 million against about 50{,}000; closes that waited for its report slowed down. The build of the main text holds the lock per 64 elections and puts close votes first. On five seeds of the hardest cell the closes went from 42 to 158\,s to 8 to 15\,s, and across the 72 RAI and pause runs of the main text no RAI validator entered an epoch more than 3.8\,s after the median one. Every RAI and pause validator also caps itself at 5{,}000 asked instances per second, active elections first. Under overload a signed vote can be dropped by every receiver; until its signer supplies it again, its report is unusable at those validators, which delayed closes by up to 110\,s in runs with an equivocating validator. With a committee change in every epoch and eight node processes on one host, a rotated-in validator falls far behind its peers at 1{,}000 blocks/s and above, in RAI and pause alike, and those runs do not settle in time; the single committee change of the main text, with six processes, settles at 2{,}000 blocks/s.

\subsection{Counting settled first votes}
\label{app:settled}
Rule~2 counts settled first votes only. During the load a checkpoint is installed 2 to 6\,s into the 8\,s epoch that follows it, so most first votes for a fresh fork are early; where a checkpoint finds no settled vote and nothing to keep from its predecessor, it keeps nothing, and the position is voted again and waits one more epoch. On one host (six processes on a MacBook Air, $f=1$ and $p=0$ on six members, so that five of six certify, 48{,}000 blocks at 2{,}000 blocks/s) with 10\% forks the first checkpoint after the load leaves 396 to 529 such positions empty, and the last open block becomes final up to 17\,s after the load. The same prototype with every first vote counted, on the same three seeds, leaves none empty, finishes within 4.1 to 6.2\,s, and confirms 1{,}499 to 1{,}531 non-fork blocks/s against 1{,}405 to 1{,}545. Blocks without forks are unaffected. Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} explains why the protocol counts settled votes nonetheless.

\subsection{Committee change}
\label{app:rotation}
The equal-weight committee model selects the heaviest $N$ representatives from the finalized state, so committee identities change when weight moves. The harness runs extra representatives outside the committee and, at the start of a chosen epoch $k$, moves a member's whole balance to a holder delegating to a standby representative; the committee derived from epoch $k$, used from epoch $k+2$, has the standby in the member's place. The load generator sends the blocks that move weight to every validator, as it does the load. The main text's committee change (its Figure~8) runs across seven regions: six seats with $f=p=1$, a seventh validator in Toronto as the standby, and Montr\'eal's balance moved to it (\texttt{run\_sweep.py --set final-wan-2000}). The six members hold equal shares of the voting weight (the genesis representative, a member, keeps about a share) and the standby a sixty-fourth of a share, so for Nano, which counts weight, 67\% still takes five of the six members' votes after the move, as in its cells without the change. On one host, six processes on a MacBook Air (Apple M1, eight cores, 16\,GiB) over loopback, with six validators and four seats ($f=1$, $p=0$, no fast path) and two members replaced in epoch~0 at 2{,}000 blocks/s with RAI, pause, and Nano, three runs each (\texttt{run\_sweep.py --set rotation}), the prototype behaves the same; the runs with a Byzantine member use \texttt{--set rotation-byz}, which moves the weight of two correct members so that the Byzantine member keeps its seat. Every validator derives the same members for every epoch.

\subsection{Restart, durable signing, and handoff}
\label{app:restart}
These runs are on the host of Appendix~\ref{app:rotation}. Every node logs each signature it releases to a file before sending it. The harness kills validators with \texttt{SIGKILL}, either at a fixed time into the load, while elections are running, or when a given close starts, restarts them on their data after a chosen downtime, and afterwards checks every logged signature of every node: no identity may have signed two blocks in one first-vote or final-vote instance or two values in one close round. Fork5 with two restarts, one at a close and one mid-load, settled twice with every checkpoint decided identically on all six validators and no conflicting signature; the restarted validators rejoined through checkpoint catch-up and follow-ahead.

Durable notarization costs little. In five paired runs per cell on this host (no forks and 10\% forks at 2{,}000 blocks/s, 10\% forks with a Byzantine validator at 1{,}000, $f=p=1$, 45{,}000 blocks), it adds about 200 bytes per block per validator and raises the average signing write from 0.9--1.2 to 1.1--1.5\,ms; the median difference within a pair is under 3 blocks/s, and latency moves both ways within the spread between runs. Five restart runs with two validators killed and kept down 10\,s found no conflicting signature and restored every notarization.

Synced signing costs latency, not goodput. Fork5, six validators, 45{,}000 blocks at 2{,}000 blocks/s, two runs per setting: without sync 1{,}765 and 1{,}820 non-fork blocks/s at p50 173 and 139\,ms; with \texttt{fsync} 1{,}802 and 1{,}688 at 282 and 310\,ms; with \texttt{F\_FULLFSYNC} 1{,}763 and 1{,}777 at 409 and 304\,ms. The evaluation of the main text runs with \texttt{fsync}.

Handoff with a crashing departing member: a member replaced at the rotation of epoch~1 is killed when the close of epoch~2, the last it decides, starts, and stays down 30\,s; eight validators, 800 blocks/s, 20{,}000 blocks, 5\% forks. Without and with a Byzantine member every run settled with every checkpoint decided identically (epochs 0 to 4 and 0 to 5), the restarted member caught up the epochs it missed and installed them, every epoch's handoff evidence was released only after $N-f$ durable acknowledgements, no epoch was dropped before release, and no signature conflicted (340 and 335 non-fork blocks/s; the client counts the outage).

\subsection{Reproduction}
The prototype, the harness, the TLA\textsuperscript{+} models, and every run record (the per-run \texttt{result.json} with the client's per-second series, its latency histograms by publication second, and the nodes' epoch events, the node configurations, and the logs) are in the artifact. The six-region matrix of the main text is produced from a controller VM by
\begin{quote}\small\texttt{python3 tools/rai/run\_sweep.py <dir> --set final-wan-2000\\
\ \ --blocks 45000 --reps 3 --reps-from 1000\\
\ \ --arm rai=<bin>:--timeout=600:--hosts=<file>\\
\ \ --arm pause=<bin>:--timeout=600:--hosts=<file>:\\
\ \ \ \ --epoch-transition=pause\\
\ \ --arm develop=<develop bin>:--no-epochs:\\
\ \ \ \ --timeout=600:--hosts=<file>}
\end{quote}
where the hosts file lists one validator address per line in representative order, and \texttt{tools/rai/gcp} describes the fleet. A funded ledger per setup comes from \texttt{--snapshot-dir}, the VMs are Spot instances, and a sweep ends at the first RAI or pause run that does not settle. Each run in the record has its seed, node hash, and client hash. The 108 runs come from three records. The RAI and pause runs and Nano's committee-change runs are \texttt{gcp-matrix3-45k-2026-11-06}: RAI and pause on the node with the voter-lock fix and the request cap (hash \texttt{8a7c1763}), Nano on \texttt{478d1750}, with equal member weights. Nano's cells without the change are \texttt{gcp-matrix2-45k-2026-11-05}, on the same Nano node; the weight change affects only setups with a standby. Eleven runs of the first record in which the client stopped publishing for 4 to 5\,s with at most 1{,}000 blocks in flight, waiting on a write to one validator, were repeated with the same seed and a client that writes to each validator separately (\texttt{gcp-matrix4-45k-2026-11-06}, client \texttt{8777c487}): RAI repetition~1 with 10\% forks, repetitions~1 and~3 without forks and with a Byzantine validator, repetition~2 without forks and repetition~3 with 5\% forks, both with the change and a Byzantine validator; pause repetition~2 with 10\% forks and the change, repetition~1 with 5\% forks and a Byzantine validator; Nano repetition~1 without forks with the change, and repetitions~3, 3, and~2 without forks, with 5\%, and with 10\% forks, all with the change and a Byzantine validator. The figures use the repeats, which publish in every second of the load but one. The same seed does not give the same run: the repeats differ from the runs they replace in close times by up to a factor of two. The one-host committee change is produced with \texttt{--set rotation} and \texttt{--set rotation-byz}, \texttt{--rate 2000 --blocks 48000 --reps 3}, with \texttt{RAI\_FAST\_PATH=0} in the environment of the RAI and pause nodes. Every arm's binary directory holds \texttt{rsnano}, a \texttt{rsnano-byzantine} built with the \texttt{rai\_byzantine} feature, and the generator, all in release mode with the \texttt{rai\_protocol} feature; Nano is the develop branch with the fork-signature check (\texttt{develop-fork-memo}). The sets seed the generator identically for every system (\texttt{--seed}), run the Byzantine node (\texttt{--byzantine-node equivocate,conflicting,equivocate}) as the first leader of every close (\texttt{--byzantine-leads-close}), and keep the nodes running after settling until no forked position is open (\texttt{--fork-grace}). One point alone is \texttt{tools/rai/run\_gate\_b.py <dir> --rate 2000 --fork-percentage 10}. The flags \texttt{--restart PR:close:E}, \texttt{--restart PR:at:S}, \texttt{--restart-down-ms}, \texttt{--audit-votes}, \texttt{--signing-sync}, \texttt{--prs}, \texttt{--standby}, and \texttt{--rotation E:FROM>TO} drive the runs of Appendices~\ref{app:rotation} and~\ref{app:restart}. The figures of the main text are regenerated with \texttt{tools/wan\_figures.py sections/eval-data/wan <matrix4> <matrix3> <matrix2>}, which takes each run from the first record that has it. The models are checked with \texttt{tla/run.sh <module> <config>} and the scripts in \texttt{tla/}.
