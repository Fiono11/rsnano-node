\section{Full Proofs}
\label{app:proofs}
\label{sec:correctness}
The arguments concern the core protocol, including the prefix-wide overlap guard, late notarization, the early-vote rule, origin-stamped records, and Rule~3 promotion, and are relative to the services of Appendix~\ref{sec:services}: safety uses C1 and A1--A3, termination additionally C2 and A4. Cryptographic guarantees hold except with negligible probability.

\begin{lemma}[Account exclusion]
\label{lem:exclusion}
In one account instance at most one block has an exclusion witness; in particular at most one has an $\NC$. If an $\FC$ or $\FF$ for $B$ can be formed in that instance, no conflicting block has an exclusion witness. If an $\FF$ for $B$ can be formed, every conflicting block has at most $f+p<r$ supporters.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Two sets of $q$ supporters intersect in at least $2q-N=f+1$ identities, one of them correct, and a correct validator supports one block per instance (Definition~\ref{def:support}), by a first vote or by a late notarization. An $\FC$ for $B$ has $q$ final voters; a correct final voter first-voted $B$ in the same instance, so the same intersection applies against any $\XW$ of a rival. An $\FF$ has $N-p\ge q$ first voters. For the last claim let $f'\le f$ be the actual number of Byzantine members. An $\FF$ has at least $N-p-f'$ correct first voters, so at most $p$ correct members and $f'$ Byzantine members can support a rival: at most $p+f'\le p+f$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Notarized ancestry]
\label{lem:ancestry}
If an epoch-$e$ $\NC$, $\FC$, or $\FF$ for a block $D$ can be formed, then every ancestor $X$ of $D$ is final, or is held under a notarization lock of a decided checkpoint (as its target or as an ancestor of it), or has an epoch-$e$ $\NC$ that can be formed.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each of the three certificates contains at least $q\ge f+1$ first votes for $D$ (a correct final voter first-voted the same block in the instance), so some correct validator first-voted $D$. By the attachment rule of Appendix~\ref{sec:eligibility} it did so on a parent $P$ that it held final, held under a notarization lock, or held complete in epoch $e$, that is, with an $\NC_e(P)$ in hand. Ancestors of a final block are final, and ancestors of a notarization-lock target are retained under that lock, which Rule~1 creates with the target's ancestry and the inherited-state rule never drops while the target is unresolved. In the third case $\NC_e(P)$ can be formed and the argument repeats for $P$; the chain is finite.
\end{proof}
The lemma rests on the re-notarization rule of Appendix~\ref{sec:eligibility}: a tip held by recovery records alone is not a final parent. With the earlier rule, under which such a tip could always be extended, it fails, and so did Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} (Case~5 of Appendix~\ref{app:cases}).

\para{Proof structure.}
Theorems~\ref{thm:closure} and~\ref{thm:safety} are proved by one induction over closures, from genesis. The step for closure $e$ uses Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} for finality through epoch $e$ (in Rule~1's exclusion of conflicts with $F^\star$ and in the earlier-epochs case) and Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} for closure $e-1$ (the closure invariant of $S_{e-1}$); Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} up to epoch $e+1$ uses Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} for closures up to $e$. Each proof below cites the other only within this order.

\begin{lemma}[Late notarization adds no old finality]
\label{lem:late}
Every epoch-$e$ $\NC$, $\FC$, or $\FF$ consists of first and final votes released by correct signers before they left epoch $e$, together with Byzantine votes. A correct validator releases a final vote, or records an $\tagN$ entry, only for a block for which it holds an $\NC$ of $q$ first votes.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By Definition~\ref{def:support} and the certificate definitions of Appendix~\ref{sec:voting} none of the three certificates counts a late notarization, and a correct validator releases no epoch-$e$ first or final vote after leaving $e$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:lifecycle}). Completeness is defined on $\NC_e$, and notarization and final voting require completeness.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Frozen-report exposure]
\label{lem:exposure}
Let $i$ be a correct reporter for closure $e$. If $i$ released an epoch-$e$ final vote for $B$, then $T_i$ tags $B$ with $\tagN$ or $\tagF$. If $i$ released an epoch-$e$ first vote for $B$, then $T_i$ tags $B$ with $\tagN$ or $\tagF$, or $h(B)\in G_i$. This holds whatever other tag $B$ inherits from $S_{e-1}$, and it persists after the report is frozen.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A final vote follows durable notarization, which is an $\tagN$ entry unless finality has replaced it by $\tagF$. For a first vote, $h(B)\in V_i^{(e)}$, and $G_i$ removes from $V_i^{(e)}$ exactly the hashes tagged $\tagN$ or $\tagF$. Stopping precedes freezing; roots and tags are then immutable, and C1 ensures that what a correct validator reconstructs is what was frozen.
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}
With the earlier definition $G_i=V_i^{(e)}\setminus\keys(T_i)$ the second statement fails when $S_{e-1}$ carries $B$ under a recovery record: $T_i$ tags $B$ with $\tagR$ and the fresh vote appears nowhere. \texttt{RaiClose} with \texttt{FIXB = FALSE} reaches two conflicting fast certificates this way (Appendix~\ref{app:models}).
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Signers hold the witness]
\label{lem:holds}
Every correct signer of an eligible epoch-$e$ $\FC$ or $\FF$ for $B$ holds, as retained evidence, an exclusion witness discharging each recovery record of $S_{e-1}$ on a block that conflicts with $B$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An $\FF_e(B)$ consists of settled first votes. A correct signer of one had installed $S_{e-1}$ when signing; the voting rule of Appendix~\ref{sec:install} refuses a rival of a retained block unless every record on it is discharged, and the signer's admission witness fixes the witnesses it used, so it holds them. A correct final voter either released its final vote after installing $S_{e-1}$, with the same check, or before. In the second case the early-vote rule required it to hold $\XW_{e-1}(B)$ for $B$ and every unresolved block of the selected prefix. That witness discharges every record on a rival that closure $e-1$ created, since those have origin $e-1$ (Rule~2 stamps the closing epoch). Records that $S_{e-1}$ inherits from $S_{e-2}$ were known to the voter, because $S_{e-2}$ is installed before the overlap of closure $e-1$ begins (at most one closing epoch per validator), and the overlap guard required the matching-origin witness for each of them.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Closure invariant]
\label{def:invariant}
A closed state $S_e$ satisfies the closure invariant if, for every epoch $e'\le e$ and every block $B$ with an eligible epoch-$e'$ account final certificate that can be formed from released signatures, $S_e$ holds $B$ (i)~as final, (ii)~under a notarization lock, or (iii)~under a recovery record of origin $e'$, directly or as an ancestor of a block so held.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Closure preserves latent and late finality]
\label{thm:closure}
Let $S_{e-1}$ be a valid closed predecessor that satisfies the closure invariant, and let $v$ be any candidate with $\Elig_e(v)$ and state $S_e$. Let $B$ have an eligible epoch-$e$ account final certificate that can be formed, possibly completed after reports froze or after the checkpoint was decided, and assume
\begin{itemize}
\item[(W)] $B$ conflicts with no lock record carried by $S_{e-1}$, or every correct signer of the certificate holds, as retained evidence, an exclusion witness that discharges each such record.
\end{itemize}
Under the early-vote rule, (W) holds for every eligible certificate (Lemma~\ref{lem:holds}); without that rule it can fail (Appendix~\ref{app:open}). Then $S_e$ holds $B$ as final, under a notarization lock, or under a recovery record of origin $e$, directly or as an ancestor of a block so held; no admissible evidence discharges that hold; and $S_e$ satisfies the closure invariant for all certificates covered by (W). The same holds for every ancestor of $B$ that the certificate finalizes with it.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $Q$ be the selected reporters, $|Q|=N-f$, and let $A$ be the signer set of the certificate.

\emph{Eligibility of $B$ in the construction.} If $B$ conflicts with no carried record there is nothing to show. Otherwise, by (W), each correct signer holds an exclusion witness for every carried record that $B$ bypasses. That witness is retained evidence; reconstructing the signer's report material delivers it, the manifest contains it, and the record is discharged at line~6 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:build}. So $B$ is eligible for Rules~1 and~2 whenever a correct signer is selected, which the counts below guarantee.

\emph{Normal certificate.} $A$ is a set of $q$ final voters, so $|(Q\cap A)\setminus\Byz|\ge(N-f)+q-N-f=q-2f=p+1\ge1$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:exposure} each such reporter froze an $\tagN$ or $\tagF$ entry for $B$. A verified $\tagF$ entry puts $B$ in $F^\star$. Otherwise $B$ is a represented $\NC$ target; it is the unique one at its position by Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}, it does not conflict with $F^\star$ by the induction hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:safety}, and Rule~1 retains it under a notarization lock.

\emph{Fast certificate.} $A$ is a set of $N-p$ settled first voters, so $|(Q\cap A)\setminus\Byz|\ge(N-f)+(N-p)-N-f=N-p-2f=r$. Take these reporters. If any selected report has a verified $\tagF$ entry for $B$ we are in the first case. Else if any has a verified $\tagN$ entry for $B$, Rule~1 applies as above. Else, by Lemma~\ref{lem:exposure}, each of the $r$ reporters has $h(B)\in G_i$ with a verified first vote, whether or not $T_i$ tags $B$ with $\tagR$; hence $|U_Q(B)|\ge r$. No rival has a represented $\NC$, and every rival $B'$ has $|U_Q(B')|\le f+p<r$, both by Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}; so $B$ is the unique block at its position with $r$ reporter votes, and Rule~2 adds a recovery record of origin $e$ for it.

\emph{No discharge.} A notarization lock is never bypassed. A record of origin $e$ on $B$ is discharged only by $\XW_e(B')$ for a conflicting $B'$, which Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion} excludes because an epoch-$e$ final certificate for $B$ can be formed. Evidence of other epochs does not apply to it, and discharging records of other origins on $B$ leaves it in place.

\emph{Finality through a descendant.} Let $X$ be an ancestor of $B$ that is not final before the certificate. If $S_{e-1}$ holds $X$ final or under a notarization lock, the inherited-state rule keeps it. Otherwise $S_e$ holds $X$ as an ancestor of $B$, by the cases above, and the question is whether that hold can be discharged. A hold through $B$ ends only when every record on $B$ is discharged or a final block excludes $B$. A record of origin $e$ on $B$ is discharged only by $\XW_e(Y)$ for the block $Y$ of a conflicting branch at the first position where that branch leaves $B$'s; that position holds $B$ or an ancestor $X$ of $B$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestry}, $X$ is final, under a notarization lock, or has an $\NC_e(X)$ that can be formed; the first two exclude the conflicting branch outright, and in the third Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion} excludes $\XW_e(Y)$. A notarization lock on $B$ or finality of $B$ is never undone. This is the step the earlier retained-tip exemption broke: without Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestry}, an $\XW_e(Y)$ can exist at an ancestor's position, and in Case~5 of Appendix~\ref{app:cases} $Y$ even becomes final.

\emph{Earlier epochs.} For $e'<e$ the predecessor holds $B$ as final, under a notarization lock, or under a record of origin $e'$. Finality is inherited. A notarization lock and a record of origin $e'$ are carried forward by the inherited-state rule, since the only ways to drop them are a conflicting final block, excluded by Theorem~\ref{thm:safety}, and an $\XW_{e'}$ of a rival, excluded by Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}.

The argument uses only signatures in $A$ and their intersection with $Q$, so it does not matter when the certificate is assembled, and unselected or Byzantine signatures cannot remove the correct intersections.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Checkpoint-promotion safety]
\label{lem:promotion}
A Rule~3 promoted block cannot conflict with a block that has a closing-epoch exclusion witness, $\FC$, or $\FF$. Hence a decision that finalizes the promoted prefix is compatible with old-domain finality and with core overlap finality.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Every promoted block has a verified closing-epoch $\NC$ of first votes, which is an exclusion witness; Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion} excludes an exclusion witness, and so an $\FC$ or $\FF$, for a conflicting block. Core overlap finality for a conflicting successor block requires a closing-epoch exclusion witness at the first divergent position, which is the same contradiction. Promotion stops at retained forks and never promotes recovery-only evidence.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Acyclic final dependencies]
\label{lem:dag}
For any finite set of valid final blocks closed under account ancestors and required finalized sends, parent and send-to-receive edges form a directed acyclic graph.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Order blocks by the earliest event at which valid final evidence justifies them. Along a parent edge this event cannot become earlier at the child; along a send-to-receive edge it is strictly later at the receive, because admission required the send's finality proof and Rule~3 cannot supply it for the checkpoint under construction. A cycle cannot consist only of parent edges, since slots increase strictly, so it contains a send-to-receive edge and a strict increase around a cycle, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[The overlap verdict does not depend on installation order]
\label{lem:verdict}
Let closure $e$ be in progress, let $B$ and $B'$ conflict at one position, and let $\OC$ be an overlap certificate for $B$: an exclusion witness $\XW_e(B)$, an $\NC_{e+1}(B)$, an epoch-$(e+1)$ $\FC$ or $\FF$ for $B$ or a descendant selecting it, and an exclusion witness of the matching origin for every recovery record on $B'$ that $S_{e-1}$ carries, with no notarization lock on $B'$ in $S_{e-1}$. Then for every candidate with $\Elig_e$ and state $S_e$: (a)~$S_e$ does not finalize $B'$; (b)~$S_e$ carries no notarization lock on $B'$; (c)~every recovery record that $S_e$ carries on $B'$ is discharged by a witness contained in $\OC$. Consequently a validator that holds $\OC$ accepts $B$ as final under the overlap exception before installing $S_e$ if and only if it accepts $B$ by ordinary rechecking after installing $S_e$, and a later manifest that contains $\OC$ verifies an $\tagF$ claim for $B$ against $S_e$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
(a) $S_e$ finalizes a block through inherited finality, an explicit closing-epoch account certificate, or Rule~3. Inherited finality of $B'$ contradicts the check of $\OC$ against $S_{e-1}$, which is installed before the overlap of closure $e$ begins. An epoch-$e$ $\FC$ or $\FF$ for $B'$, and the $\NC_e(B')$ that Rule~3 requires, are excluded by $\XW_e(B)$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}. (b) A new notarization lock needs a represented $\NC_e(B')$, excluded the same way; an inherited one is excluded by hypothesis. (c) A record on $B'$ in $S_e$ is either created by Rule~2 at this closure, with origin $e$, and then $\XW_e(B)\subseteq\OC$ discharges it; or inherited from $S_{e-1}$, and then $\OC$ contains its discharge witness by hypothesis.

For the consequence: before installation the validator checks exactly the contents of $\OC$ against $S_{e-1}$. After installation, ordinary rechecking tests $B$ against the frontier, the finalized history, and the carried records of $S_e$; by (a)--(c) these tests pass on the evidence in $\OC$ and on no other. The same evaluation is what line~6 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:build} performs at closure $e+1$. The lemma is stated for one divergent position; the prefix-wide guard applies it at the first divergent unresolved position of the finalized prefix, as the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} does, and the \texttt{RaiPrefix} model of Appendix~\ref{app:models} checks that guard on two positions.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Deterministic construction]
\label{lem:construction}
Assume the predecessor satisfies the closure invariant. For fixed usable selected reports and a valid immutable manifest, $\Build$ returns one canonical candidate state at every correct validator. It preserves inherited finality, carries all mandatory surviving records, and applies only explicit account finality plus Rule~3 promotion. All-correct usable inputs yield a finite valid construction.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By C1 every correct validator reconstructs the same $(T_i,G_i)$ for each selected header, and the manifest fixes witnesses and proof choices; hence explicit finality, discharges, and $R_Q$ are the same everywhere, with lock records compared as sets of (block, strength, origin). Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}, predecessor validity, attachment, and Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} make the retained finality and records compatible. Rule~3's tests fix $P_Q$, safe by Lemma~\ref{lem:promotion}; Lemma~\ref{lem:dag} and the canonical tie-break fix replay. An $\tagN$ entry of a correct reporter is backed by an $\NC$ of first votes (Lemma~\ref{lem:late}), which the manifest can carry, so the entry verifies. An $\tagF$ entry of a correct reporter for a block finalized under the overlap exception is backed by its overlap certificate, which is retained evidence; by Lemma~\ref{lem:verdict} line~6 accepts the claim and discharges every conflicting record of $S_{e-1}$, so no all-correct selection is rejected on that account. Malformed Byzantine inputs are excluded by usability; all-correct usable inputs yield a finite state.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Transfer safety across epochs]
\label{thm:safety}
Assuming C1 and A1--A3, finalized histories at correct validators are compatible and irreversible across all core-protocol epochs; finalized balances are nonnegative, and each send is received at most once.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct from genesis, jointly with Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}. \emph{Within an epoch}, let two blocks be final through epoch-$e$ certificates for $D$ and $D'$ whose branches conflict, and let $X\ne X'$ be their blocks at the first position where the branches differ. Conflicting finality need not be visible at a single position: either certificate may finalize $X$ or $X'$ only as an ancestor. By Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestry} each of $X$, $X'$ is final through older evidence, held under a notarization lock, or has an $\NC_e$ that can be formed. Two $\NC_e$ at one position contradict Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}. A notarization lock on $X$ is never bypassed: a correct validator that installed it refuses $X'$ and its descendants, and one that had not accepts $D'$ only under an overlap certificate, whose $\XW_{e-1}(X')$ contradicts the closing-epoch $\NC$ behind a new lock (Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}) and whose check against the latest closed state refuses an inherited one. Finality through older evidence is the boundary case. At a boundary, A2 ensures that the decided $v_e$ is eligible, so by Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} it preserves every formable old final branch and by Lemma~\ref{lem:promotion} its promotion is compatible; A1 and A3 ensure that every correct validator installs the state of the same $v_e$, which by Lemma~\ref{lem:construction} is the same state. For overlap, the first divergent unresolved position on an early successor-final prefix has a closing-epoch exclusion witness for the successor block by the prefix-wide guard, whose late notarizations add no old finality that Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} would have to preserve (Lemma~\ref{lem:late}); a conflicting closing-epoch certificate or a conflicting Rule~3 promotion would require a conflicting $\NC$, contradicting Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}; and by Lemma~\ref{lem:verdict} correct validators that hold the same overlap certificate reach the same verdict whether they verify it before or after installing $S_e$, so they agree on which overlap blocks are final and not only on the compatibility of what they have finalized. Matching-origin discharge and inherited records remain enforced after installation, and the base/overlay union with applied-operation identifiers prevents rollback and duplicate application. Replay checks balances and finalized-send dependencies.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Correct reports become usable]
\label{lem:reports-live}
Under the data service, finite relevant admitted work, fair processing, and C2, every correct report eventually becomes usable at every correct validator.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A correct reporter stops old first and final voting, so its old vote set and roots are finite, and its report is well formed: every $\tagN$ entry rests on an $\NC$ of first votes (Lemma~\ref{lem:late}), every $\tagF$ entry on inherited finality, an account certificate, or a retained overlap certificate, and every $G_i$ member on its own first vote and on no hash tagged $\tagN$ or $\tagF$. By C2 every correct validator eventually reconstructs $(T_i,G_i)$. The data service delivers the blocks, ancestry, signed votes, late notarizations, and overlap certificates behind its claims, and fair processing completes the finite validation. A withholding Byzantine reporter can make only its own report unusable; $N-f$ correct reports remain.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Handoff termination]
\label{thm:handoff-live}
Assume the data service, fair processing, C2, A4, and recurring prepared windows with $N-f$ responsive correct members of $K_e$ long enough for reconstruction, candidate preparation, and the consensus service's rounds. Every enabled handoff is eventually decided and, with eventual retained-data delivery, installed by correct successors.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:reports-live}, $N-f$ correct reports become usable at every correct member, which by Lemma~\ref{lem:construction} can then construct an eligible candidate; the canonical selection order makes correct members that hold the same usable reports construct the same one. A4 then decides some eligible value in a sufficient window; A3 lets successors verify it, and retained authorizing data let them recompute and install the state, which enables the next deferred boundary.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Account continuation and forked positions]
\label{thm:account-live}
Assume handoff termination, available final-send dependencies, and recurring common-open ready intervals.
(a)~An owner following the retry-or-fresh-child policy of Appendix~\ref{sec:account} eventually finalizes each target of a finite valid uncontested continuation, provided each required new position has such an interval, and the finalized targets eventually enter a checkpoint.
(b)~At a forked position at most one block ever becomes final, the position is held identically by every correct validator that installed the same checkpoints, and it delays no other account except through receives of its own sends.
(c)~If a decided checkpoint holds a block $A$ at the position under a lock that no rival can release, a fresh child of $A$ is eventually final, and $A$ with it. A notarization lock is never released. A recovery record of origin $o$ on $A$ can be released only if some validator supported both $A$ and a rival in epoch $o$; this needs a Byzantine validator, since a correct one supports one block per instance.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(a)~In a ready interval, $N-f\ge q$ correct first votes create an $\NC$ and the same validators can final-vote; after they install the predecessor their first votes are settled, so an available $N-p$ fast quorum finalizes in two stages. An attempt interrupted by a boundary is preserved by Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}; an omitted block can be retried, and a uniquely retained notarized target may finalize at the handoff.

(b)~The first claim is Theorem~\ref{thm:safety}, the second follows from A1 and Lemma~\ref{lem:construction}, and no rule of another account waits for an unresolved block except a receive of its send.

(c)~Rule~2 created the record from $r$ selected reporters' epoch-$o$ first votes for $A$, and $\XW_o(Y)$ for a rival $Y$ needs $q$ epoch-$o$ supporters; $r+q=N+1$, so some validator supported both. If no rival can release the lock, every correct validator that installed the checkpoint refuses every rival of $A$ and every descendant of one, for good. A correct validator ends epoch $e+1$ only after closure $e$ is complete (Appendix~\ref{sec:lifecycle}), so from the second epoch after the checkpoint on, a correct member first-votes at the position only with the checkpoint installed, and only $A$ is admissible there; $A$ has an instance in every such epoch (Appendix~\ref{sec:install}), the cross-epoch rule delays a member that supported a rival in the epoch before only until the next checkpoint is installed, and a ready interval gives $A$ an $\NC$ of $q$ correct first votes. With it $A$ is a complete parent, so the owner's fresh child is an uncontested target and (a) applies; the same votes usually finalize $A$ itself first.

The guarantee does not extend further. If a rival $Y$ holds a releasing witness, $Y$ stays admissible in every later epoch, correct validators may first-vote either block, and a Byzantine validator that rebroadcasts both and withholds the one vote either side needs can keep the position unresolved; the owner's child of $A$ waits for $A$'s re-notarization, which Lemma~\ref{lem:ancestry} makes necessary for safety. The same holds where the checkpoint kept nothing, since the owner's single block then competes with the old ones. Appendix~\ref{app:stall} sketches rules that would remove this stall. Repeating this over the finite continuation and using eventual evidence delivery gives finalization and checkpoint inclusion. Normal finality takes three delivery stages after broadcast and an $N-p$ settled first-vote quorum two; a sufficient account interval budget is $D/(1+\rho)>\kappa_A+R_A+3\delta$, where $\kappa_A$ bounds opening skew and $R_A$ readiness after the last opening, including predecessor closure.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Early votes against a carried lock}
\label{app:open}
Without the early-vote rule, hypothesis (W) of Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} is not cosmetic. The following run violates the theorem in the protocol as it stood before the repairs, and equally after Fix~B alone. \texttt{RaiHandoff} finds it with \texttt{RULE = FALSE}. Take $N=6$ with validator~6 Byzantine, and conflicting blocks $B$, $B'$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item Epoch $e$: validators 1, 2, 6 first-vote $B'$. Validators 3, 4, 5 cross their boundary without voting at this position.
\item Before $S_e$ is known, 3, 4, 5 first-vote $B$ in epoch $e+1$ and late-notarize it in $e$. Validator~6 signs both votes for $B$ and shows its late notarization to validator~1 only.
\item Closure $e$ selects reports 1, 2, 3, 4, 6. Rule~2 gives $B'$ a recovery record of origin $e$.
\item Validators 3, 4, 5 install $S_e$ without holding $\XW_e(B)$; their early work on $B$ is invalid in their view. Validator~1 installs $S_e$, holds $\XW_e(B)=\{3,4,5,6\}$, discharges the record and first-votes $B$ in $e+1$. Now $\{1,3,4,5,6\}$ is an $\FF_{e+1}(B)$ that can be formed.
\item Closure $e+1$ selects reports 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. The only correct holder of the witness is not selected, the manifest has no $\XW_e(B)$, the votes for $B$ are an unexplained speculative path, and $S_{e+1}$ retains $B'$ only.
\item In $e+2$ validators 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 first-vote the retained $B'$: $\FF_{e+2}(B')$. Validator~6 then publishes its signatures; $B$ has an $\FF_{e+1}$ whose eligibility against $S_e$ follows from $\XW_e(B)$.
\end{enumerate}
With a committee change the same failure needs no late notarization: four new members early-vote $B$, the old committee's first votes for $B$ plus one withheld Byzantine vote are the exclusion witness, and the second closure sees four unexplained votes. The cause is that the eligibility of an early first vote can depend on a witness its signer never held. The early-vote rule removes the dependency: in step~4 the set $\{1,3,4,5,6\}$ is no longer a fast certificate, because three of its votes are early, and validators 3, 4, 5 final-vote $B$ only once they hold $\XW_e(B)$, in which case their reports deliver it to the next closure.

Two alternatives were considered and set aside. Retaining both blocks at the second closure keeps the one-round path but can stall a contested position permanently after a committee change. Counting only early votes of members of the closing committee is unsafe: the committee-change run above defeats it.

\subsection{Owner recovery against a releasable lock}
\label{app:stall}
Theorem~\ref{thm:account-live}(c) needs a lock that no rival can release. When a rival holds a releasing witness, the re-notarization rule leaves a stall. In the first run of Case~5, take the rule as given: each epoch validators 3, 4, 5 first-vote $B$ again (the cross-epoch rule allows re-voting the same block, and the old witness keeps $B$ admissible), and validator~6 withholds the one vote $B$ needs. $B'$ is never notarized again, so the owner's child $C'$ is never admitted. Without the rule this run is a safety violation, so the earlier promise of recovery by extending the kept block held only because that route was unsafe. In the second run $B$ may already be final when the owner publishes $C'$, and validators 1 and 2 cannot tell the two runs apart, so no safe rule lets $C'$ finalize in epoch $e+1$.

Three further rules would, we expect, remove the stall. (i)~\emph{Witnesses validate, checkpoints decide}: after installing a checkpoint that keeps $K$, a validator casts no new first, late, or final vote for a rival of $K$; witnesses still let it accept a rival's complete overlap certificate (Lemma~\ref{lem:verdict}) and still discharge records inside a manifest. (ii)~\emph{No early re-votes at a known fork}: a validator that has seen two blocks at a position waits for the checkpoint before voting there in the new epoch; this is probably also what the unresolved forks of defect~(17) need. (iii)~\emph{Always pick a side}: where no notarization is represented, Rule~2 keeps the eligible block with the most reporter first votes, ties broken by hash, instead of keeping nothing; a block that could be fast-final has at least $r$ reporter votes and is then the unique maximum (Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}), and a normal certificate implies a represented notarization, which Rule~1 handles first. Rules (i) and (ii) only remove votes and cannot create a conflicting certificate; rule (iii) changes the recipe and needs its argument in Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}. With all three, we expect a fork to resolve within about two good epochs, with or without the owner. This is a sketch: none of the three rules is in the protocol, the models, or the prototype.

\section{The TLA\textsuperscript{+} Models}
\label{app:models}
\label{sec:mc}
The five modules, their TLC configurations, a run script, and the TLC outputs (including the violating traces) are in the \texttt{tla/} directory of the artifact. \texttt{RaiClose}, \texttt{RaiOverlap}, and \texttt{RaiHandoff} take one account position with two conflicting blocks $X$ and $Y$, $N=6$, $f=p=1$, five correct validators and one Byzantine validator whose signatures are available for every block, vote kind, and epoch and whose report is chosen adversarially subject to the usability checks of Appendix~\ref{sec:usable}. \texttt{RaiPrefix} and \texttt{RaiRecovery} take two positions and are run with $N=4$ ($f=1$, $p=0$) and $N=6$. The consensus service is the nondeterministic choice of one eligible candidate from any $N-f$ usable reports. TLC explores all interleavings; a \texttt{VIEW} identifies validators up to permutation. The runs of Table~\ref{tab:tlc} used TLC build 2026-10-06 (two workers) and TLC~2.20 (eight workers, \texttt{RaiPrefix}).

\para{Invariant names.}
\texttt{NoConflict} and \texttt{Agreement}: no two conflicting blocks are final (Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} at the position). \texttt{ClosureThm1}, \texttt{ClosureThm1a}, \texttt{ClosureThm1b}: Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} for the respective closure, stated without hypothesis (W); the archived outputs of the runs made before this revision name them \texttt{Thm64}, \texttt{Thm64a}, \texttt{Thm64b}. \texttt{OneFinal}: a checkpoint finalizes at most one block per position. \texttt{SameVerdict}: Lemma~\ref{lem:verdict}. \texttt{UsableReports}: Lemma~\ref{lem:reports-live} once every validator has left the epoch. \texttt{WitnessAdmissible}: line~6 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:build} at the next closure can verify a correct $\tagF$ claim against an inherited lock on its rival. \texttt{EarlyCompatible} (\texttt{RaiPrefix}): every block finalized early is compatible with every formable epoch-1 certificate. In \texttt{RaiRecovery}, \texttt{NoConflict} counts a parent as final when its child is. \texttt{Probe*}: reachability probes, expected to be violated, which show that the invariants are not vacuous.

\para{\texttt{RaiClose}: three voting epochs, two closures.}
Votes are cast only after the voter's predecessor checkpoint is decided, so there is no early voting, late notarization, or overlap finality. Each closure runs $\Build$ with Rules~1--3, inherited records with origins, and matching-origin discharge; the manifest's discharge set is any subset of the dischargeable records that contains those a selected correct vote relied on. Switch \texttt{FIXB} selects the definition of $G_i$. Abstractions, each of which only adds behaviours: a validator's activity in different epochs is carried by separate identities, so a validator may still sign in epoch $e$ while epoch $e+1$ votes; a validator that observes its $\NC$ releases its final vote in the same step; reports are forgotten once their closure is decided.

\para{\texttt{RaiOverlap}: closing epoch, open epoch, one closure.}
Validators cross their local boundary one by one. After it, and before installing $S_1$, a validator may first-vote in epoch~2 under the cross-epoch guard and then also signs a late notarization in epoch~1. A block may finalize before $S_1$ is known under the overlap exception, or by rechecking against $S_1$ after installation. Switches: \texttt{NCSPLIT} (a validator records $\tagN$ and final-votes only on an $\NC$ of $q$ first votes; off, late notarizations count too), \texttt{DISCHARGE} (a recovery record is discharged by a conflicting epoch-1 exclusion witness; off, never), \texttt{LATEEV} (late notarizations are admissible manifest evidence for discharge; off, they are ``never construction input''). Finality decisions are recorded globally rather than per validator: \texttt{ovf} for the overlap exception at a validator that has not installed $S_1$, \texttt{pof} for rechecking at one that has. Closure~2 is not built; its acceptance condition for the case of interest is \texttt{WitnessAdmissible}.

\para{\texttt{RaiHandoff}: early voting, two closures, changing committee.}
Epoch~1 is voted and closed by $K_1$, epoch~2 by $K_2$; the Byzantine validator sits in both. A member of $K_2$ may first-vote in epoch~2 before installing $S_1$ (an early vote); if it is also in $K_1$ and supported nothing else in epoch~1, that vote comes with a late notarization in epoch~1. Fix~B, the NC split, matching-origin discharge, and late votes as evidence are built in. \texttt{RULE = FALSE} is the earlier certificate definition: a fast certificate counts all first votes and a validator final-votes on its epoch-2 $\NC$ alone. \texttt{RULE = TRUE} is the early-vote rule: a fast certificate counts only first votes cast after the signer installed $S_1$, and an early final vote is released only by a validator that holds the epoch-1 exclusion witness. A block is final when its certificate can be formed from released signatures and it is valid against $S_1$, through the overlap bundle or by rechecking with discharge. The builder of $S_2$ holds a discharge witness only if a selected correct reporter relied on it; otherwise the adversary decides, which models a withheld signature. Further abstractions: correct reports carry no $\tagF$ tag for an observed certificate (covered by the other two models); a claim that conflicts with a surviving record is ignored rather than invalidating the selection, which only adds checkpoints; epoch~3 is not modelled, so safety after $S_2$ is checked as Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} for $S_2$.

\para{\texttt{RaiPrefix}: the prefix-wide guard on two positions.}
One account with a parent $P$ and a rival $P'$ at position~1 and two children $C_1$, $C_2$ of $P$ at position~2. Epoch~1 is closing and voted by $K_1$, epoch~2 is open and voted by $K_2$; one Byzantine validator sits in both committees, and the two committees are identical, disjoint, or share $k$ members. Members of $K_1$ cross their boundary one by one. $S_1$ is never installed within the model, so every epoch-2 first vote is early: early votes form no fast certificate, and an epoch-2 final vote is released only under the overlap guard. A member of both committees that first-votes $x$ in epoch~2 also signs a late epoch-1 notarization for $x$, unless it first-voted at that position in epoch~1, and the cross-epoch rule stops it from first-voting a different block where it supported one in epoch~1. The closure and $\Build$ are left out: early finality is checked against latent epoch-1 finality, that is, against any formable epoch-1 $\FC$ or $\FF$. Switches: \texttt{GUARD = "prefix"} requires $\XW_1$ and $\NC_2$ for every unresolved block on the path to the anchor, \texttt{GUARD = "target"} checks only the finalizing block; \texttt{FRESHCHILD = TRUE} requires a child to have its own $\XW_1$, \texttt{FALSE} lets $\NC_2$ for the child plus a parent with $\NC_1$ and $\NC_2$ suffice. Abstractions that only add behaviours: votes are signer sets, checks read formable certificates, finality is global, and members of $K_2\setminus K_1$ may vote in epoch~2 from the start.

\para{\texttt{RaiRecovery}: building on a recovery-locked block.}
One account with conflicting blocks $X$ and $Y$ at position~1 and a child of each at position~2, $C_X$ on $X$ and $C_Y$ on $Y$. Epoch~1 is voted by $K_1$ at position~1 and closed as in \texttt{RaiHandoff}; epoch~2 is voted by $K_2$ at both positions, early (before installing $S_1$, with late notarization, the cross-epoch rule, and the early-vote rule) or settled. Before installation a child is first-voted only on a parent complete in epoch~2 or final in epoch~1; after installation, on a parent final in $S_1$, or complete in epoch~2 and admissible against $S_1$, or, with \texttt{EXEMPT = TRUE}, retained by $S_1$ (the earlier retained-tip exemption). Early final votes need the prefix-wide overlap bundle with a fresh child witness. A final child finalizes its parent, and \texttt{NoConflict} counts that. Abstractions: nobody votes at position~2 in epoch~1, finality is global, and closure~2 is not built, so the model checks Theorem~\ref{thm:safety} within epoch~2 and against $S_1$. Run with $N=4$ and $N=6$.

\section{Scripted Regression Cases}
\label{app:cases}
The random Byzantine voter of the benchmark harness does not find the runs of Appendix~\ref{app:models}. The following scripted cases were derived from the violating traces; $N=6$, validator~6 is Byzantine, $B$ and $B'$ conflict.
\begin{description}
\item[Case 1 (Fix B).] Epoch~1: validators 1, 2, 3 vote $B'$; 4, 5 vote $B$; 6 reports $B$ only; 3's report is not selected. Epoch~2: 1 to 4 first-vote the retained $B$, nobody assembles the $\NC$. Release the epoch-1 $\NC$ for $B'$ after the second close. Expected: the second checkpoint carries $B$ with an origin-2 record; $B$ finalizes; $B'$ never does.
\item[Case 2 (discharge).] Before the boundary 1, 2, 6 vote $B'$. After it 3, 4, 5 vote $B$ in the open epoch and late-notarize it; 6 joins both. Install the checkpoint at 1 and 2 before they see $B$'s certificate. Expected: every validator finalizes $B$, and the next closure builds.
\item[Mixed NC.] One validator still in the closing epoch receives three first votes and one late notarization for its block. Expected: it does not record $\tagN$; its report is usable.
\item[Case 3 (early votes).] The run of Appendix~\ref{app:open}. Expected: $B$ has no fast certificate; 3, 4, 5 final-vote $B$ only if they hold the witness, and then the second checkpoint finalizes $B$. Without the early-vote rule this run ends with both blocks final.
\item[Case 3, new members.] Epoch~1: 1, 2, 3 vote $B$; 4, 5 vote $B'$; 6 reports $B'$ only and withholds its vote for $B$. Four members who were not in the old committee early-vote $B$. Same expectation as Case~3.
\item[Case 5 (re-notarization).] Epoch~$e$: 1, 2, 6 first-vote $B'$ at position~7; 3, 4, 5 cross their boundary without voting there. Before $S_e$ is known, 3, 4, 5 first-vote $B$ in epoch $e+1$ and late-notarize it in $e$; 6 signs both for $B$, so $\XW_e(B)$ and $\NC_{e+1}(B)$ hold. Closure $e$ selects 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and keeps $B'$ under a recovery record of origin $e$. 3, 4, 5 final-vote $B$, either after installing $S_e$, where $\XW_e(B)$ discharges the record, or before it under the early-vote rule once 6 shows them its signatures; with 6, $B$ is final. The owner publishes $C'$ on $B'$ at position~8, and 1 to 5 first-vote it after installing $S_e$: a fast certificate for $C'$ finalizes $B'$ too. \texttt{RaiRecovery} with \texttt{EXEMPT = TRUE} finds a shorter run without early votes: in epoch~$e$, 1 and 2 vote $X$ and 3 votes $Y$ ($N=4$), the checkpoint keeps $Y$ on the Byzantine report, and after installing it 1, 2, 3 first-vote both $X$ (whose witness discharges $Y$'s record) and $C_Y$. Expected under the re-notarization rule: $C'$ is refused at its first vote, because $B'$ has at most the epoch-$(e+1)$ votes of 1, 2, and 6 at position~7; in a run where nobody voted $B$, $B'$ is notarized again and $C'$ finalizes both.
\item[Case 4 (prefix).] Epoch~1: $P$ is notarized; $C_1$ has first votes from 1, 2, 3. After the boundary, members of a committee sharing two members with the old one first-vote $C_2$ early and late-notarize it. Expected: $C_2$ does not finalize early without its own epoch-1 witness, and no validator finalizes $C_2$ while an epoch-1 certificate for $C_1$ can form.
\end{description}
\texttt{RaiClose} with \texttt{FIXB = FALSE} prints Case~1 step by step, \texttt{RaiOverlap} with \texttt{DISCHARGE = FALSE} prints Case~2, \texttt{RaiHandoff} with \texttt{RULE = FALSE} prints both Case~3 runs, and \texttt{RaiPrefix} with \texttt{FRESHCHILD = FALSE} or \texttt{GUARD = "target"} prints Case~4, and \texttt{RaiRecovery} with \texttt{EXEMPT = TRUE} prints the short form of Case~5. In the prototype, Cases 1, 2, Mixed NC, 3, and 4 exist as unit tests of the construction and of the voting rules, and Case~5 as unit tests of the attachment rule (a child of a recovery-only tip is first-voted only once the tip is notarized in the child's epoch, and at once under a notarization lock); the benchmark harness's Byzantine validator votes at random and does not play these scripts, and a live harness with a scripted Byzantine validator that withholds named votes was not built. We consider this the main gap between the models and the measurements.

\section{Prototype Notes}
\label{app:proto}
\label{sec:eval}
\subsection{Departures from the protocol}
First, the manifest commits to signed votes and marks the closing-epoch witness of every overlap certificate its evidence proves: the closing-epoch exclusion witness, the current-epoch $\NC$, a matching-origin witness for every record of the last closed state $S_{e-2}$ the block bypasses, and a parent finalized in $S_{e-1}$ or itself certified. The marks are a function of the evidence, the selection, and the two closed states, so a validator recomputes them and refuses a manifest that marks any other set. Blocks and ancestry are placed by the report items, and the witnesses that discharge records of the predecessor checkpoint are re-derived by every validator from the manifest's votes rather than bound to the claims they serve. Second, a first vote names as its base the state hash of the checkpoint its signer had installed, $d_{e-1}$ for a settled vote and $d_{e-2}$ for an early one, rather than a digest of the per-block admission evidence; a settled vote counts toward a fast certificate only at a validator that installed the checkpoint it names, and a validator that has not yet installed it holds the vote and counts it at installation. Third, retained vote records keep a fixed cap of four epochs, since the reports of the next three epochs may still cite an epoch's late finality, and beyond it only the support that may still discharge a surviving recovery record of its origin at its position. The handoff evidence, an epoch's frozen reports and their reconstructions, is released once members of the successor committee carrying $N-f$ of its weight acknowledge the checkpoint; a member acknowledges once the decided state is written and synced, empty checkpoints included, and unreleased epochs are kept up to sixteen, a forced drop being logged (none occurred in any run). Fourth, durability rests on the host's \texttt{fsync}: first, final, late, and close-election votes, frozen reports, overlap evidence, the epoch origin, and decided checkpoints are synced before any signature that depends on them leaves the process, but on the macOS host \texttt{fsync} hands the data to the drive without forcing its cache. A stricter mode issues \texttt{F\_FULLFSYNC} after every signing write (about 4\,ms each). Power loss was not simulated; the restart runs kill processes and test crash consistency only. Fifth, a position whose committed first votes are split so that no block can be notarized any more is not carried into the new epoch at the boundary; if the checkpoint then retains neither side, the position is voted afresh, and if it retains one side, that side is continued. Sixth, the \emph{pause} transition used as a baseline in the evaluation is a configuration switch of the same code: under it no instance of epoch $e+1$ is started before $S_e$ is decided locally, so the overlap rule and late notarization never apply; everything else, including reports, the construction, and installation, is unchanged. Seventh, the emulated link delay is applied per channel at the sender: every outbound message leaves its channel a fixed time after it was queued, in order, which approximates a one-way link latency without a network emulator.

\subsection{Defects found by the benchmark matrix}
Several defects were found and repaired while measuring; none is a failure of the two services. (1)~A validator records, for every block it votes for, the parent that block names, so that its residual records place the block; the record was dropped when the slot state of the older of two consecutive epochs that both voted the block was discarded. The newer report then committed a residual record with a zero parent, $\Build$ failed at every leader that selected it, and two variants never closed an epoch. The record now outlives every slot state that votes the block, and a report naming a block without its parent is malformed and never selected. (2)~A block whose first votes split across an epoch boundary, three cast in the epoch left and two in the next, was retained as a recovery lock and never finalized; every unfinalized instance of the epoch left is now re-voted in the new epoch, and the generator implements the owner policy for the positions that remain. (3)~Manifest chunk requests were flooded to every representative and the inbound queues overflowed; requests now go to the proposal's sender. (4)~Re-voting every unfinalized instance filled the election container to its former cap of 5,000, after which no new block was activated on any validator; carried instances whose lock cannot continue are discarded at installation and the cap was raised. (5)~Committee derivation deduplicated the epoch's finalized blocks quadratically, durable vote records shared the ledger's LMDB writer, and the predecessor gate was opened only after the committee bookkeeping, which together lengthened every wait at the gate. (6)~Late notarization first shared a vote generator with the consensus instantiation's notarization votes and opened an instance of the closing epoch at its receivers for each late vote. (7)~After the repairs of Appendix~\ref{app:models}, the manifest's entries grew to 72 bytes with the late and settled voter bit sets, a full manifest reply overflowed its 16-bit length field, and validators whose own manifest differed from the leader's silently refused every proposal; the chunk size was reduced. (8)~A released epoch was recreated by a peer's repeated report and re-verified on every tick under the election container's lock, which stalled vote application for about a second after each installation; the exchange now remembers released epochs. (9)~Copies of a proposal that leaders repeat every 200\,ms were re-checked in full after the epoch was decided while they held the report-exchange lock, so inbound publishes were dropped and the p95 of one variant swung between 0.4 and 2.9\,s between runs; a queued copy now returns once the value is held or the epoch decided.

The restart, rotation, and handoff runs of Appendix~\ref{app:restart} found further defects. (10)~The epoch origin $T_0$ was not persisted, so a restarted validator stayed in epoch~0; the origin, the genesis committee, the decided checkpoints, and the close votes are now durable records. (11)~Once its peers had released an epoch's reports at $N-f$ acknowledgements, a validator one close behind could no longer reconstruct them and derive the checkpoint: it now fetches the value and state by the certificate's value hash from any holder; it also asks for the first rounds' statements of a close open for 2\,s, ends its epoch once members holding more than $f$ of the weight are seen voting in later epochs (otherwise a lagging replica leaves one epoch per boundary and never closes the gap), and, while its epoch has no election, asks once per epoch for the epoch's close. (12)~Synced signing first added up to 440\,ms to vote release, since the voter waited for the environment's single writer behind synced evidence batches and boundary deletions; decided states and evidence moved to a second environment and deletions to bounded transactions. (13)~Rotation exposed four membership defects: a leader selected a non-member's report, which the manifest's member bit sets cannot name; a member whose weight had moved away stopped voting with its key and was tiered by weight; its votes were dropped by a weight gate; and a validator missing a frontier block's delegation derived another committee. Members are now known by membership, and derivation waits for the frontier blocks. (14)~A position split three against three in epoch $e$, retained under recovery records of origin $e$, was finalized in $e+1$ by a changed committee: four of its members first-voted one side early, and after installing $S_e$ the validators final-voted without rechecking eligibility, although no epoch-$e$ witness discharged the rival's record. Every correct report then carried an $\tagF$ entry the report check refuses and the close stalled. The rule of Appendix~\ref{sec:voting} already forbids this final vote; the prototype now withholds it. (15)~A validator that fell behind solicited more evidence, the replies overflowed its 64-message per-channel inbound queue, and among the drops were the few close-round votes it needed; with a Byzantine member every correct report is needed, so one straggler stalled the close. Close-round votes now bypass that queue, and members are solicited by membership. (16)~The acknowledgement of an installed checkpoint was sent on the in-memory installation, never for a checkpoint that finalized nothing new, and the report exchange dropped epochs after four whether released or not.

Two prototype behaviours found by the matrix of the main text are not fixed. (17)~At a boundary the prototype re-votes in the new epoch every position at which a block can still be notarized, each validator voting the block it voted before. It decides this by counting the weight of members not seen voting as possibly still to come, so with a validator offline a position split three against two among the five running validators looks open: it is carried, the split repeats in the new epoch, and the minority's one first vote there is spent before the checkpoint that dropped their side is known. With all six running the same split is recognised and not carried, and after the checkpoint every validator votes the kept block. (18)~An epoch's timer starts with the first new election in it; positions carried into the epoch do not start it, so once the load stops no further checkpoint is decided. Together these leave the forked positions of some runs kept, identically everywhere, rather than finalized (the unresolved-fork figure of the main text). Neither affects safety or the owner-recovery guarantee of Theorem~\ref{thm:account-live}(c).

A review after the matrix found one more defect, in the protocol and in the prototype alike. (19)~A maximum-depth tip held by recovery records alone counted as a final parent, so a child of it was first-voted without a new notarization of the tip, and Case~5 finalized both sides of a fork. The prototype now requires such a tip to hold a notarization in the child's epoch, as for any other unresolved parent. The matrix runs predate the change; the load generator never builds on a forked position, so they never took the changed path.

\subsection{What late notarization buys}
On the build of October~7, 2026, before the repairs of Appendix~\ref{app:models}, the nine-variant matrix was run twice on the same day: with the core prefix-wide overlap rule alone and with late notarization added. Without late notarization a block published during a close has no closing-epoch exclusion witness, so every such block waited for the checkpoint: 1,900 to 7,100 instances per boundary, client confirmations stopped for one to three seconds at each close, p95 was 1.3 to 2.4\,s without forks and 2.1 to 8.2\,s with forks, and 10\%-fork goodput was 15 to 25\% lower. With late notarization p95 fell four- to eightfold in every variant (no forks: 305, 176, and 186\,ms for the all-online, one-offline, and one-Byzantine variants) while medians stayed within 10\,ms. Late notarization keeps the core rule and its proof, costs one more signature per block and committee member during each close, and needs no members common to the two committees. The pause baseline of the main text's evaluation is the other end of the same scale: no early votes at all.

\subsection{Committee change}
\label{app:rotation}
A first committee model keeps the same six members and recomputes their weights from the finalized state at each checkpoint within $\pm10\%$ of the equal share; every validator derived the same committee digests, and the no-fork variants settled with latencies close to the equal-weight runs. The equal-weight model re-selects the heaviest $N$ representatives from the finalized state, so committee identities change when weight moves. The harness runs extra representatives outside the committee and, at the start of a chosen epoch $k$, moves a member's whole balance to a holder delegating to a standby representative; the committee derived from epoch $k$, used from epoch $k+2$, has the standby in the member's place. At 800 blocks/s, 20{,}000 blocks, and 5\% forks, with eight validators of which six form the committee: replacing two members settled in 3 of 3 runs (667 to 748 non-fork blocks/s, p50 103 to 106\,ms); replacing two members and restoring them two epochs later with a Byzantine member in every committee settled in 2 of 2 runs (578 and 581 blocks/s, p95 385 and 572\,ms); and with a four-member committee ($f=1$, $p=0$) whose three correct members were all replaced while the Byzantine member stayed, 1 of 1 (728 blocks/s, p50/p95 208/337\,ms). Every validator derived the same members for every epoch, checked by listing them, and no run produced a conflicting signature. These counts are after the repairs (13) to (15) above; before them 2 of 5 two-member runs and every Byzantine run stalled at the first close after the change. Ten validators were more than the host could carry, hence the four-member committee for the full replacement. These runs are from the build of October~9, 2026 (commit \texttt{4582bf003} and later), one day before the matrix of the main text.

\subsection{Restart, durable signing, and handoff}
\label{app:restart}
Every node logs each signature it releases to a file before sending it. The harness kills validators with \texttt{SIGKILL}, either at a fixed time into the load, while elections are running, or when a given close starts, restarts them on their data after a chosen downtime, and afterwards checks every logged signature of every node: no identity may have signed two blocks in one first-vote or final-vote instance or two values in one close round. Fork5 with two restarts, one at a close and one mid-load, settled twice with every checkpoint decided identically on all six validators and no conflicting signature; the restarted validators rejoined through checkpoint catch-up and follow-ahead.

Synced signing costs latency, not goodput. Fork5, six validators, 45{,}000 blocks at 2{,}000 blocks/s, two runs per setting: without sync 1{,}765 and 1{,}820 non-fork blocks/s at p50 173 and 139\,ms; with \texttt{fsync} 1{,}802 and 1{,}688 at 282 and 310\,ms; with \texttt{F\_FULLFSYNC} 1{,}763 and 1{,}777 at 409 and 304\,ms. The matrix of the main text runs with \texttt{fsync}.

Handoff with a crashing departing member: a member replaced at the rotation of epoch~1 is killed when the close of epoch~2, the last it decides, starts, and stays down 30\,s; eight validators, 800 blocks/s, 20{,}000 blocks, 5\% forks. Without and with a Byzantine member every run settled with every checkpoint decided identically (epochs 0 to 4 and 0 to 5), the restarted member caught up the epochs it missed and installed them, every epoch's handoff evidence was released only after $N-f$ durable acknowledgements, no epoch was dropped before release, and no signature conflicted (340 and 335 non-fork blocks/s; the client counts the outage).

Committing the overlap marks and naming the vote base were each compared against the build before them, two fork5 runs and one fork5 run with a Byzantine member per arm, alternating. Neither changed goodput, latency, or close time beyond the spread between runs of the same build (1{,}510 to 1{,}792 non-fork blocks/s, p50 167 to 332\,ms across the twelve runs); checking the marks adds 30 to 60\,ms to a follower's evidence check, and the base moves about 10\% of fast finalizations to final votes, since a settled vote whose base a receiver has not installed no longer counts there. About 20{,}000 of the 25{,}000 blocks of a fork5 run finalize under the overlap exception, and the closes mark 5{,}000 to 14{,}000 certificates per epoch.

The prototype, the harness, the TLA\textsuperscript{+} models, and every run record (the per-run \texttt{result.json} with the client's per-second series and the nodes' epoch events, the node configurations, and the logs) are in the artifact. The matrix of the main text is produced by
\begin{quote}\small\texttt{python3 tools/rai/run\_eval\_matrix.py <dir> --reps 3\\
\ \ --arm rai=<bin> --arm pause=<bin>:--epoch-transition=pause\\
\ \ --arm nano=<develop bin>:--no-epochs}

after building the node and the generator in release mode with the \texttt{rai\_protocol} feature, and the develop node without it; \texttt{--extra "--network-delay-ms 40"} adds the link delay.
