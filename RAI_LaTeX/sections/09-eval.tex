\section{Implementation and Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}
\para{Prototype.}
We implemented RAI in RsNano~\cite{rsnano}, a Rust implementation of the Nano node~\cite{nano}. Account elections follow Section~\ref{sec:account} with the equal-weight thresholds of Table~\ref{tab:thresholds} ($f=p=1$, $N=6$). Epochs end at fixed times $T_0+kD$ on every validator's local clock. At its boundary a validator freezes and signs its report, and the two services of Section~\ref{sec:services} are instantiated as follows.

\para{Convergence by rateless set reconciliation.}
A report's two committed sets are reconstructed with rateless invertible Bloom lookup tables~\cite{iblt,whatsthedifference,rateless}. Each set element is a fixed 105-byte item naming account, height, hash, parent, and tag. A validator that needs a report starts from its own live projection of the closing epoch, which usually differs from the reporter's frozen set in a few entries, and asks for coded symbols of the frozen set; any validator that holds the reconstructed set answers, so the reporter is not a single point of service. Batches start at 16 symbols and grow to half of what has been received, up to 500. Peeling the symbols against the local projection yields the symmetric difference; the requester applies it and checks the signed root, which gives C1. In the runs below, reconstruction needed 1.5 to 3.2 coded symbols per differing item and no stream was abandoned. Before a report is usable, the validator fetches, by hash, the signed votes behind every $\tagN$ and $\tagF$ entry and every residual record it does not already hold, and rechecks only the entries that were missing.

\para{Consensus by a Kudzu-style election.}
The closing committee decides the checkpoint with one leader-based voting instance in the style of Kudzu~\cite{kudzu}, whose rounds, vote kinds, thresholds, and proof arguments are given in the supplement. Leaders rotate through the committee in canonical key order; a leader proposes the candidate built from the canonical prefix of usable reports, so two correct leaders holding the same reports propose the same value and the first round normally decides. A validator first-votes a proposal only after reconstructing its reports and recomputing the state hash, which is A2 on the eligibility predicate; final votes with threshold $q$ form the decision proof, which successors verify against the committee (A1, A3). A round whose leader is offline or Byzantine times out and the next leader proposes (A4). A sufficient local timeout condition for this instantiation is $\Delta_E/(1+\rho)>\kappa_E+\tau_E+\delta$, where $\kappa_E$ bounds round-entry skew and $\tau_E$ bounds proposal preparation and validation~\cite{dls}. Successors install a decided checkpoint off the vote-processing path and fetch, by hash, any block of a retained or finalized branch they do not hold.

\para{Departures from the protocol.}
First, first votes, terminal states, and retained evidence are held in memory; there is no durable signing record, so the runs assume no crashes. Second, the candidate value carries the report selection and the state digest but no manifest digest $\mu_e$: evidence is content-addressed and fetched by hash, and a validator that cannot assemble it does not vote. Third, the prototype implements the overlap exceptions of an earlier revision, which include the predecessor-backed route of the supplement, rather than only the prefix-wide restriction of Section~\ref{sec:eligibility}. Fourth, the measured build predates Rule~3: it installed certificate-backed finality only. Promotion of a unique retained child with a closing-epoch $\NC$ at the finalized tip was implemented afterwards, with unit tests, and the numbers below were not re-measured with it; the positions left retained in the measured runs had no notarization on either side, which is the condition Rule~3 requires. Fifth, retained votes and blocks are kept for four epochs.

\para{Baseline.}
The baseline is unmodified RsNano (\texttt{develop}), which runs Nano's open representative voting: a block is confirmed once final votes reach 67\% of the online weight, after a first round of non-final votes. It has no epochs and no checkpoints. Final votes cannot be withdrawn, so a fork whose votes split can leave no side able to reach the quorum; the baseline gives no termination guarantee in that case, and its unresolved elections keep their slots in a bounded election container.

\para{Setup.}
All validators run as separate processes on one MacBook Air (Apple M1, eight cores, 16\,GiB, macOS~14.6) and communicate over loopback; LMDB runs without synchronous writes. Six equal-weight representatives hold the voting weight. A load generator funds 45{,}000 accounts and then publishes 45{,}000 blocks at an offered 2{,}000 blocks/s, each on the confirmed frontier of a randomly chosen account, so account chains grow during the run. With forks enabled, it publishes a conflicting sibling for 5\% or 10\% of the blocks, each sibling to half of the validators. An \emph{offline} validator runs no node and never votes but keeps its weight. A \emph{Byzantine} validator also runs no node; the generator votes with its key at random, choosing any vote kind for recently published blocks and contradicting itself. Both arms use the same load-generator source; the binaries differ only in the vote encoding, since RAI votes carry their epoch. RAI uses 8\,s epochs, so a run spans three checkpoints. A run \emph{settles} when every running validator holds the same cemented state and, for RAI, has decided the same checkpoints; a position left retained by a split vote counts as settled when it is uncemented on every validator alike. We report non-fork blocks only: goodput is the number of non-fork blocks confirmed divided by the time from the start of publishing to the last such confirmation, and latency runs from a block's publication to its confirmation at one validator. The baseline's publishing phase may last 1.5 times RAI's longest publishing phase for the same variant; setup time is not counted. Each variant ran once per system on an otherwise idle machine; the harness refuses to start while any other process uses more than a quarter of a core. The baseline rows were taken in an earlier pass on the same host with the same generator; the baseline code did not change. The RAI rows were taken in one pass on the build that includes the fix described below.

\para{Without forks.}
Table~\ref{tab:eval} shows both systems keeping up with the offered load. RAI's median latency is about half the baseline's, with a lower p95 in all three variants and a lower p99 in two. A likely cause is that an uncontested RAI block finalizes on one round of $N-p$ first votes, where the baseline needs a round of final votes after a quorum of non-final ones. One offline or Byzantine validator slows neither system; with five running nodes instead of six, both use less of the shared CPU. Three checkpoints were decided on every running validator in all variants. The first two closes of every run decided in the first election round; the third close decided in the first round when all six validators were running and in the second round with one validator offline or Byzantine, when leader rotation reached the missing validator and its round timed out. From the first validator leaving an epoch to the last validator seeing the decision took 1.1 to 2.4\,s for first-round closes without forks or at 5\% forks, up to 3.9\,s at 10\% forks, and 3.6 to 4.3\,s when a round timed out; epoch $e+1$ voting continued throughout.

\para{With forks.}
The baseline finished none of the six fork variants: forks whose votes split stay unresolved and occupy election slots, which delays the non-fork blocks queued behind them, and the confirmed share at the deadline ranged from 69\% to 95\%. RAI settled all six fork variants with three checkpoints decided identically on every running validator. Its median stayed near the no-fork median; the p95 and p99 tails are longer, and they are the pipeline's visible cost: a block published while an epoch closes is held provisional by the predecessor gate until the close decides, so its latency includes the close duration. Goodput falls with the fork rate because it is measured to the last non-fork confirmation and a few late blocks stretch that interval. Most fork positions stayed retained: the generator sends each sibling to half of the validators, so a fork's first votes split three against three and neither side reaches $q=4$, and without a notarization Rule~3 cannot promote either side, so with no owner continuation in the workload the position remains a recovery lock. About 2{,}200 positions at 5\% forks and 4{,}200 to 4{,}500 at 10\% were uncemented when the runs settled, identically on every validator; they did not prevent settlement, and every non-fork block confirmed.

\para{A defect found by the matrix.}
With the build before the measured one, two variants never closed an epoch: at 10\% forks without faults and at 5\% forks with one validator offline, every election round ended with the leader unable to construct a candidate, and the epoch stayed open until the run ended. The cause was a bookkeeping slip in the report. A validator records, for every block it votes for, the parent that block names, so that its residual records place the block; the record was dropped when the slot state of the older of two consecutive epochs that both voted the block was discarded, although the newer epoch's state still named it. The newer report then committed a residual record with a zero parent, and $\Build$ failed with a missing-ancestry error at every leader that selected the report, which with $N-f=5$ usable reports required of six validators was every leader. The record now outlives every slot state that votes the block. The incident also showed a liveness exposure to a Byzantine reporter: a single malformed report in every canonical selection would stall the close the same way. A report naming a block without its parent is now malformed and is never selected, as Section~\ref{sec:transitions} specifies. Neither the consensus service nor the convergence service failed here: the reports converged and no eligible candidate existed, which is exactly the premise of A4 that RAI's own report rules must supply. Two earlier defects found by the same workloads, a mismatch between how an election and a report checker counted notarization support and a solicitation that re-sent held fork candidates every round, are described in the records that accompany the prototype.

\para{Limitations.}
Each variant ran once, so the table gives no run-to-run variance; in earlier repeated runs of this prototype, p95 varied by up to 40\% between identical runs. All validators share one host, so CPU contention rather than network latency sets the delays, and the fixed offered load measures neither saturation capacity nor wide-area behavior. The two arms were not interleaved in one pass. The prototype's in-memory signing records, its broader overlap rule, and its missing manifest digest mean these results do not validate the durability, the exact overlap restriction, or the fixed-input commitment assumed by the proofs, and the measured build did not yet promote notarized prefixes at checkpoints.

\section{Conclusion}
RAI makes epoch transitions for account-local payments pipelined and safe. The next epoch's voting starts at each validator's local boundary while the previous epoch closes; immutable reports expose enough of the outgoing committee's released votes to preserve latent and late finality; a deterministic construction over eligible report selections produces the checkpoint; and a predecessor gate with a narrow prefix-wide overlap exception keeps successor finality compatible with whatever the outgoing committee could still certify. The protocol defines the reports and the eligibility of checkpoint candidates and leaves reconstruction and agreement to two abstract services, convergence and consensus, with stated interfaces; safety and termination follow from their safety and termination together with explicit fault, data, and progress assumptions. A prototype instantiating them with rateless set reconciliation and a Kudzu-style election settles all nine workload variants, including forks with one offline or one Byzantine validator, while keeping non-fork median latency near 100\,ms across three checkpoints.
