\section{Inside an Epoch}
\label{sec:epoch}
Within an epoch RAI votes like Nano or FastPay, with two twists that pay off at the boundary: a validator never switches sides, and a final vote is preceded by a notarization.

\para{Confirming a block.}
Alice signs block $A$ at position 7 of her account and sends it to the six validators. Each validator checks the block against Alice's chain as it knows it (parent, balance, no duplicate receive) and, if it has not voted at position 7 in this epoch, casts a first vote for $A$ (\ref{r:one}). With four first votes $A$ is \emph{notarized}; a validator that holds the notarization and first-voted $A$ casts its final vote. Four final votes make $A$ final. If five first votes arrive from validators that have installed the previous checkpoint, $A$ is final at once, in one round trip; this is the common case, and the reason RAI's median latency in Section~\ref{sec:eval} is below Nano's, which always needs a second round.

\para{Why the thresholds are safe.}
Any two groups of four validators out of six share at least two, and at most one of those is Byzantine. A correct validator supports one block per position (\ref{r:one}), so two conflicting blocks cannot both reach four supporters. Four supporters for $A$ therefore \emph{exclude} every rival: no rival can be notarized or final in this epoch. We call a set of $q$ supporters an \emph{exclusion witness}; it is the one piece of evidence the boundary rules will ask for again and again. Five first votes for $A$ leave at most one correct validator free to support a rival, so a fast-final block has no rival with three honest supporters either; this is what makes the recovery threshold $r=3$ of~\ref{r:build} work.

\para{A double spend.}
Suppose Alice also signs $A'$ at position 7, paying someone else, and shows $A$ to three validators and $A'$ to the other three (Figure~\ref{fig:fork}). Neither reaches four. In Nano this position is stuck: final votes cannot be withdrawn, no block at position 7 will ever confirm, and every later block of Alice's account waits behind it. In RAI the next checkpoint decides what may still happen at position 7 (\ref{r:build}). It is built from five of the six reports, which show three first votes for one side and two for the other, say three for $A$. $A$ is kept under a \emph{recovery lock} of origin $e$ and $A'$ is dropped: from now on no validator admits $A'$ or any other rival of $A$ at position 7, so $A'$ can never become final. Should the five reports show three against three, which needs a reporter who voted both sides, nothing is kept at position 7.

\para{How Alice recovers.}
RAI guarantees that Alice gets her account back once she stops contradicting herself (Theorem~\ref{thm:live}). She reads the decided checkpoint from any validator and acts on what it says:
\begin{itemize}
\item If it kept $A$, she signs one new block $C$ at position 8 on top of $A$; $C$ may carry a payment or nothing at all. Nothing conflicts with $C$, so it collects four first votes and four final votes in the next epoch and becomes final, and finalizing $C$ finalizes $A$ below it (Figure~\ref{fig:fork}).
\item If it kept nothing at position 7, she publishes one block there, $A$, $A'$, or a new one, and only that one; it is uncontested and becomes final like any other block.
\item If one side is already final, there is nothing to recover.
\end{itemize}
She cannot pick the side the checkpoint dropped: a block on top of $A'$ is refused while the lock on $A$ stands. In the normal case recovery takes until the checkpoint after the fork is installed, plus one confirmation.

Alice often does not need to do anything. In the next epoch the validators vote the kept block $A$ again, and those that voted $A'$ in epoch $e$ may now vote $A$, because the decided checkpoint proves that no epoch-$e$ certificate for $A'$ can form; $A$ then becomes final on its own. This is a convenience, not a guarantee: a validator may already have spent its one first vote in the new epoch, the votes can split again, and an attacker who keeps signing alternatives can keep a position open. What RAI guarantees for such a position is that it is kept identically on every validator, that at most one side ever becomes final, and that it blocks nothing outside the account.

\para{Durable votes.}
A correct validator writes its first vote and its final vote to disk before releasing them, so that a crash and restart cannot make it vote twice. Our prototype syncs these writes; Section~\ref{sec:eval} reports what that costs.
