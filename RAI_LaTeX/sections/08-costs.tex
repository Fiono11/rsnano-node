\section{Costs, Scope, and Limitations}
\label{sec:costs}
The agreed value contains $N-f$ report headers and two digests, so its reference portion is $O(N)$, independent of the number of account blocks; the manifest, reconstruction, validation, and retained histories scale with old state, new votes, and unresolved branches. A correct validator releases at most one first vote, one final vote, and, during a close, one late notarization per account instance; this bounds neither an adversary's owner-signed alternatives nor traffic or validation work.

The pipeline has an explicit frontier. A block whose late notarizations split, or that lacks one of the $q$ supporters, waits for the predecessor checkpoint; a block first-voted during a close finalizes in two rounds, since early votes form no fast certificate; and a block whose closing-epoch exclusion witness is withheld cannot become final behind the checkpoint. Checkpoint promotion finalizes a uniquely retained closing-epoch notarized prefix without an account certificate, never recovery-only state, and resolves no fork: a position whose votes split evenly stays retained, identically everywhere, until the owner extends it or a later epoch's exclusion witness discharges the records of one side.

Forks and retained dependencies can accumulate without bound, and lock records, late notarizations, and overlap certificates must be kept as long as a record they can discharge survives; the model supplies no admission-control or garbage-collection policy. RAI is for authenticated committees with an externally maintained fault bound, not a permissionless staking system; the static adversary excludes later compromise of departed validators, and account operations exclude shared objects, multiwriter transactions, and a guaranteed winner under arbitrary equivocation.
