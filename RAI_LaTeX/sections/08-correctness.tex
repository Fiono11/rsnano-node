\section{Why It Is Safe and Why It Terminates}
\label{sec:correctness}
We state the results and give the reason for each in a sentence or two; the supplement has the full proofs, numbered as here, with Theorem~\ref{thm:live} split in two. Safety relies on the integrity of set reconstruction and on the agreement, eligibility, and verifiability of agreement; termination also relies on their termination properties.

Three lemmas carry the arguments. \emph{Account exclusion} (Lemma~1): two sets of $q$ supporters share a correct validator, so at most one block per position has an exclusion witness, and a block that can become fast-final leaves every rival fewer than $r$ supporters. \emph{Notarized ancestry} (Lemma~2): a child is first-voted only on a parent that is final, under a notarization lock, or notarized in the child's epoch, so two conflicting final branches meet at one position where Lemma~1 or a lock excludes one. \emph{Exposure} (Lemma~4): a correct reporter's frozen report shows every block it final-voted as notarized or final and lists every other block it first-voted, including blocks the predecessor already kept.

\begin{theorem}[Closure preserves latent and late finality]
\label{thm:closure}
If an epoch-$e$ final certificate for $B$ can be formed from released votes, even one completed after the reports froze or after $S_e$ was decided, then every eligible checkpoint $S_e$ holds $B$: as final, under a notarization lock, or under a recovery lock of origin $e$. No admissible evidence releases that hold.
\end{theorem}
\emph{Why.} A normal certificate's $q$ final voters and the $N-f$ selected reporters share $p+1$ correct validators, each reporting $B$ notarized; a fast certificate's $N-p$ voters share $r$ correct ones, so $B$ reaches $r$ settled reporter votes while no rival does, and the recipe, working down, locks $B$ or a child of it. Only an epoch-$e$ witness for a rival could release that lock, and Lemma~1 excludes one.

\setcounter{lemma}{7}
\begin{lemma}[Same verdict before and after installation]
\label{lem:verdict}
A validator that finalizes $B$ under the overlap rule before installing $S_e$ accepts $B$ if and only if it would accept $B$ by re-checking after installing $S_e$; and a later checkpoint verifies a report that claims $B$ final.
\end{lemma}
\emph{Why.} The overlap rule asks for exactly the evidence that re-checking consults: the epoch-$e$ witness rules out a final or notarized rival in $S_e$ and releases any origin-$e$ lock on one, and matching-origin witnesses release any older lock.

\begin{theorem}[Transfer safety across epochs]
\label{thm:safety}
Finalized histories at correct validators are compatible and never rolled back, finalized balances are nonnegative, and each send is received at most once.
\end{theorem}
\emph{Why.} By induction over epochs, jointly with Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}: inside an epoch Lemmas~1 and~2 apply; at a boundary every correct validator installs the same eligible checkpoint, which preserves every formable old certificate; and in the overlap, Lemma~\ref{lem:verdict} makes all correct validators agree on which blocks are final.

\begin{theorem}[Termination]
\label{thm:live}
Every started handover is eventually decided and installed by correct validators. An owner whose new blocks are uncontested sees them final and in a checkpoint eventually. A forked position whose parent is final becomes final, on one side, by the end of any two consecutive epochs of normal operation, whatever the owner and the Byzantine validators do; the owner then continues from it.
\end{theorem}
\emph{Why.} $N-f$ correct reports become usable, so agreement decides an eligible checkpoint. At a fork still open after a good epoch, every correct validator has seen both blocks; the next checkpoint keeps a side, preferring the one it kept before, and in the next good epoch all of them vote only that side, which becomes final.
