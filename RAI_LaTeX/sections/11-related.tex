\para{Payments without consensus.}
Asset transfer needs no consensus~\cite{consensusnumber}. FastPay settles prefunded payments without a global order~\cite{fastpay}, Astro and Carbon build payments on reliable broadcast~\cite{astro,camaioni2024carbon}, Zef adds private coins~\cite{zef}, and Nano uses account chains with open representative voting~\cite{nano}. Table~\ref{tab:related} compares how the systems that change validators treat the boundary and an owner who equivocates.

\para{Handovers that pause.}
Sui Lutris proves the reconfiguration of a consensusless fast path safe; its handover waits for $2f+1$ validators to end the epoch, drops the lock stores, and rolls back transactions that no checkpoint contains~\cite{lutris}. Sui holds user transactions for a few hundred milliseconds at the boundary and keeps an equivocated object locked until the next epoch, about a day later~\cite{sui2026epochs,sui2026lifecycle}. Mysticeti-FPC pauses its fast path with an epoch-change bit and unlocks transactions that equivocation blocked once the epoch ends~\cite{babel2025mysticeti}; Cuttlefish broadens the fast path with collective objects and FastUnlock~\cite{cuttlefish}. Their pause is short because consensus already orders every certificate. RAI has no ordering layer, and its close needs reports and an agreement that take seconds, so it overlaps the close instead of pausing; unable to drop both sides of a fork, it keeps one (Section~\ref{sec:intro}).

\para{Handovers that fence or migrate.}
Carbon lets clients vote validators in and out and moves between views as dynamic Byzantine reliable broadcast does; once a new view is approved, operations addressed to the old one no longer commit~\cite{camaioni2024carbon,guerraoui2020dbrb}. Pastro and asynchronous Byzantine reconfiguration treat changing participation more generally~\cite{pastro,reconfiguration}, and DynaStore, FreeStore, SmartMerge, and reconfigurable lattice agreement change the replicas of a storage service without consensus~\cite{aguilera2011dynastore,alchieri2017freestore,jehl2015smartmerge,kuznetsov2019rla}. PDCC accepts payment certificates only for the installed configuration and assumes correct members common to consecutive configurations~\cite{pdcc}. In Linera each chain moves to a new epoch with one of its own blocks, so epochs overlap and an old committee serves until its epoch is removed~\cite{linera2026docs,linera2026execution}. RAI keeps every certificate the old epoch can still complete, needs no members in common, and moves all accounts at once, with no owner acting.

\para{State machines and view change.}
Replicated state machines reconfigure through their log: a configuration chosen by command $i$ governs commands from $i+\alpha$ on~\cite{lamport2010reconfiguring}, Stoppable Paxos stops one configuration before the next~\cite{lamport2008stoppable}, Vertical Paxos switches through an auxiliary master~\cite{lamport2009vertical}, Raft passes through a joint configuration~\cite{ongaro2014raft}, BFT-SMaRt orders reconfiguration requests~\cite{bessani2014bftsmart}, and in PaLa both committees vote during a handover~\cite{chan2018pala}. RAI has no log to cut; its lag of two epochs between deciding a committee and using it plays the role of $\alpha$. Its resilience $N=3f+2p+1$ is that of SBFT and Kudzu~\cite{gueta2019sbft,kudzu}, and its checkpoint is a view change run per account position: a notarization lock re-proposes a prepared block, and a recovery lock keeps one that may have committed on the fast path, found from $r=f+p+1$ of $N-f$ reports as SBFT's new primary finds it~\cite{gueta2019sbft}. Unlike a view change, it has no leader, one committee decides it while the next installs it, and the next epoch votes before it completes.

\para{Building blocks.}
Agreement with external validity is a standard interface~\cite{externalvalidity}, instantiated here in the style of Simplex and Kudzu~\cite{simplex,kudzu}; set reconciliation is mature~\cite{iblt,whatsthedifference,rateless}; and model checking small configurations of Byzantine protocols with TLC is common practice~\cite{tla}.
