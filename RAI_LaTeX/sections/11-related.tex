\para{Payments without consensus.}
Asset transfer needs no consensus~\cite{consensusnumber}. FastPay settles prefunded payments without a global order~\cite{fastpay}, Astro and Carbon build payments on reliable broadcast~\cite{astro,camaioni2024carbon}, Zef adds private coins~\cite{zef}, and Nano uses account chains with open representative voting~\cite{nano}. Table~\ref{tab:related} compares those that change validators.

\para{Handovers that pause.}
Sui Lutris proves safe a consensusless fast path whose handover waits for $2f+1$ validators to end the epoch, rolls back transactions no checkpoint contains, and only then frees objects that equivocation locked~\cite{lutris}. Deployed Sui now sends every transaction through Mysticeti, where the first of two conflicting ones takes the lock, and holds transactions a few hundred milliseconds at the boundary~\cite{suimysticetiv2,sui2026epochs,sui2026lifecycle}. Mysticeti-FPC pauses its fast path with an epoch-change bit and unlocks what equivocation blocked once the epoch ends~\cite{babel2025mysticeti}; Cuttlefish adds FastUnlock~\cite{cuttlefish}. Their pause is short because consensus already orders every transaction. RAI has no ordering layer and its close takes seconds, so it overlaps the close and, unable to drop both sides of a fork, keeps one (Section~\ref{sec:intro}).

\para{Handovers that fence or migrate.}
Carbon lets clients vote validators in and out and moves between views as dynamic Byzantine reliable broadcast does; once a new view is approved, operations addressed to the old one no longer commit~\cite{camaioni2024carbon,guerraoui2020dbrb}. Pastro and asynchronous Byzantine reconfiguration treat changing participation more generally~\cite{pastro,reconfiguration}, and DynaStore, FreeStore, SmartMerge, and reconfigurable lattice agreement change the replicas of a storage service without consensus~\cite{aguilera2011dynastore,alchieri2017freestore,jehl2015smartmerge,kuznetsov2019rla}. PDCC accepts payment certificates only for the installed configuration and assumes correct members common to consecutive configurations~\cite{pdcc}. In Linera each chain moves to a new epoch with one of its own blocks, so epochs overlap and an old committee serves until its epoch is removed~\cite{linera2026docs,linera2026execution}. RAI keeps every certificate the old epoch can still complete, needs no members in common, and moves all accounts at once, with no owner acting.

\para{State machines and view change.}
Replicated state machines reconfigure through their log: a configuration chosen by command $i$ governs commands from $i+\alpha$ on~\cite{lamport2010reconfiguring}, Stoppable Paxos stops one configuration before the next~\cite{lamport2008stoppable}, Vertical Paxos switches through an auxiliary master~\cite{lamport2009vertical}, Raft passes through a joint configuration~\cite{ongaro2014raft}, BFT-SMaRt orders reconfiguration requests~\cite{bessani2014bftsmart}, and in PaLa both committees vote during a handover~\cite{chan2018pala}. RAI has no log to cut; its lag of two epochs between deciding a committee and using it plays the role of $\alpha$. Its minimal committee $N=3f+2p+1$ is that of SBFT and Kudzu~\cite{gueta2019sbft,kudzu}, and its checkpoint is a view change run per account position: a notarization lock re-proposes a prepared block, and a recovery lock keeps one that may have committed on the fast path, found from $r=f+p+1$ of $N-f$ reports as SBFT's new primary finds it~\cite{gueta2019sbft}. Unlike a view change, it has no leader, one committee decides it while the next installs it, and the next epoch votes before it completes.

\para{Building blocks.} External-validity agreement~\cite{externalvalidity,simplex,kudzu}, set reconciliation~\cite{iblt,whatsthedifference,rateless}, and TLC~\cite{tla} are standard.
