\para{Prototype.}
We implemented RAI in RsNano~\cite{rsnano}, a Rust implementation of the Nano node~\cite{nano}. Account elections follow Section~\ref{sec:epoch} with the thresholds of Table~\ref{tab:thresholds} ($f=p=1$, $N=6$), late notarization, the early-vote rule, and the prefix-wide overlap rule; checkpoints carry origin-stamped locks and are built by the recipe of Section~\ref{sec:close}. Epochs end at fixed times $T_0+kD$ on every validator's local clock. Every first, final, late, and close-election vote, frozen report, decided checkpoint, and overlap certificate is written to its own LMDB environment and synced before a signature that depends on it leaves the process; a restarted validator reloads these records, refuses whatever they forbid, and fetches the checkpoints it missed. Set reconstruction uses rateless invertible Bloom lookup tables~\cite{rateless}: a validator that needs a report starts from its own view of the closing epoch and asks any holder for coded symbols until the symmetric difference peels, then checks the signed root. Agreement is one leader-based election per handover in the style of Kudzu~\cite{kudzu}: a validator votes for a proposal only after reconstructing its reports, checking the manifest, running the recipe, and matching the state digest, which gives eligibility; leaders rotate, and a round whose leader is silent times out after 2\,s. The supplement lists the prototype's departures from the protocol and the defects the measurements found.

\para{What we compare.}
Three systems run the same workload. \emph{RAI} is the protocol of this paper. \emph{Pause} is RAI with one switch flipped: no instance of epoch $e+1$ starts before $S_e$ is decided locally, so the overlap rule and late notarization never apply. It is the stop-and-drain transition that RAI set out to avoid, with everything else (reports, recipe, agreement, installation) identical. \emph{Nano} is the unmodified RsNano \texttt{develop} node (commit \texttt{ff2b32710}), which runs Nano's open representative voting: a block is confirmed once final votes reach 67\% of the online weight, after a round of non-final votes; it has no epochs, no checkpoints, and no committee, and since final votes cannot be withdrawn, a fork whose votes split leaves neither side able to confirm. All three are driven by the same load generator, built from the RAI branch.

\para{Setup.}
All validators run as separate processes on one MacBook Air (Apple M1, eight cores, 16\,GiB) over loopback. Six equal-weight representatives hold the voting weight. A load generator funds 45{,}000 accounts and then publishes 45{,}000 blocks at an offered load of 500 to 3{,}000 blocks/s, each on the frontier of a randomly chosen account: it builds on a confirmed frontier, or extends an unconfirmed non-fork frontier with a fresh child once it has waited 5\,s, and it never builds on a position where it published a fork. With forks enabled it publishes a conflicting sibling for 5\% or 10\% of the blocks, each sibling to half of the validators. The generator's choices are seeded identically for the three systems at each load. RAI and pause use 8\,s epochs and sync every signing record before releasing it. Only non-fork blocks count: \emph{latency} runs from a block's first publication to its confirmation at one validator, and \emph{throughput} is sustained, 99\% of the non-fork blocks over the time until they were confirmed, so that the last stragglers show in the 99th percentile rather than in the rate. Loads of 1{,}500 blocks/s and above ran three times and report the median; lower loads ran once. A block is \emph{published during a close} if some validator had left its epoch and not yet seen the checkpoint decided.

\para{Throughput and latency (Figure~\ref{fig:eval-sweep}).}
Without forks, all three systems sustain the offered load up to 2{,}000 blocks/s (1{,}856/s each at 2{,}000). RAI has the lowest median, 97 to 158\,ms against Nano's 179 to 188\,ms: an uncontested block finalizes on one round of five settled first votes, where Nano always needs a round of final votes. Its 95th percentile, 185 to 489\,ms, lies between Nano's 215 to 225\,ms and pause's 681 to 1{,}002\,ms. Beyond 2{,}000 blocks/s the six validators saturate the host; Nano, with no closes, degrades last (2{,}621/s at 3{,}000 offered, against 2{,}345 for RAI and 2{,}121 for pause). With forks the gap to pause widens: at 5\% forks and 2{,}000 blocks/s RAI sustains 1{,}764/s with a 95th percentile of 968\,ms, against 1{,}692/s and 2{,}974\,ms for pause; at 10\% forks and 2{,}000 blocks/s, 1{,}535/s and 4.5\,s against 1{,}291/s and 7.0\,s. At 5\% forks Nano's latency is unaffected, but at 10\% its throughput falls to 300 to 1{,}100 blocks/s and its 99th percentile to 14 to 49\,s, because blocks behind a split fork wait for elections that never complete. Pause's client never saw every non-fork block confirmed at 2{,}500 blocks/s with forks in any run; RAI completed every run.

\para{During closes (Figure~\ref{fig:eval-closes}).}
The difference comes from the closes. Without forks, blocks published during a close have a 95th percentile of 251 to 615\,ms under RAI up to 2{,}000 blocks/s and 1.0 to 1.8\,s under pause; outside closes RAI's is equal or lower (125 to 351\,ms against 128 to 461\,ms). RAI's closes are longer, because they share the host with voting: their median grows from 1.7\,s at 500 blocks/s to 7.6\,s at 2{,}000, so that up to 39\% of RAI's blocks are published during one, against 8 to 11\% for pause, whose closes take 1.4\,s with voting stopped. RAI keeps confirming through them; pause does not confirm at all until the checkpoint is decided.

\para{A Byzantine leader for every close (Table~\ref{tab:eval-byz}).}
With one Byzantine validator that leads the first round of every close and stays silent in it, every close in every run was decided in round 1, after the round-0 timeout. Pause stops for that timeout at every boundary: its 95th percentile is 3.3 to 7.9\,s without forks, against 226 to 1{,}065\,ms for RAI, and at 5\% forks 4.3 to 10.1\,s against 268 to 1{,}751\,ms. Nano has no closes and is unaffected by the leader.

\para{Forks left unresolved (Table~\ref{tab:eval-forks}).}
The generator never builds on a forked position, so what is left open measures what the validators resolve on their own. In these runs the nodes keep running for up to 90\,s, about eleven epochs, after the run settles. RAI left no position open at 1{,}000 blocks/s and at 5\% forks and 2{,}000; at 10\% forks and 2{,}000 blocks/s, where the host is saturated and few epochs are good, 65 of 4{,}469 were still open, down from 3{,}061 when the run settled. Every validator held the same positions open. Nano left 244 to 738 positions open, and at 10\% forks and 2{,}000 blocks/s its validators still disagreed on which. Pause resolved everything except at 10\% forks and 2{,}000 blocks/s, where 1{,}364 positions stayed open throughout the grace.

\para{Restarts, committee changes, and handoff.}
A second harness kills validators mid-load or as a close starts, restarts them on their data, and checks every logged signature for conflicts. Restarted validators rejoined by checkpoint catch-up. With eight validators of which six form the committee, replacing two members, replacing them with a Byzantine member in every committee, and replacing every correct member of a four-member committee all settled with identical checkpoints everywhere, and a departing member killed when its last close starts and kept down 30\,s left every checkpoint decided identically. No run produced a conflicting signature. Synced signing costs about 130\,ms at the median and nothing in goodput. The supplement reports these runs in detail. Under load, a committee change in every epoch is beyond this host: with eight node processes, rotating runs at 500 blocks/s completed (RAI's 95th percentile 227\,ms, against 213\,ms with a static committee), but at 1{,}000 blocks/s and above a rotated-in validator fell behind its peers in most runs of both RAI and pause, and the runs did not settle in time.

\para{Threats to validity.}
All validators share one host, so every system saturates at 2{,}000 to 3{,}000 blocks/s, and at saturation RAI's close competes with voting for the same cores; on separate machines both saturate later, but RAI still does more work per block (late notarizations and overlap evidence), so its peak throughput may stay at or below pause's. The network is loopback. One to three runs per point make the spreads indicative, not statistical; the Byzantine validator votes at random apart from leading every close; and process kills test crash consistency, not power loss.
