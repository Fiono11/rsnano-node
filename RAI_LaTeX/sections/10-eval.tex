\para{Prototype.}
We implemented RAI in RsNano~\cite{rsnano}, a Rust implementation of the Nano node~\cite{nano}. Account elections follow Section~\ref{sec:account} with the equal-weight thresholds of Table~\ref{tab:thresholds} ($f=p=1$, $N=6$), late notarization, the early-vote rule, and the prefix-wide overlap rule; checkpoints carry origin-stamped records and are built by Algorithm~\ref{alg:build} with Rule~3 promotion and matching-origin discharge. Epochs end at fixed times $T_0+kD$ on every validator's local clock. Every first vote, final vote, late notarization, left epoch, frozen report, and overlap certificate is written to an LMDB environment of its own before its signature leaves the process, and a restarted node reloads these records and refuses whatever they forbid. Late notarizations open no account instance at their receivers and are kept apart from the vote records that reports are built from; a manifest carries them, with the settled bit of every first vote, as bit sets over the committee. A position whose first votes are already split so that no block can reach $q$ is not re-voted at the boundary.

\para{Convergence by rateless set reconciliation.}
A report's two committed sets are reconstructed with rateless invertible Bloom lookup tables~\cite{iblt,whatsthedifference,rateless}. Each set element is a fixed 105-byte item naming account, height, hash, parent, and tag. A validator that needs a report starts from its own live projection of the closing epoch, which usually differs from the reporter's frozen set in a few entries, and asks any holder of the reconstructed set for coded symbols in growing batches; peeling them against the local projection yields the symmetric difference, which the requester applies and checks against the signed root (C1). It then fetches, by hash, the signed votes behind every $\tagN$ and $\tagF$ entry and every residual record it does not already hold.
\para{Consensus by a Kudzu-style election.}
The closing committee decides the checkpoint with one leader-based voting instance that adapts Kudzu's voting construction~\cite{kudzu}: first votes, second-look notarization support, timeout votes, and certificates with thresholds $q$ (normal), $N-p$ (fast), and $q$ timeout votes to skip a slot. Election votes and account votes are separate domains, and every election signature binds the handoff context, the deciding committee, the slot, and the proposal hash or timeout value. Four points differ from Kudzu and are what makes the instance provide A1--A4. There is one instance per handoff, not a chain: only a child of the genesis slot may introduce a fresh payload $(Q,\mu_e,d_e)$ and a proposal on a certified parent copies its parent's payload, which with Kudzu's exclusion of a conflicting certificate in a decided slot gives A1. Validity is RAI's eligibility: a validator first-votes a proposal only after reconstructing its reports, checking every manifest claim, running $\Build$, and matching $d_e$ (A2), so a final quorum contains correct validators that validated the fixed evidence, which with context binding gives A3. The outgoing committee decides and the successor only verifies the decision proof. Kudzu's erasure-coded payload distribution is not used and its communication bound is not claimed. Leaders rotate in canonical key order and propose the candidate built from the canonical prefix of usable reports; a validator whose own votes yield $\mu_e$ has checked that evidence already, and otherwise fetches the manifest from the proposer. A slot whose leader is offline or Byzantine times out and the next leader proposes. The timeout is local: a validator waits up to three times longer while it is still checking the leader's proposal, or while the leader is a usable reporter whose proposal has not arrived, since a validator that times out cannot vote for the proposal once checked; a later leader proposes again a payload it already validated, which validators that checked it accept at once. Under recurring prepared windows with $N-f$ responsive correct members and a local timeout satisfying $\Delta_E/(1+\rho)>\kappa_E+\tau_E+\delta$, where $\kappa_E$ bounds slot-entry skew and $\tau_E$ bounds proposal preparation and validation~\cite{dls}, Kudzu's progress argument gives A4.

\para{Departures from the protocol.}
The manifest commits to signed votes; blocks and ancestry are placed by the report items, and admission witnesses are not committed separately. The close election's own votes are not persisted. Retained vote records keep a cap of four epochs plus whatever may still discharge a surviving record. A first vote names its base by one signed bit, settled or early, since $S_{e-1}$ is unique. The restart path is exercised by unit tests only. The supplementary material lists the defects found while measuring.

\para{Baseline and setup.}
The baseline is unmodified RsNano (\texttt{develop}), which runs Nano's open representative voting: a block is confirmed once final votes reach 67\% of the online weight, after a round of non-final votes; it has no epochs and no checkpoints, and since final votes cannot be withdrawn, a fork whose votes split can leave no side able to reach the quorum. All validators run as separate processes on one MacBook Air (Apple M1, eight cores, 16\,GiB) over loopback; LMDB runs without synchronous writes. Six equal-weight representatives hold the voting weight. A load generator funds 45{,}000 accounts and then publishes 45{,}000 blocks at an offered 2{,}000 blocks/s, each on the frontier of a randomly chosen account, following the owner policy of Section~\ref{sec:account}: it builds on a confirmed frontier or, once an unconfirmed non-fork frontier has waited 5\,s, extends it with a fresh child, and never extends a position where it published a fork. With forks enabled it publishes a conflicting sibling for 5\% or 10\% of the blocks, each sibling to half of the validators. An \emph{offline} validator runs no node but keeps its weight; a \emph{Byzantine} validator also runs no node, and the generator votes with its key at random, choosing any vote kind for recent blocks and contradicting itself. RAI uses 8\,s epochs. A run \emph{settles} when every running validator holds the same cemented state and, for RAI, has decided the same checkpoints; a position left retained by a split vote counts as settled when it is uncemented on every validator alike. We report non-fork blocks only: goodput is the number of non-fork blocks confirmed divided by the publishing-to-last-confirmation time, and latency runs from publication to confirmation at one validator. Each variant ran once per system on an otherwise idle machine. The baseline rows were taken in an earlier pass on the same host with unchanged baseline code; the RAI rows in one pass on the final prototype commit.

\para{Without forks.}
Table~\ref{tab:eval} shows both systems keeping up with the offered load. RAI's median latency is 112 to 118\,ms against 189 to 207\,ms: an uncontested block finalizes on one round of $N-p$ settled first votes, where the baseline needs a round of final votes after a quorum of non-final ones. With one validator offline or Byzantine RAI's tails are close to the baseline's (p95 265 to 281\,ms against 234 to 238); with all six online its p95 of 394\,ms came from one close that took 4.5\,s, during which blocks one supporter short of a witness waited. Closes otherwise took 1.0 to 2.7\,s in the first election round, or 3.7 to 4.0\,s when leader rotation reached the missing validator and a second round was needed. In every no-fork variant 89 to 98\% of the blocks late-notarized during a close reached a closing-epoch exclusion witness and finalized before the checkpoint.

\para{With forks.}
The baseline finished none of the six fork variants: forks whose votes split stay unresolved and occupy election slots, which delays the non-fork blocks queued behind them. RAI settled all six with every running validator deciding the same checkpoints, three to six of them, since a close that outlasts the epoch defers the next boundary. At 5\% forks the median rose to 162 to 225\,ms and p95 to 1.0 to 1.2\,s, and the longest close to 8.2 to 9.0\,s. Most forked positions were resolved by the time the runs settled, about a third of them by the sibling, each split decided by a matching-origin discharge or by the continuation of the retained block in the next epoch; with one validator offline, 1{,}433 positions at 5\% forks and 2{,}920 at 10\% stayed retained, identically on every validator. At 10\% forks the medians reached 406 to 618\,ms and p95 2.7 to 3.6\,s. Forks lengthen the closes, since reports, manifests, and discharge witnesses grow with the retained positions: the longest close took 10\,s with all validators correct and 15\,s with one offline or Byzantine, where a round led by the missing validator costs the timeout and, with a Byzantine member, every proposal carries a manifest the validators must fetch and check; with the timeout rules above no correct-led round failed. Late-notarized blocks reached their closing-epoch witness in 86 to 89\% of cases at 5\% forks and 81 to 83\% at 10\%; the rest wait for the checkpoint.

\para{Limitations.}
Each variant ran once. An earlier pass the same day without the timeout rules gave the same medians within noise and, for 10\% forks with a Byzantine validator, closes of 17 to 21\,s with up to four rounds, p95 3.8 to 5.8\,s, and goodput 1{,}050 to 1{,}086 blocks/s over five runs; whether split positions end up resolved or retained varied between passes. All validators share one host, so CPU contention rather than network latency sets the delays. Committees are identical across epochs in this matrix; the supplementary material reports a run with per-epoch reweighting but no change of identities. The departures above mean these results validate neither the consensus instantiation's durability nor a manifest covering admission witnesses.

\section{Conclusion}
RAI makes epoch transitions for account-local payments pipelined and safe: the next epoch's voting starts at each validator's local boundary while the previous epoch closes, immutable reports preserve latent and late finality, a deterministic construction produces a checkpoint with origin-stamped locks, and a predecessor gate with a prefix-wide overlap exception, fed by late notarizations and guarded by the early-vote rule, keeps successor finality compatible with whatever the outgoing committee could still certify. Safety and termination are relative to two abstract services, and model checking found the three failures that written arguments had missed. A prototype settles all nine workload variants with non-fork median latency near 100\,ms; a missing member lengthens the closes.
