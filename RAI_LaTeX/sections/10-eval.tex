\section{Implementation and Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}
\input{sections/eval-figures}
The evaluation asks three questions. Does RAI resolve the forks Nano leaves open, and what does that cost in confirmation rate and latency? Does it keep confirming payments while an epoch closes, where a stop-and-drain transition stops, also when a validator is Byzantine? Does a committee change disturb it?

\para{Prototype.}
We implemented RAI in RsNano~\cite{rsnano}, a Rust implementation of the Nano node~\cite{nano}. Account elections follow Section~\ref{sec:epoch}, with late notarization, the early-vote rule, and the prefix-wide overlap rule; checkpoints carry origin-stamped locks and are built by the recipe of Section~\ref{sec:close}. Epochs end at fixed times $T_0+kD$ on every validator's local clock. The content of every vote, the first votes behind every final vote, and every frozen report, decided checkpoint, and overlap certificate are synced to disk before a signature that depends on them leaves the process, so a restarted validator refuses whatever its earlier signatures forbid, reports what it notarized, and signs its recorded votes again to serve them as evidence. Set reconstruction uses rateless invertible Bloom lookup tables~\cite{rateless}. Agreement is one leader-based election per handover in the style of Kudzu~\cite{kudzu}: a validator votes for a proposal only after reconstructing its reports, running the recipe, and matching the state digest; leaders rotate, and a round whose leader stays silent times out after 2\,s. A validator does not repeat a request for votes before its reply could arrive: the floor is set to the longest measured round trip. The supplement lists where the prototype departs from the protocol.

\para{What we compare.}
\emph{RAI} is the protocol of this paper with $f=p=1$ on six members: a certificate needs four of six, and the fast path five settled first votes. \emph{Pause} is the same code with one switch flipped: no instance of epoch $e+1$ starts before $S_e$ is decided locally. It is the stop-and-drain transition RAI set out to avoid, with reports, recipe, agreement, and installation unchanged. \emph{Nano} is the RsNano \texttt{develop} node (commit \texttt{ff2b32710}) with one optimization of RAI's prototype ported to it, checking a fork block's signature once: a block is confirmed once final votes reach 67\% of the online weight, five of six here, after a round of non-final votes. It has no epochs and no committee, and since final votes cannot be withdrawn, a fork whose votes split leaves neither side able to confirm. RAI and Nano both tolerate one Byzantine validator, but RAI's normal path needs fewer votes.

\para{Setup.}
Six equal-weight validators run on separate Google Cloud VMs (n2-standard-4, four vCPUs), one each in Warsaw, Iowa, Montr\'eal, Las Vegas, Taiwan, and Melbourne, with round trips from about 30 to over 300\,ms. A load generator on a seventh VM in Warsaw funds 45{,}000 accounts and then publishes 45{,}000 blocks, each on the frontier of a randomly chosen account, with the same random choices for the three systems. It offers 2{,}000 blocks/s without a Byzantine validator and 1{,}000 blocks/s with one, the highest rate at which we saw RAI settle with an equivocating validator across these links. In the fork scenarios it publishes a conflicting sibling for 5\% or 10\% of the blocks, each sibling to half of the validators, and never builds on a forked position, so what is left open measures what the validators resolve on their own. In the Byzantine scenarios one validator runs a modified node: it votes for both blocks of every fork, signs two versions of its report and sends each to half of the validators, and leads the first round of every close with two different proposals. Nano's Byzantine validator votes for both blocks of every fork, the one attack Nano has a phase for. RAI and pause use 8\,s epochs, so a close starts about every 8\,s during the load. The \emph{confirmation rate} counts the non-fork blocks the client sees confirmed per second over the whole run, so it stays below the offered load. \emph{Latency} runs from a block's first publication to its confirmation at the Warsaw validator and counts non-fork blocks only. After the load, the validators keep running until no block is open on any of them, or until none has become final for 45\,s or the harness's time limit is reached. Each cell ran three times; we report the median and the range.

\para{Latency and confirmation rate (Figure~\ref{fig:eval-matrix}).}
Without forks RAI has a median of 381\,ms and a 95th percentile of 461\,ms against Nano's 538 and 594\,ms, and confirms 1{,}768 against 1{,}608 blocks/s. Nano needs five of six votes in both of its rounds, so it waits for a distant validator twice; RAI's normal path waits for four, and 27 to 65\% of its finalizations, depending on the validator and run, take the one-round fast path. With forks RAI's median stays at 399 and 400\,ms and its 95th percentile at 511 and 566\,ms, at 1{,}654 and 1{,}518 blocks/s. Nano keeps its median (541 and 534\,ms), but with 10\% forks its 95th percentile rises to 3.3\,s and it confirms 816 blocks/s (491 to 897). With a Byzantine validator at 1{,}000 blocks/s, RAI's median is 386 to 393\,ms and its 95th percentile 463 to 935\,ms, at 826 to 944 blocks/s; Nano's are 534 to 537\,ms and 593 to 647\,ms, at 716 to 906 blocks/s. Pause is far behind both: its median is 0.5 to 2.9\,s and its 95th percentile 4.4 to 13.6\,s, ten to sixteen times RAI's in every cell, and RAI confirms 12 to 43\% more blocks per second.

\para{RAI resolves forks (Figure~\ref{fig:eval-forks}).}
With forks, RAI finalized every open block within 20 to 27\,s of the end of the load without a Byzantine validator and within 17 to 29\,s with one; pause took 10 to 24\,s and 10 to 34\,s. In all of these runs every validator cemented the same state. RAI is often the slower of the two here; one reason is that its recipe counts settled first votes only, so a fork whose first votes all came before the previous checkpoint was installed waits one more epoch. Nano did not finalize them all. When the harness stopped waiting it had 36 to 1{,}020 blocks open on a validator without a Byzantine validator and 19 to 1{,}174 with one. Its validators held the same blocks in all 12 runs with forks, but in 6 of them had cemented different numbers of them.

\para{RAI keeps confirming while an epoch closes (Figure~\ref{fig:eval-timeline}).}
Pause holds new blocks while an epoch closes and then confirms them in a burst. With 10\% forks and a Byzantine validator it had 22 to 29 seconds without a single confirmation during the 45\,s load, up to 10 in a row, and averaged 442 to 549 blocks/s while an epoch closed. RAI, on the same load, had one or two such seconds, never two in a row, and averaged 785 to 892 blocks/s while an epoch closed. Without a Byzantine validator RAI had no such second and averaged 1{,}522 to 1{,}962 blocks/s during closes at 2{,}000 offered; pause had 7 to 10 and averaged 162 to 967. RAI's closes are not short: with 10\% forks and a Byzantine validator the longest of each run takes 7.6 to 8.0\,s, about the length of an epoch, since a close must wait for the reports of all five honest validators (pause: 14.5 to 18.9\,s). But no block waits for them. Each validator finds 14{,}000 to 25{,}000 of the 45{,}000 blocks eligible for the overlap rule.

\para{A committee change (Figure~\ref{fig:eval-rotation}).}
These runs are on one host: six processes on a MacBook Air (Apple M1, eight cores) over loopback, 48{,}000 blocks at 2{,}000 blocks/s, so the delays are dominated by CPU contention and the numbers do not compare with the cells above. With four seats ($f=1$, $p=0$), two members hand their weight to two standby validators in epoch~0, so the committee derived from epoch~0's state, the one epoch~2 runs, replaces both. RAI keeps confirming through the handover. Across the three scenarios it confirms 1{,}582 to 1{,}836 blocks/s with medians of 206 to 396\,ms, and resolves every fork. Pause's 95th percentile is 2.9, 1.8, and 1.1 times RAI's without forks, with 5\%, and with 10\% forks; Nano leaves forks open (687 to 1{,}135 blocks with 5\%) and confirms 528 blocks/s with 10\%. With a Byzantine member that keeps its seat through the change, RAI again resolves every fork; its median is 242 to 1{,}200\,ms against pause's 1.3 to 2.3\,s and its 95th percentile 0.7 to 5.1\,s against 4.9 to 6.9\,s. RAI confirms slightly less than pause without forks (1{,}762 against 1{,}814 blocks/s) and more with 5\% and 10\% forks (1{,}650 and 1{,}436 against 1{,}545 and 1{,}209).

\para{Restarts and handoff.}
A second harness, on the same host, kills validators mid-load or as a close starts, restarts them on their data, and checks every logged signature for conflicts. Restarted validators rejoined by checkpoint catch-up, and a departing member killed when its last close starts and kept down 30\,s left every checkpoint decided identically. No run produced a conflicting signature. Signing recorded votes again and restoring notarizations after a restart are covered by unit tests and five further restart runs on this host (no conflicting signature, every fork resolved, every notarization restored), not by the runs above; on this host, writing those notarizations changed neither confirmation rate nor latency beyond the spread between runs. Synced signing costs about 130\,ms at the median and nothing in confirmation rate. The supplement reports these runs.

\para{Threats to validity.}
RAI's normal path needs four votes and Nano's five, so part of RAI's lower latency comes from the smaller quorum, not from the protocol's other rules. Latency is measured at one validator, beside the load generator; a client elsewhere would see other numbers. The cells with a Byzantine validator run at half the rate of the others and do not compare with them. Across these links RAI did not settle with an equivocating validator at 1{,}500 blocks/s with 10\% forks or at 2{,}000 with 5\%: equivocation keeps thousands of forks contested until a checkpoint decides them, the evidence traffic overflows the queues of the slowest validator, and each close waits for that validator's report. The committee change and the restarts were not run across regions, and the six-region runs did not audit signatures for conflicts. Three runs per cell bound the spread only roughly, and process kills test crash consistency, not power loss.
