\section{Correctness}
\label{sec:correctness}
The arguments concern the core protocol, including the prefix-wide overlap guard, late notarization, the early-vote rule, origin-stamped records, and Rule~3 promotion, and are relative to the services of Section~\ref{sec:services}: safety uses C1 and A1--A3, termination additionally C2 and A4. Cryptographic guarantees hold except with negligible probability. We state the results with the arguments that carry the weight; the supplementary material has the full proofs.

\subsection{Safety}
\begin{lemma}[Account exclusion]
\label{lem:exclusion}
In one account instance at most one block has an exclusion witness; in particular at most one has an $\NC$. If an $\FC$ or $\FF$ for $B$ can be formed in that instance, no conflicting block has an exclusion witness. If an $\FF$ for $B$ can be formed, every conflicting block has at most $f+p<r$ supporters.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Two sets of $q$ supporters intersect in $2q-N=f+1$ identities, one correct, and a correct validator supports one block per instance (Definition~\ref{def:support}). A correct final voter first-voted the same block, so an $\FC$ intersects a rival's $\XW$ the same way; an $\FF$ has $N-p\ge q$ first voters, and with $f'\le f$ actual Byzantine members at least $N-p-f'$ correct ones, leaving at most $p+f'$ supporters for a rival.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Late notarization adds no old finality]
\label{lem:late}
Every epoch-$e$ $\NC$, $\FC$, or $\FF$ consists of first and final votes released by correct signers before they left epoch $e$, together with Byzantine votes. A correct validator releases a final vote, or records an $\tagN$ entry, only for a block for which it holds an $\NC$ of $q$ first votes.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Frozen-report exposure]
\label{lem:exposure}
Let $i$ be a correct reporter for closure $e$. If $i$ released an epoch-$e$ final vote for $B$, then $T_i$ tags $B$ with $\tagN$ or $\tagF$. If $i$ released an epoch-$e$ first vote for $B$, then $T_i$ tags $B$ with $\tagN$ or $\tagF$, or $h(B)\in G_i$, whatever tag $B$ inherits from $S_{e-1}$. Both persist after the report is frozen.
\end{lemma}
\begin{lemma}[Signers hold the witness]
\label{lem:holds}
Every correct signer of an eligible epoch-$e$ $\FC$ or $\FF$ for $B$ holds, as retained evidence, an exclusion witness discharging each recovery record of $S_{e-1}$ on a block that conflicts with $B$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An $\FF$ consists of settled first votes, whose correct signers had installed $S_{e-1}$ and were refused a rival of a retained block unless every record on it was discharged. A correct final voter either voted after installing $S_{e-1}$, with the same check, or before, holding $\XW_{e-1}(B)$ by the early-vote rule, which discharges every record closure $e-1$ created; the records $S_{e-1}$ inherited were known to the voter, and the overlap guard required their matching-origin witnesses.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Closure invariant]
\label{def:invariant}
A closed state $S_e$ satisfies the closure invariant if, for every epoch $e'\le e$ and every block $B$ with an eligible epoch-$e'$ account final certificate that can be formed from released signatures, $S_e$ holds $B$ (i)~as final, (ii)~under a notarization lock, or (iii)~under a recovery record of origin $e'$, directly or as an ancestor of a block so held.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Closure preserves latent and late finality]
\label{thm:closure}
Let $S_{e-1}$ be a valid closed predecessor satisfying the closure invariant, and let $v$ be any candidate with $\Elig_e(v)$ and state $S_e$. If an eligible epoch-$e$ account final certificate for $B$ can be formed, possibly completed after reports froze or after the checkpoint was decided, then $S_e$ holds $B$ as in (i)--(iii) with $e'=e$, no admissible evidence discharges that hold, and $S_e$ satisfies the closure invariant.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $Q$ be the selected reporters and $A$ the certificate's signers. If $B$ conflicts with a carried record, each correct signer holds the discharging witness (Lemma~\ref{lem:holds}); it is retained evidence, the manifest contains it, and line~6 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:build} discharges the record, so $B$ is eligible for Rules~1--2 whenever a correct signer is selected. For a normal certificate, $|(Q\cap A)\setminus\Byz|\ge q-2f=p+1$, and each such reporter froze an $\tagN$ or $\tagF$ entry for $B$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:exposure}): a verified $\tagF$ entry gives (i), otherwise $B$ is the unique represented $\NC$ target at its position (Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}) and Rule~1 gives (ii). For a fast certificate, $|(Q\cap A)\setminus\Byz|\ge N-p-2f=r$; if none of these reporters has an $\tagN$ or $\tagF$ entry for $B$, each has $h(B)\in G_i$ with a verified first vote, whether or not $T_i$ tags $B$ with $\tagR$, so $|U_Q(B)|\ge r$, while every rival has fewer than $r$ supporters and no represented $\NC$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}); Rule~2 gives (iii). A record of origin $e$ on $B$ is discharged only by an $\XW_e$ of a rival, excluded by Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}; holds of earlier origins are inherited, since dropping one needs a conflicting final block or an $\XW_{e'}$ of a rival, both excluded. Only $A\cap Q$ is used, so it does not matter when the certificate is assembled.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Checkpoint-promotion safety]
\label{lem:promotion}
A Rule~3 promoted block cannot conflict with a block that has a closing-epoch exclusion witness, $\FC$, or $\FF$; a decision that finalizes the promoted prefix is compatible with old-domain and core overlap finality.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Acyclic final dependencies]
\label{lem:dag}
For any finite set of valid final blocks closed under account ancestors and required finalized sends, parent and send-to-receive edges form a directed acyclic graph.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Overlap verdict independent of installation order]
\label{lem:verdict}
Let closure $e$ be in progress, let $B$ and $B'$ conflict at one position, and let $\OC$ be an overlap certificate for $B$: an $\XW_e(B)$, an $\NC_{e+1}(B)$, an epoch-$(e+1)$ $\FC$ or $\FF$ for $B$ or a descendant selecting it, and a matching-origin exclusion witness for every recovery record on $B'$ in $S_{e-1}$, with no notarization lock on $B'$ there. Then every candidate with $\Elig_e$ and state $S_e$ (a)~does not finalize $B'$, (b)~carries no notarization lock on $B'$, and (c)~carries on $B'$ only recovery records discharged by witnesses in $\OC$. Hence a validator holding $\OC$ accepts $B$ before installing $S_e$ if and only if it accepts $B$ by rechecking after, and a later manifest containing $\OC$ verifies an $\tagF$ claim for $B$ against $S_e$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
An epoch-$e$ certificate for $B'$, or the $\NC_e(B')$ that Rule~1 or Rule~3 needs, is excluded by $\XW_e(B)$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}; inherited finality or a notarization lock on $B'$ contradicts the check of $\OC$ against $S_{e-1}$. A record on $B'$ in $S_e$ has origin $e$, discharged by $\XW_e(B)\subseteq\OC$, or is inherited, discharged by hypothesis. Rechecking after installation tests exactly these conditions, as does line~6 at closure $e+1$. The prefix-wide guard applies the lemma at the first divergent unresolved position.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Deterministic construction]
\label{lem:construction}
If the predecessor satisfies the closure invariant then, for fixed usable selected reports and a valid immutable manifest, $\Build$ returns one canonical candidate state at every correct validator, preserving inherited finality and all mandatory surviving records and applying only explicit account finality plus Rule~3 promotion. All-correct usable inputs yield a finite valid construction.
\end{lemma}
The proof uses C1 for identical inputs, compares records as sets of (block, strength, origin), and relies on Lemma~\ref{lem:verdict} for line~6 to accept a correct reporter's $\tagF$ claim for an overlap-final block.

\begin{theorem}[Transfer safety across epochs]
\label{thm:safety}
Assuming C1 and A1--A3, finalized histories at correct validators are compatible and irreversible across all core-protocol epochs; finalized balances are nonnegative, and each send is received at most once.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Induct from genesis. Within an epoch, Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion} excludes incompatible account finality. At a boundary, A2 makes the decided $v_e$ eligible, so Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} preserves every formable old final branch and Lemma~\ref{lem:promotion} makes its promotion compatible; A1 and A3 make every correct validator install the same $v_e$, hence by Lemma~\ref{lem:construction} the same state. For overlap, the first divergent unresolved position on an early successor-final prefix has a closing-epoch exclusion witness for the successor block, whose late notarizations add no old finality (Lemma~\ref{lem:late}); a conflicting closing-epoch certificate or Rule~3 promotion would need a conflicting $\NC$, contradicting Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}; and by Lemma~\ref{lem:verdict} correct validators holding the same overlap certificate reach the same verdict before or after installing $S_e$, so they agree on which overlap blocks are final. The base/overlay union with applied-operation identifiers prevents rollback and duplicate application.
\end{proof}

The proof uses nothing about how the consensus service decides, since Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} is universal over eligible candidates.

\subsection{Conditional termination}
\begin{lemma}[Correct reports become usable]
\label{lem:reports-live}
Under the data service, finite relevant admitted work, fair processing, and C2, every correct report eventually becomes usable at every correct validator.
\end{lemma}
A correct report is finite and well formed (Lemma~\ref{lem:late}); C2 reconstructs it and the data service delivers the evidence behind its claims. A Byzantine reporter can make only its own report unusable.

\begin{theorem}[Handoff termination]
\label{thm:handoff-live}
Assume the data service, fair processing, C2, A4, and recurring prepared windows with $N-f$ responsive correct members of $K_e$ long enough for reconstruction, candidate preparation, and the consensus service's rounds. Every enabled handoff is eventually decided and, with eventual retained-data delivery, installed by correct successors.
\end{theorem}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:reports-live} every correct member constructs an eligible candidate (Lemma~\ref{lem:construction}), A4 decides one, A3 lets successors verify it, and retained data let them install it.

\begin{theorem}[Uncontested account continuation]
\label{thm:account-live}
Assume handoff termination, available final-send dependencies, and recurring common-open ready intervals. An owner following the retry-or-fresh-child policy of Section~\ref{sec:account} eventually finalizes each target of a finite valid uncontested continuation, provided each required new position has such an interval, and the finalized targets eventually enter a checkpoint.
\end{theorem}
In a ready interval, $N-f\ge q$ correct first votes create an $\NC$ and the same validators final-vote; an attempt interrupted by a boundary is preserved by Theorem~\ref{thm:closure}, an omitted block can be retried, and a remaining retained fork can be selected by a fresh uncontested child. Normal finality takes three delivery stages after broadcast and an $N-p$ settled first-vote quorum two; a sufficient account interval budget is $D/(1+\rho)>\kappa_A+R_A+3\delta$, where $\kappa_A$ bounds opening skew and $R_A$ readiness after the last opening, including predecessor closure. These are conditional communication bounds, not measured latency.
