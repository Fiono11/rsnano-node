\section{Overview}
\label{sec:overview}
\subsection{The pipeline}
Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} shows one epoch transition. Account voting for epoch $e$ is done by committee $K_e$. At its local boundary a validator stops signing epoch-$e$ account votes, freezes its report, and opens epoch $e+1$ for the same accounts. The checkpoint $S_e$ is decided by the outgoing committee from $N-f$ reports, while epoch $e+1$ voting is already under way. The successor committee verifies and installs $S_e$; only then does ordinary epoch-$(e+1)$ work become final. Committees are lagged by two epochs: $S_e$ selects the committee that votes in epoch $e+2$, so the committee of $e+1$ is known throughout the transition and can prepare without waiting.

Three things must hold for this pipeline to be safe. Old-epoch finality that can still be formed must survive in $S_e$: the report rules and the construction of Section~\ref{sec:transitions} guarantee it. New-epoch work must not finalize anything the old epoch could still contradict: the predecessor gate holds ordinary successor work provisional until $S_e$ is known, and the overlap exception finalizes early only where old-domain exclusion is proven at every position. The outgoing committee supplies that proof for fresh blocks by \emph{late notarization}: until it installs $S_e$, each member also notarizes in epoch $e$ the blocks it first-votes in epoch $e+1$, with votes that count toward no finality. And every correct validator must install the same $S_e$: this is where the consensus service enters, and it decides only among candidates that RAI's eligibility predicate accepts.

\subsection{A six-validator example}
Let $N=6$, $f=p=1$, so notarization and normal finality require four votes, fast finality requires five first votes, and recovery requires three reporter first votes. Validators $1,\ldots,5$ are correct and validator~6 is Byzantine. Validators $1,2,3,4,6$ first-vote for an eligible payment block $B$. Vote dissemination is delayed, so no correct reporter has assembled a notarization or final certificate when it freezes its report.

Suppose a checkpoint candidate selects the reports of $1,2,3,5,6$. Each of reporters $1,2,3$ commits $h(B)$ in its voted-but-not-ledger set $G_i$. Validator~6 may omit its vote; validator~5 did not vote for $B$. The three correct memberships meet the recovery threshold. The construction retains $B$ and its ancestry as a recovery-protected branch without changing balances. Later the five first votes form a fast final certificate, which finalizes the retained branch; a subsequent checkpoint exposes that finality explicitly. The example generalizes in two ways. Every selected report quorum intersects a normal final-vote quorum in at least $p+1$ correct signers, each of whom recorded notarization before final-voting. Every selected report quorum intersects a fast first-vote quorum in at least $f+p+1$ correct signers, whose obligations appear in their ledger set or in $G_i$. These are the two exposure cases of the closure theorem.

\subsection{What is abstract and what is not}
RAI fixes the report format, the tagged ledger projection, the eligibility of a report selection, the deterministic construction, the overlap and installation rules, and the lifecycle of epochs. It does not fix how a validator obtains another reporter's frozen sets, nor how the outgoing committee agrees on one eligible candidate. Section~\ref{sec:services} states what RAI needs from these two services; Section~\ref{sec:eval} describes the instantiation we measured.
