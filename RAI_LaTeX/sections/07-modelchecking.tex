\section{Model Checking the Handoff}
\label{sec:mc}
Three rules of Sections~\ref{sec:account} and~\ref{sec:transitions} replaced earlier rules that written arguments had accepted: the second committed set $G_i$ (Fix~B), discharge by an exclusion witness of matching origin with the overlap certificate, and the early-vote rule. We wrote three TLA\textsuperscript{+} models of the handoff and checked them with TLC. Each takes one account position with two conflicting blocks, $N=6$, $f=p=1$, five correct validators and one Byzantine validator whose signatures are available for every block, vote kind, and epoch and whose report is chosen adversarially subject to the usability checks. The consensus service is the nondeterministic choice of one eligible candidate; TLC explores all interleavings, with validators identified up to permutation. The models, configurations, and TLC outputs with the violating traces are in the supplementary material.

\texttt{RaiClose} has three voting epochs and two closures, no early voting, and $\Build$ with Rules~1--3, inherited records, and discharge; its invariants are transfer safety at the position, Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} without any hypothesis about witnesses, and one final block per checkpoint. \texttt{RaiOverlap} has one closing and one open epoch: validators cross their boundary one by one, first-vote early under the cross-epoch guard, late-notarize, finalize under the overlap exception or by rechecking after installation, and one closure builds $S_1$; its invariants add the same verdict before and after installation (Lemma~\ref{lem:verdict}), usability of every correct report (Lemma~\ref{lem:reports-live}), and admissibility of the discharge witness at the next closure, and three switches turn the repairs off. \texttt{RaiHandoff} combines early voting, two closures, and a changing committee, with the Byzantine validator in both committees; a discharge witness reaches the builder of $S_2$ only if a selected correct reporter relied on it, which models a withheld signature.

Table~\ref{tab:mc} gives the results. With the earlier $G_i=V_i^{(e)}\setminus\keys(T_i)$, a reporter that first-voted a block the predecessor carries under a recovery record tags it $\tagR$, the fresh vote appears nowhere, and two conflicting fast certificates are reached. Without discharge, two correct validators finalize different blocks depending on installation order; without late notarizations as evidence, the next closure cannot verify a correct $\tagF$ claim; with late notarizations counting toward an $\NC$, a correct report becomes unusable, which costs termination but not safety. With the fast path counting every first vote, Theorem~\ref{thm:closure} fails for the second closure with identical and with disjoint committees: four early votes for $B$ are eligible only through a witness that one unselected validator holds, so $S_2$ retains the rival, which a later epoch finalizes while $B$'s fast certificate can still be formed. With the repairs the invariants hold in every run, and probes confirm that a block can still finalize before $S_1$ is decided, that the second closure does discharge first-checkpoint records, and that a block can carry records of two origins.
