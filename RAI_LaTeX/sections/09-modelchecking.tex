\section{Model Checking the Handover}
\label{sec:mc}
Several rules of Sections~\ref{sec:close} and~\ref{sec:pipeline} guard against interleavings that are easy to miss in a written argument. We wrote TLA\textsuperscript{+} models of the handover and checked them with TLC, each with the full rule set and with switches that weaken one rule, to show that the rule is needed. Each takes one account, mostly with $N=6$, $f=p=1$, five correct validators and one Byzantine validator whose signatures are available for every block, vote kind, and epoch, and whose report is chosen by the adversary subject to the usability checks. Agreement is the nondeterministic choice of one eligible candidate; TLC explores every interleaving, with validators identified up to permutation. The models, configurations, and TLC outputs with the violating traces are online~\cite{extended}.

There are eight models: \emph{Close} (three epochs, locks of several origins), \emph{Overlap} (early votes, late notarization, overlap finality), \emph{Handoff} (a committee change), \emph{Prefix} (the prefix-wide guard on two positions), \emph{Recovery} (the owner extends a forked block), \emph{Resolve} (good epochs close a fork), \emph{Restart}, and \emph{Recover} (four epochs, up to three rivals).

Table~\ref{tab:rules} lists the counterexamples by rule; \cite{extended} lists every run and explains each violation. A recipe that counts all first votes and works bottom-up loses a fast-final child, and so does a bottom-up recipe on settled votes with a forking owner; the recipe of Section~\ref{sec:close} passes the safety, side-change, and recovery checks by simulation, and safety in every run of up to 17 steps, two more than the bottom-up order needs to fail. Probes show that the invariants are not vacuous; Resolve and Recover also check the early-vote fallback.

The models are small: one or two positions, two to four epochs, no receives, restarts in one model only, $N\in\{4,6\}$. Prefix, Recovery, and Resolve finish exhaustively only with $N=4$ ($p=0$, no fast path); with $N=6$ they ran as capped searches or simulation. Their abstractions only add behaviours, except the recover model's owner and Byzantine policies, so a violation in the model is a violation of the protocol, but a clean run is evidence, not proof.
