\section{Model Checking the Handover}
\label{sec:mc}
Several rules of Sections~\ref{sec:close} and~\ref{sec:pipeline} guard against interleavings that are easy to miss in a written argument. We wrote TLA\textsuperscript{+} models of the handover and checked them with TLC, each with the full rule set and with switches that weaken one rule, to show that the rule is needed. Each takes one account with $N=6$, $f=p=1$, five correct validators and one Byzantine validator whose signatures are available for every block, vote kind, and epoch, and whose report is chosen by the adversary subject to the usability checks. Agreement is the nondeterministic choice of one eligible candidate; TLC explores every interleaving, with validators identified up to permutation. The models, configurations, and TLC outputs with the violating traces are in the supplement and the artifact.

\begin{description}
\item[Close] three voting epochs and two checkpoints, no early voting; the recipe with locks of several origins and matching-origin release. Switch: a second set that omits fresh votes for blocks tagged inherited.
\item[Overlap] one closing and one open epoch; validators cross their boundary one by one, vote early, late-notarize, and finalize under the overlap rule or after installing. Switches turn off matching-origin release, late supports as evidence, and the separation of notarization from late supports.
\item[Handoff] early voting, two checkpoints, and a committee change with the Byzantine validator in both committees; the early-vote rule on or off; a releasing witness reaches the second checkpoint only through a selected correct report, which models a withheld signature.
\item[Prefix] two positions of one account: a parent with a rival at position 1 and two children at position 2, so that the prefix-wide guard of~\ref{r:overlap} is exercised; committees identical, disjoint, or sharing members. Switches check only the finalizing block instead of the prefix, and let a child inherit its parent's witness.
\item[Recovery] a child of each forked block at the next position, installation, and settled votes. Switch: any kept tip may be extended without a new notarization.
\item[Resolve] one forked position over three epochs and two checkpoints; a Byzantine validator that votes both sides or withholds; epochs flagged as good (synchronous) or not. Switch: the termination rules.
\item[Recover] four epochs, three checkpoints, two or three rivals with a child each, blocks replayed to anyone, an owner that forks or not. Switches: the recipe's vote count, keep rule, and order.
\end{description}

Table~\ref{tab:mc} summarizes; the supplement explains each violation. Extending any kept tip is unsafe even without early votes. A recipe that counts all first votes and works bottom-up loses a fast-final child, and so does a bottom-up recipe on settled votes with a forking owner; the recipe of Section~\ref{sec:close} passes the safety, side-change, and recovery checks by simulation, and safety in every run of up to 17 steps, two more than the bottom-up order needs to fail. Probes show that the invariants are not vacuous.

The models are small: one or two positions, two to four epochs, no receives, no restarts, $N\in\{4,6\}$. Their abstractions only add behaviours, except the recover model's owner and Byzantine policies, so a violation in the model is a violation of the protocol, but a clean run is evidence, not proof.
