\section{Model Checking the Handover}
\label{sec:mc}
Several rules of Sections~\ref{sec:close} and~\ref{sec:pipeline} guard against interleavings that are easy to miss in a written argument. We wrote TLA\textsuperscript{+} models of the handover and checked them with TLC, each with the full rule set and with switches that weaken one rule, to show that the rule is needed. Each takes one account with $N=6$, $f=p=1$, five correct validators and one Byzantine validator whose signatures are available for every block, vote kind, and epoch, and whose report is chosen by the adversary subject to the usability checks. Agreement is the nondeterministic choice of one eligible candidate; TLC explores every interleaving, with validators identified up to permutation. The models, configurations, and TLC outputs with the violating traces are in the supplement and the artifact.

There are eight models. \emph{Close} runs three epochs and two checkpoints with locks of several origins; \emph{Overlap} has validators cross their boundary one by one, vote early, late-notarize, and finalize under the overlap rule; \emph{Handoff} adds a committee change with the Byzantine validator in both committees; \emph{Prefix} exercises the prefix-wide guard of~\ref{r:overlap} on two positions, with identical, disjoint, or overlapping committees; \emph{Recovery} lets the owner extend a forked block; \emph{Resolve} checks that two good epochs close a fork; \emph{Restart} restarts validators before they freeze their report; and \emph{Recover} runs four epochs and three checkpoints with two or three rivals. Each has switches that weaken one rule.

Table~\ref{tab:rules} lists the counterexamples by rule; the supplement lists every run and explains each violation. Extending any kept tip is unsafe even without early votes. A recipe that counts all first votes and works bottom-up loses a fast-final child, and so does a bottom-up recipe on settled votes with a forking owner; the recipe of Section~\ref{sec:close} passes the safety, side-change, and recovery checks by simulation, and safety in every run of up to 17 steps, two more than the bottom-up order needs to fail. Probes show that the invariants are not vacuous.

The models are small: one or two positions, two to four epochs, no receives, restarts in one model only, $N\in\{4,6\}$. Their abstractions only add behaviours, except the recover model's owner and Byzantine policies, so a violation in the model is a violation of the protocol, but a clean run is evidence, not proof.
