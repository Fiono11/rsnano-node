\section{Account-Local Processing}
\label{sec:account}
\subsection{Blocks, operations, and validation}
A block is $B=(a,h_p,x,\sigma_a)$: account, parent hash, ordered operation batch, and owner signature, with identifier $h(B)=H(\mathsf{Block},a,h_p,x)$. The epoch is absent from the identifier, so that a nonfinal omitted block can be retried with new-epoch votes; an empty batch is permitted for owner-authorized branch continuation. The operations are $\Send(b,z)$, for destination $b$ and positive amount $z$, and $\Receive(s)$, where $s=(h(B),j)$ identifies a send at operation index $j$. A send debits its owner; a receive credits only the named destination and requires an explicit proof that the send is already final.

Validation replays the entire unresolved selected path from its finalized anchor, then the proposed batch, checking signatures, consecutive parent slots, amounts, balances, and duplicate receives; it has no application effect, and every finalization repeats the checks and applies only unapplied operations. A finality proof is an account-certificate proof with the evidence needed to verify eligibility, or a verified decided checkpoint that promoted the block. A proof used for epoch-$e$ voting cannot depend on $S_e$ or a later checkpoint, and a receive's send proof must be available before a correct validator supports the receiving path, which makes replay dependencies well founded (Lemma~\ref{lem:dag}).

\subsection{Single-support voting and durable state}
\label{sec:voting}
An account instance is $(\mathit{session},e,a,v)$. A correct validator first-votes at most once in that instance, for the first admissible available block it accepts; there is no second-look support for a rival and no account timeout vote.

\begin{definition}[Support, notarization certificate, exclusion witness]
\label{def:support}
A member of $K_e$ \emph{supports} a block $B$ in an instance if it signed an epoch-$e$ first vote for $B$ or an epoch-$e$ late notarization for $B$; a correct validator supports at most one block per instance. A \emph{notarization certificate} $\NC_e(B)$ is a set of $q$ epoch-$e$ \emph{first votes} for $B$ from distinct members. An \emph{exclusion witness} $\XW_e(B)$ is a set of $q$ epoch-$e$ supports for $B$ from distinct members, first votes or late notarizations in any mix. Every $\NC_e(B)$ is an $\XW_e(B)$; the converse fails.
\end{definition}

An $\NC$ of first votes is what makes a block complete, what a validator records before final-voting, what an $\tagN$ report entry claims, and what Rules~1 and~3 of Section~\ref{sec:build} consume; an $\XW$ serves only as the closing-epoch evidence of the overlap exception, as the discharge of a recovery record of origin $e$ on a conflicting block, and inside the overlap certificate behind an $\tagF$ claim. Letting late notarizations count toward an $\NC$ makes correct reports unusable (Section~\ref{sec:mc}).

A block is \emph{complete} in epoch $e$ when its data and required ancestry are available and the validator has assembled its $\NC_e$. A complete eligible block is durably recorded as notarized before the validator final-votes, uses it as a complete parent, or freezes its report. The validator can final-vote only for its own first-voted block, and atomically records a terminal flag with that signature.

\para{Certificates and the early-vote rule.}
Every first vote names its \emph{base}, the last checkpoint its signer had installed when signing; the admission-witness digest binds it. An epoch-$e$ first vote is \emph{settled} if its base is $S_{e-1}$ and \emph{early} otherwise. An eligible block finalizes on an $\FC_e(B)$ of $q$ final votes or an $\FF_e(B)$ of $N-p$ \emph{settled} first votes; early first votes count toward $\NC_e(B)$ and $\XW_e(B)$ and toward nothing else. A validator that has not installed $S_{e-1}$ releases an epoch-$e$ final vote for $B$ only while it holds, for $B$ and every unresolved block of its selected prefix, the closing-epoch exclusion witness and the current-epoch $\NC$ that the overlap exception requires, and it retains them with its notarization record; after installing $S_{e-1}$ it final-votes under ordinary rechecking. A block first-voted during a close therefore finalizes in two rounds rather than one. Without the rule, a fast certificate can be formed from early votes whose eligibility rests on a witness their signers never held, and the next closure cannot see it (Section~\ref{sec:mc}).

A finalized block's selected unresolved ancestors finalize with it, and incompatible provisional branches leave the effective live view; an $\NC$ alone has no balance effect.

\para{Cross-epoch voting record.}
A correct identity stops epoch-$(e-1)$ first and final voting before opening epoch $e$. While $S_{e-1}$ is unknown, it does not first-vote for a different block at a position where it supported a block in epoch $e-1$; re-voting the same block is allowed. Once the predecessor is installed, its finalized history, frontier, and carried lock records govern the position.

\para{Late notarization.}
A member of $K_{e-1}$ keeps notarizing for its closing epoch until it installs $S_{e-1}$. When it first-votes $B$ at $(a,v)$ in epoch $e$ while $S_{e-1}$ is unknown, it also signs a notarization-only epoch-$(e-1)$ vote for $B$, unless it supported a different block at $(a,v)$ in epoch $e-1$; if it first-voted $B$ itself there, that vote already supports $B$. The late vote is durably recorded before release. It counts toward an epoch-$(e-1)$ exclusion witness and toward nothing else: no $\NC$, $\FF$, or $\FC$ counts it, no final vote and no $\tagN$ entry follows from it, and no report contains it, since reports are frozen before it is cast. Late notarizations are retained evidence that a manifest may carry, but only inside an exclusion witness. They give a block published after the boundary the closing-epoch exclusion witness that the overlap exception requires.

\subsection{Attachment and prefix-wide eligibility}
\label{sec:eligibility}
Let $d_a(S)$ be the maximum depth of any block for account $a$ retained in checkpoint $S$. With $S_{e-1}$ installed, a \emph{new} epoch-$e$ root starts at depth $d_a(S_{e-1})+1$ and extends a maximum-depth retained tip; a retained tip continues at its own depth (Section~\ref{sec:install}). Subsequent children require complete epoch-$e$ parents, and an extension must respect finalized history and every carried lock record on its selected ancestry. Before $S_{e-1}$ is known, first voting may prepare work from a maximum-depth tip of the latest closed ledger, from a closing-epoch finalized parent with its proof, or from a complete current-epoch parent. The signed admission-witness digest fixes the anchor and old evidence actually used.

Ordinary early work is provisional: when the predecessor arrives, it is rechecked against the decided frontier, finalized history, and carried lock records, and a tally of fast-final size does not bypass this check. A notarization lock constrains settlement to its branch. A recovery record of origin $o$ on a block $B'$ is \emph{discharged} by a verified exclusion witness $\XW_o(B)$ for a block $B$ that conflicts with $B'$: account exclusion (Lemma~\ref{lem:exclusion}) then proves that no epoch-$o$ final certificate for $B'$ can exist. The witness is retained. Evidence of any other epoch does not discharge the record, and a block carrying several records is released only when each has been discharged by a witness of its own origin.

\para{The core overlap exception.}
A block may finalize before $S_{e-1}$ is known only if the verifier checks its entire selected prefix against the latest closed state and proves cross-boundary exclusion at \emph{every unresolved position being finalized}: each such block, including the certificate target, must have a closing-epoch exclusion witness $\XW_{e-1}$ and a current-epoch $\NC_e$ for the same hash. A closing-epoch finalized prefix is already a safe anchor. Any inherited recovery record bypassed by this path requires the matching-origin discharge witness above; inherited notarization locks cannot be bypassed. We call the bundle of these witnesses, the final certificate, and the discharge witnesses for the bypassed records an \emph{overlap certificate} $\OC$. Its validity is a function of its contents and of the last closed state alone (Lemma~\ref{lem:verdict}), so a validator that learns it before installing $S_{e-1}$ and one that learns it after reach the same verdict.

A fresh descendant without its own closing-epoch exclusion witness remains provisional, even if its parent is notarized in both epochs; otherwise disjoint committees could certify different children of that parent. If the predecessor arrives first, nonfinal early work falls back to ordinary rechecking; already finalized overlap work remains in the certified live overlay.

\para{Fresh-child recovery.}
If a checkpoint retains unresolved tips, the owner can extend a permissible tip with a fresh child; finalizing that child selects its unresolved ancestors rather than reopening their slots, and an undischarged lock record still constrains the branch choice. Persistent submission therefore means retrying an omitted block or supplying a fresh, possibly empty, uncontested child of a retained admissible tip.
