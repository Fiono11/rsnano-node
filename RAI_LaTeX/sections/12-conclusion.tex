\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
RAI's checkpoints grow with the unresolved forks they keep, and locks, late supports, and overlap witnesses must be kept while a lock they can release survives; we give no garbage-collection policy beyond the prototype's four-epoch cap. The committee model is authenticated and equal-weight with an externally kept fault bound. The evaluation runs on one host, so the delays it measures are dominated by CPU contention even with emulated link delay, and process kills test crash consistency, not power loss. The models cover at most two positions and three epochs.

Within these limits RAI does what it set out to do. It keeps account-local payments safe across committee changes without stopping: the next epoch votes while the previous one closes, immutable reports preserve whatever the old epoch could still confirm, and four overlap rules keep the two epochs from contradicting each other. It keeps at most one side of every contested position, identically everywhere, and lets an honest owner recover the account by building on that side. And it does so at a cost in latency tails rather than in throughput, where a stop-and-drain transition stalls confirmations at every boundary.
