\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
RAI's checkpoints grow with the unresolved forks they keep, and the garbage-collection argument of Section~\ref{sec:model} covers only the prototype's four-epoch window. The committee model is authenticated and equal-weight with an externally kept fault bound. A close waits for every honest report, so with forks and a Byzantine validator it can outlast an epoch. Forks cannot endanger other accounts' payments, but they compete with them for votes, processing, and closes; RAI does not isolate resources, and with twelve validators and 10\% forks it confirms less than a pause. Each change we ran replaced one or two seats; each cell ran three times; and process kills test crash consistency, not power loss. The models cover at most two positions and four epochs and check the recipe only by simulation and to 17 steps.

Within these limits RAI changes the validators of account-local payments without a global barrier: the next epoch votes while the previous one closes, the checkpoint keeps whatever the old epoch could still confirm; overlap payments wait only if too few members stay. It keeps one side of every contested position and finalizes it within two epochs of normal operation, so the owner gets the account back. With six validators across six and seven regions it confirms through every boundary with a 95th percentile 6.7 to 38 times lower than when it pauses, at 8\,s epochs, and finalizes the forks Nano leaves open.
