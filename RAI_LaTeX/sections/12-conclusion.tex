\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
RAI's checkpoints grow with the unresolved forks they keep, and the garbage-collection argument of Section~\ref{sec:model} covers only the prototype's four-epoch window. The committee model is authenticated and equal-weight with an externally kept fault bound. A close waits for every honest report, so with forks and a Byzantine validator it can outlast an epoch; the restarts were measured on one host only; each cell ran three times; and process kills test crash consistency, not power loss. The models cover at most two positions and four epochs and check the recipe only by simulation and to 17 steps.

Within these limits RAI changes the validators of account-local payments without stopping: the next epoch votes while the previous one closes, and the checkpoint keeps whatever the old epoch could still confirm. It keeps one side of every contested position and finalizes it within two epochs of normal operation, so the owner gets the account back. Across six and seven regions it keeps confirming through every boundary, with a 95th percentile 6.7 to 38 times lower than a stop-and-drain handover, and it finalizes the forks Nano leaves open. Because every validator agrees on the epoch boundaries, a protocol change can also take effect at an agreed epoch.
